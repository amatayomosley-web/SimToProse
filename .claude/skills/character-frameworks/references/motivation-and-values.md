# Motivation, Needs and Values

What *pulls* a person — the standing "worth" a character weighs before every choice. This is the **Level 2 / Layer 2** of the repo's scheme (`docs/character-anatomy.md`, `docs/values-and-stakes.md`): not *how* a person behaves (that is the trait layer, `trait-models.md`) but *what is worth more to them* — the menu of needs, values, and morals against which a scene's concrete stakes are priced. The repo's spine treats **the "model" of a person as an individualized weighting over a shared menu** (`docs/generative-model.md`, `docs/values-and-stakes.md`) — Reiss's exact finding: motivation is not a different *set* of desires per person but a different *profile of strengths* over the same desires everyone has. This file is the well of those menus: the needs hierarchies (what a body and a psyche require), the value taxonomies (what a person orients toward), the moral taxonomies (what a person holds sacred and is outraged by), and the acquired-motive and craft frameworks that carve the same territory at different angles. The repo commits to a specific stack — **Schwartz's values as the value spine, Moral Foundations as the ethics lens, Self-Determination Theory plus survival as the needs substrate, and concrete stakes as the in-scene layer** (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`), with drives (`docs/drives-schema.md`) as the per-person operands that *instantiate* the menu. Those are the reference defaults; they sit inside a much larger library, and this file lays the library out.

The single most important idea in this file is not any one taxonomy — it is that **a menu with built-in oppositions is the engine of character**. A value system that only *adds up* produces a saint or a want-machine, not a person. Every framework below is chosen partly for the **conflict structure** it exposes: Schwartz's circumplex has opposing poles by construction; Maslow's floor pits survival against everything above it; SDT's autonomy fights relatedness; Moral Foundations' care fights loyalty. **Two good people who weight the same menu differently produce principled, tragic collision** — the drama the repo's decision engine resolves. A closing section returns to this as the master key. Read the frameworks as a set of *tension maps*, not lists of nice things.

A note on double-counting. The taxonomies overlap heavily — benevolence ≈ care ≈ relatedness ≈ affiliation, four names for one region seen from four disciplines. This is not a bug; it is triangulation, and each angle names a texture the others miss. But when *authoring* a character you do not track all of them. Per the repo's discipline (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`): **Schwartz = the value spine, Moral Foundations = the ethics lens that adds loyalty/authority/sanctity, SDT + survival = the needs substrate, concrete stakes = the in-scene layer** — track the spine plus concrete for everyone, add moral/needs granularity only for principals. The other frameworks here are lenses you reach for when a scene needs *that* specific carving.

## Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs — the deficiency-to-growth pyramid

**Lineage.** Abraham **Maslow**, "A Theory of Human Motivation" (*Psychological Review*, 1943) and *Motivation and Personality* (1954), the humanistic-psychology tradition (with Carl Rogers). Originally five levels; Maslow later inserted **cognitive** and **aesthetic** needs (1960s, *Toward a Psychology of Being*) and, near the end of his life, crowned it with **self-transcendence** (1969, "the farther reaches of human nature"). The most famous psychological model in the world and the most misremembered — Maslow never drew the pyramid; a management textbook did.

**Core idea.** Human needs fall into a **prepotent hierarchy**: lower needs, when unmet, dominate attention and behaviour and must be *substantially* (never fully) satisfied before higher needs become motivating. The lower four are **deficiency needs (D-needs)** — driven by *lack*, quieted by satisfaction. The top tier(s) are **being / growth needs (B-needs)** — never sated, they *grow* with feeding (the more you self-actualise, the more you want to). A starving person is not moved by beauty; a safe, fed, loved person is moved by little else *but* meaning.

**Structure in full** (bottom to top):

1. **Physiological** — air, water, food, warmth, sleep, sex, homeostasis. The absolute floor; overrides everything until met.
2. **Safety** — physical security, health, shelter, employment, property, freedom from fear, a predictable ordered world. (Maslow noted a neurotic adult can be *stuck* seeking safety — the person who cannot stop making the world controllable.)
3. **Love / Belonging** — intimacy, friendship, family, sexual intimacy, group membership, being *accepted*. Deficiency here (loneliness, ostracism) is a powerful driver.
4. **Esteem** — split in two: *lower* esteem = respect *from others* (status, recognition, fame, attention); *higher* esteem = respect *for oneself* (competence, mastery, independence, dignity). Maslow held the higher form more durable.
5. **Cognitive** *(later addition)* — knowledge, understanding, curiosity, meaning-making, exploration.
6. **Aesthetic** *(later addition)* — beauty, balance, form, order for its own sake.
7. **Self-Actualisation** — realising one's full potential, becoming *everything one is capable of becoming*, the pursuit of peak experiences and creative fulfilment ("what a man *can* be, he *must* be"). A B-need — unquenchable.
8. **Self-Transcendence** *(final addition)* — going *beyond* the self: serving a cause, devotion to an ideal, mystical or peak experience, the welfare of others, connection to something larger. Maslow came to see this, not self-actualisation, as the true summit.

**Vivid exemplars.** The whole arc read *downward* is a survival story: a shipwreck strips a character from self-actualisation back to physiological in an afternoon (the point of nearly every disaster narrative). Jean Valjean begins at *physiological/safety* (stealing bread, hunted) and climbs across the novel to *self-transcendence* (living for Cosette and for mercy itself). Ebenezer Scrooge is stuck at *safety* — hoarding against a poverty that no longer threatens him — and the ghosts drag him up to *belonging* and *transcendence*. A self-actualiser: the artist who paints though it never sells, the scientist who *must* know. A self-transcender: the monk, the whistle-blower who burns their life for a truth, Sydney Carton's "far, far better thing." The tragedy engine: a character forced to *choose which level to sacrifice* — the parent who gives up their food (physiological) so the child eats, the dissident who trades safety for meaning.

**What it gives you for character depth.** The **prepotency dynamic** is the gift, not the list: a character's *centre of gravity* (which level currently owns them) tells you what they will notice, fear, and reach for — and a scene that *drops* them a level (threat, loss, hunger) predicts the regression. It is the cleanest tool for writing *stakes escalation*: threaten a higher need and the character resists; threaten a lower one and they *break form*, because the floor outranks the principle — until, in the character-defining moment, it doesn't. That inversion — a person who sacrifices the floor for a higher stake (starves for a cause, dies for a person) — is the repo's dramatic gold (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`, "the floor... usually outweighs the rest *until met*, but people sacrifice it for higher stakes").

**Combines-with.** The repo uses **Maslow's floor (physiological + safety) as the survival substrate** paired with **SDT's three psychological needs** in place of Maslow's love/esteem/actualisation (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`) — SDT is the better-validated carving of the upper pyramid. Maslow's *esteem* ≈ McClelland's *achievement* + Schwartz *achievement/power*; *belonging* ≈ SDT *relatedness* ≈ McClelland *affiliation* ≈ Schwartz *benevolence*; *self-actualisation/cognitive* ≈ SDT *competence/autonomy* + Schwartz *self-direction/stimulation*; *self-transcendence* ≈ Schwartz *universalism* + Moral Foundations' binding cluster. The prepotency = the repo's claim that survival threats (tier 1 harm) usually dominate the weighting (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`, `severity(e)`). In the Panksepp basis (`docs/generative-model.md`), the physiological/safety floor is **FEAR + SEEKING** for resources; belonging is **CARE + PANIC/GRIEF**.

**Cautions.** (1) **The strict hierarchy is not empirically supported.** Wahba & Bridwell (1976) found little evidence for prepotency or for five discrete levels; Tay & Diener (2011, Gallup World Poll, 123 countries) found the needs are *universal* but people pursue them *simultaneously* and out of order — a starving artist creates, a wealthy person is lonely. **Use it as a menu of need-categories and a stakes-escalation intuition, not as a literal ladder.** (2) It is culturally individualist — collectivist cultures may place belonging *below* or *fused with* survival. (3) The pyramid is a myth-object: cite the *needs*, not the "five-step staircase." (4) Self-actualisation is nearly circular ("actualised people are the ones who self-actualise") and Maslow's exemplar sample was hand-picked. Its poetry outruns its psychometrics.

**Sources.** Maslow, "A Theory of Human Motivation" (*Psychological Review*, 1943); Maslow, *Motivation and Personality* (1954/1970); Wahba & Bridwell, "Maslow reconsidered" (*Organizational Behavior and Human Performance*, 1976); Tay & Diener, "Needs and subjective well-being around the world" (*JPSP*, 2011); Kaufman, *Transcend: The New Science of Self-Actualization* (2020).

## Alderfer's ERG — Maslow compressed, with regression

**Lineage.** Clayton **Alderfer**, "An empirical test of a new theory of human needs" (*Organizational Behavior and Human Performance*, 1969), Yale. A direct, empirically-motivated revision of Maslow.

**Core idea.** Collapse Maslow's five into **three** categories and drop the strict staircase. Needs operate **concurrently**, in any order, and — the distinctive contribution — a **frustration-regression** principle: when a higher need is blocked, the person *intensifies* a lower one to compensate. Thwarted at growth, they double down on relationships or on material security.

**Structure in full.** Three needs (mnemonic **ERG**):

- **Existence** — material and physiological: survival, safety, sustenance, pay, working conditions (≈ Maslow physiological + safety).
- **Relatedness** — social and interpersonal: belonging, recognition, meaningful relations with others (≈ Maslow belonging + external esteem).
- **Growth** — intrinsic development: self-esteem, mastery, self-actualisation, creative and personal fulfilment (≈ Maslow internal esteem + self-actualisation).

Two principles govern them: **satisfaction-progression** (met needs free attention for higher ones, as in Maslow) *and* **frustration-regression** (blocked higher needs make lower ones more salient — the man stalled in his vocation who pours it all into food, or status, or a controlling love).

**Vivid exemplars.** Frustration-regression is a whole character mechanism: the artist blocked at *Growth* who becomes obsessed with money and recognition (*Relatedness/Existence*) — Salieri in *Amadeus*, unable to reach genius, feeding on court status instead. The lonely person (blocked *Relatedness*) who over-invests in wealth or work (*Existence/Growth*) — Charles Foster Kane, buying belonging he cannot earn. The prisoner denied *Growth* and *Relatedness* who ritualises *Existence* (the exact routine of the cell) to stay sane.

**What it gives you for character depth.** **Frustration-regression is the gift** — it names *why a character over-pursues a lower need*, giving a mechanism for compensation, addiction-substitution, and displaced hunger that Maslow's clean staircase can't. It is the tidier, better-validated cousin: three dials, concurrent, with a compensation rule — cheap to author and immediately dramatic (block the thing they really want; watch where the pressure vents).

**Combines-with.** ERG is Maslow rebuilt to match the repo's own move — the repo already fuses Maslow's floor with SDT's psychological needs, and ERG's Existence/Relatedness/Growth ≈ *survival / relatedness / (competence+autonomy)*. Frustration-regression is a drives-schema mechanism (`docs/drives-schema.md`): a blocked terminal goal raises the salience (and heat) of a lower substitute — model it as a state-layer reweighting under blockage. Growth ↔ Schwartz self-direction/stimulation; Relatedness ↔ benevolence/affiliation.

**Cautions.** (1) Still a *needs-category* model, not a mechanism — it says *what* is wanted, not how it resolves against a competing want. (2) Frustration-regression is intuitive but only modestly evidenced; use it as a characterisation heuristic, not a law. (3) Three categories under-carve — Existence lumps survival with pay, Growth lumps mastery with meaning; reach past ERG when you need the finer Schwartz/SDT grain.

**Sources.** Alderfer, "An empirical test of a new theory of human needs" (*OBHP*, 1969); Alderfer, *Existence, Relatedness, and Growth* (1972).

## Self-Determination Theory — autonomy, competence, relatedness (repo needs default)

**Lineage.** Edward **Deci** & Richard **Ryan**, University of Rochester, from the 1970s (Deci's intrinsic-motivation experiments) through the canonical statements: *Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior* (1985), the *Psychological Inquiry* target article (2000), and *Self-Determination Theory* (2017). The dominant contemporary theory of human motivation in psychology, education, and work; a macro-theory built of six mini-theories. **This is the repo's psychological-needs substrate** (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`).

**Core idea.** Humans have **three innate, universal psychological needs** whose satisfaction is *necessary* for intrinsic motivation, healthy development, and well-being — and whose thwarting produces defensiveness, ill-being, and controlled (non-self-endorsed) behaviour. Crucially, SDT is not only about *how much* motivation but *what kind*: motivation runs on a **quality continuum** from controlled to autonomous, and the same behaviour (studying, working, obeying) can be experienced as coerced or as one's own.

**Structure in full.**

*The three basic psychological needs:*
- **Autonomy** — the need to be the *author* of one's own actions, to act from genuine self-endorsement and volition rather than coercion. (Not independence — an autonomous person can *choose* to depend or obey; the point is that the choice is *theirs*.)
- **Competence** — the need to feel effective, to master challenges, to grow capacities, to produce desired outcomes. Fed by optimal challenge and clear feedback.
- **Relatedness** — the need to feel connected, to care and be cared for, to belong, to matter to others.

*The Organismic Integration continuum (OIT) — the quality of motivation, least to most self-determined:*
- **Amotivation** — no intention, no valuing ("why bother").
- **External regulation** — acting for reward or to avoid punishment ("I do it or I'm fired").
- **Introjected regulation** — acting from internal pressure: guilt, shame, ego, contingent self-worth ("I'd feel terrible if I didn't"). Internal, but *not* self-endorsed — the harsh internalised *should*.
- **Identified regulation** — consciously valuing the goal as personally important ("this matters to me").
- **Integrated regulation** — the value is fully assimilated into the self, coherent with other values ("this is who I am").
- **Intrinsic motivation** — doing it for the inherent satisfaction of the activity itself ("I love this").

*The other mini-theories (in brief):* **Cognitive Evaluation Theory** — how external rewards, deadlines, and surveillance *undermine* intrinsic motivation (the **overjustification effect**: pay a child to draw and they stop drawing for free), while choice and positive feedback *enhance* it. **Causality Orientations Theory** — dispositional styles: *autonomy-oriented*, *controlled-oriented*, *impersonal* (helpless). **Goal Contents Theory** — *intrinsic aspirations* (growth, relationships, community) support well-being; *extrinsic aspirations* (wealth, fame, image) undermine it even when achieved.

**Vivid exemplars.** The autonomy need drives every *rebellion-against-control* arc — Katniss forced into the Games (autonomy annihilated) whose entire trajectory is reclaiming authorship; the child of a domineering parent choosing a "wrong" life just to make it *theirs*. Competence: the apprentice's hunger to be *good* at the thing, the Karate Kid, the montage. Relatedness: every found-family story. The OIT continuum is a character *X-ray*: two soldiers who both fight — one under *external regulation* (conscript, will desert at the first chance) and one under *integrated regulation* (this is who I am) — behave identically until the pressure lifts, then diverge completely. **Introjected regulation is the richest** for interiority: the character who works themselves to collapse not because they value the work but because *stopping* triggers shame — the internalised critical parent running the show, self-image ≠ true want (the repo's self-deception engine, `docs/drives-schema.md` `view: self`).

**What it gives you for character depth.** SDT's distinct contribution is the **quality-of-motivation continuum** — it lets you write *the same act with a different soul*. A character can obey, achieve, or love from coercion, from guilt, from conviction, or from joy, and the OIT stage predicts how they hold up under stress, whether they resent it, and what happens when the external prop is removed. It also gives the cleanest theory of **why extrinsic success rings hollow** (Goal Contents Theory) — the arc of the person who got the money/fame and is emptier, because extrinsic aspirations don't feed the three needs. And **autonomy-vs-relatedness is a first-class value conflict** (see the conflict section): the need to be one's own author fighting the need to belong is the engine of every leaving-home, every doomed-love-vs-duty story.

**Combines-with.** The repo's needs substrate is **survival (Maslow floor) + SDT's three** (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`). SDT autonomy ↔ Schwartz *self-direction*; competence ↔ *achievement*; relatedness ↔ *benevolence* and McClelland *affiliation*. The OIT continuum maps onto the drives schema's terminal/instrumental chain (`docs/drives-schema.md`) — an *integrated* goal is a terminal goal that serves self-image; an *introjected* one is a goal driven by a fear (shame) rather than a value. Overjustification and controlled motivation feed the decision engine's read of *why* a character acts. In Panksepp terms, competence and autonomy ride **SEEKING**; relatedness rides **CARE + PANIC/GRIEF**.

**Cautions.** (1) The three needs are proposed as *universal*, but the *expression and weighting* of autonomy in particular is debated cross-culturally (collectivist critique — though SDT replies that autonomy ≠ individualism, and choosing to fulfil a family obligation *can* be autonomous). Weight autonomy by culture (`docs/world-model.md` formative coupling), don't zero it. (2) Don't confuse **autonomy with independence** — the single most common misreading; a devoted, obedient, deeply connected character can be highly autonomous if the devotion is self-endorsed. (3) SDT is a needs-and-quality theory, not a full value taxonomy — pair it with Schwartz for the *content* of what a person values. (4) The overjustification effect is real but bounded (it undermines *already-intrinsic* motivation; it doesn't destroy motivation for dull tasks) — don't over-apply.

**Sources.** Deci & Ryan, *Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior* (1985); Ryan & Deci, "Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being" (*American Psychologist*, 2000); Ryan & Deci, *Self-Determination Theory: Basic Psychological Needs in Motivation, Development, and Wellness* (2017); Deci, "Effects of externally mediated rewards on intrinsic motivation" (*JPSP*, 1971, the overjustification origin).

## McClelland's Three Needs — achievement, affiliation, power

**Lineage.** David **McClelland** (Harvard), *The Achieving Society* (1961) and *Human Motivation* (1987), building on Henry Murray's need taxonomy and John Atkinson's achievement-motivation experiments. Also called the **Acquired-Needs** or **Learned-Needs** theory: the three motives are *shaped by culture and experience*, not innate, and one usually **dominates** in each person. Measured by the projective **Thematic Apperception Test (TAT)** — subjects invent stories about ambiguous pictures and *project* their dominant motive.

**Core idea.** Adult social motivation is largely governed by three learned drives whose relative strengths form a person's motivational signature. What differs between people is *which* dominates — and each dominant motive predicts a distinct pattern of goals, satisfactions, stresses, and characteristic failures.

**Structure in full.**

- **Need for Achievement (nAch)** — the drive to accomplish difficult things, meet a *standard of excellence*, master, out-perform *one's own* prior best, and get concrete feedback. High-nAch people prefer **moderate, calculated risk** (too easy = no pride, too hard = no control), personal responsibility for outcomes, and unambiguous feedback. They often make poor managers (they want to do it themselves) but excellent individual contributors and entrepreneurs.
- **Need for Affiliation (nAff)** — the drive to be *liked and accepted*, to belong, to maintain warm harmonious relationships, to avoid conflict and rejection. High-nAff people conform to group norms, seek approval, and are made anxious by competition or by having to give unpopular orders.
- **Need for Power (nPow)** — the drive to *influence, control, and have impact* on others. McClelland's key subdivision: **personalized power** (dominating for personal gain, prestige, and the raw thrill of controlling others — "I win, you lose") vs **socialized power** (organising and empowering others toward a shared goal, disciplined by concern for the group). The best leaders show the **Leadership Motive Pattern**: high socialized nPow, *lower* nAff (willing to be disliked), and high self-control (activity inhibition).

**Vivid exemplars.** *nAch:* the driven athlete or founder chasing the personal best — Andrew in *Whiplash*, Gatsby's self-made striving, any "I will be the greatest" arc; note the high-nAch failure mode is the person who cannot delegate and burns out or alienates. *nAff:* the peacemaker who cannot bear conflict — the people-pleasing friend, the manager who won't fire anyone, Chidi in *The Good Place* (agonised by anyone's disapproval). *Personalized nPow:* the tyrant who rules for the pleasure of ruling — Commodus, most mob bosses, the schoolyard bully grown up. *Socialized nPow:* the servant-leader who builds others — a good general, Aragorn, the coach who makes the team. The clash of *personalized vs socialized* power is a whole political-drama engine: two ambitious people, one who wants the throne to *serve*, one who wants it to *own*.

**What it gives you for character depth.** A compact, potent **three-dial motivational signature** that predicts *what a character finds satisfying and what stresses them* — and the personalized/socialized power split is one of the sharpest single distinctions in all of characterisation, cleanly separating the tyrant from the statesman who look alike from outside. nAch gives you the texture of *striving* (the moderate-risk preference, the hunger for feedback — very writable); nAff gives the conflict-avoidant, approval-hungry interior; nPow gives ambition with a *moral valence dial*. Because the motives are *learned*, they hook directly to backstory (`docs/generative-model.md` history): a high-nAch character was shaped by a demanding parent or an early triumph; the socialized/personalized split traces to whether power ever *protected* or only *hurt* them.

**Combines-with.** nAff ≈ SDT relatedness ≈ Schwartz benevolence ≈ Reiss social contact/family; nAch ≈ SDT competence ≈ Schwartz achievement ≈ Reiss status; nPow ≈ Schwartz power (personalized = power-dominance/resources; socialized straddles power and benevolence) ≈ Reiss power. In the drives schema (`docs/drives-schema.md`) these are *terminal goal flavours* that many concrete goals serve. Personalized nPow correlates with **low Honesty-Humility** and the Dark-Triad region (`trait-models.md`); socialized nPow with high H + high socialized concern. In Panksepp terms nPow rides **SEEKING + RAGE/dominance**, nAff rides **CARE + PANIC/GRIEF**, nAch rides **SEEKING**.

**Cautions.** (1) The **TAT is psychometrically weak** — low reliability, hard to score; McClelland argued it taps *implicit* motives that self-report questionnaires (measuring *explicit*, self-attributed motives) miss, and the two can *diverge* (a person who *says* they value achievement but whose implicit motive is affiliation). That implicit/explicit split is itself great character material (the self-image ≠ true-drive gap) but makes the theory hard to measure. (2) Three motives *under-carve* — no room for curiosity, order, safety, meaning; pair with Reiss/Schwartz for breadth. (3) "Learned" is overstated; the motives have heritable components. (4) Don't flatten *power* into villainy — socialized power is a virtue; the dial is the point.

**Sources.** McClelland, *The Achieving Society* (1961); McClelland, *Human Motivation* (1987); McClelland & Burnham, "Power is the great motivator" (*Harvard Business Review*, 1976, the Leadership Motive Pattern); Atkinson, *An Introduction to Motivation* (1964).

## Murray's Psychogenic Needs — the ancestral taxonomy

**Lineage.** Henry **Murray**, *Explorations in Personality* (1938), Harvard Psychological Clinic; co-developer (with Christiana Morgan) of the TAT. The taproot from which both McClelland's three needs and Reiss's sixteen desires descend.

**Core idea.** Personality is organised around a set of roughly **20+ psychogenic (psychological) needs** — recurring motivational forces — that interact with **press** (the motivationally relevant features of the environment; *alpha press* = objective, *beta press* = as perceived). Behaviour = need × press; a **thema** is a recurring need–press pattern across a life. Needs vary in strength between people and can *fuse*, *conflict*, or *subsidiate* (one serve another).

**Structure in full** (the needs, grouped — the useful ones for characterisation):

- **Ambition:** *Achievement* (accomplish, master), *Exhibition* (be seen, shock, thrill others), *Recognition* (status, prestige).
- **Power / status:** *Dominance* (control, influence), *Deference* (admire, follow a superior), *Autonomy* (resist coercion, be independent), *Aggression* (overcome, punish, avenge), *Abasement* (submit, accept blame, seek pain/punishment).
- **Affection:** *Affiliation* (form friendships, belong), *Nurturance* (care for the helpless), *Succorance* (be nurtured, seek help and protection), *Rejection* (exclude, snub), *Play*.
- **Information / control:** *Cognizance* (curiosity, explore, ask), *Exposition* (teach, explain), *Order* (arrange, organise), *Understanding*.
- **Defensive:** *Infavoidance* (avoid failure and humiliation), *Defendance* (justify oneself), *Counteraction* (overcome weakness, retaliate), *Harmavoidance* (avoid pain and danger).

**Vivid exemplars.** Murray's value now is as a **fine-grained need vocabulary** for textures the big theories skip: *Succorance* (the adult who is forever seeking someone to take care of them — Blanche DuBois), *Abasement* (the character drawn to their own punishment — Dostoevsky's guilt-seekers), *Infavoidance* (the one whose every choice is organised around *never being humiliated* — a whole avoidant life), *Exhibition* (the compulsive performer). The need × press = the repo's *baseline drive × situation* exactly.

**What it gives you for character depth.** A **superset menu** — when Schwartz/SDT/McClelland don't name the specific hunger you need, Murray probably does (succorance, abasement, infavoidance, counteraction, deference). And **beta press** — the *perceived* environment, not the real one — is precisely the repo's three-views / perception layer (`docs/self-and-perception.md`): a character acts on the press they *read*, often wrongly.

**Combines-with.** Direct ancestor of McClelland (achievement/affiliation/dominance→power) and Reiss (which re-derived a similar list empirically). Need × press ≈ drive × situation (`docs/drives-schema.md`, `docs/decision-engine.md`). Harmavoidance ≈ Panksepp FEAR; Nurturance ≈ CARE; Aggression ≈ RAGE. Reach for a single Murray need as a *texture*, not the whole list.

**Cautions.** (1) The list is **unfactored and overlapping** — never validated as a dimensional structure; it is a *vocabulary*, not a measurement model. (2) Tied to the shaky TAT. (3) Use it to *name* a specific hunger, not to author 20 dials.

**Sources.** Murray, *Explorations in Personality* (1938); Murray & Morgan, "A method for investigating fantasies: the Thematic Apperception Test" (*Archives of Neurology and Psychiatry*, 1935).

## Reiss's 16 Basic Desires — the empirical motivation profile

**Lineage.** Steven **Reiss** (Ohio State), *Who Am I? The 16 Basic Desires That Motivate Our Actions and Define Our Personalities* (2000) and "Multifaceted nature of intrinsic motivation" (*Review of General Psychology*, 2004). Derived *bottom-up* by factor-analysing what >6,000 people across four continents said motivates them — the most empirically ambitious desire-based motivation model. Commercialised as the **Reiss Motivation Profile (RMP)**.

**Core idea.** Human motivation reduces to **16 basic desires** — *intrinsic* ends (wanted for their own sake, not as means to something else), each with a **genetic and evolutionary root**, each experienced by everyone but at a characteristic **set-point / satiation intensity**. What makes a person unique is their **profile** — the strength-ranking across all sixteen. Critically, each desire has a **satiation point**: acting on it produces a specific *intrinsic feeling* that fades and must be renewed, so a strong desire is *never permanently satisfied* — it recurs. Reiss explicitly rejected Maslow's hierarchy (no ordering) and the reducibility of the 16 to a few super-motives.

**Structure in full** (the sixteen, each an intrinsic end, with the feeling it seeks):

- **Power** — influence, leadership, mastery, will imposed on the world (feeling sought: *efficacy*).
- **Independence** — self-reliance, autonomy, freedom from others (*freedom*).
- **Curiosity** — knowledge, understanding, truth, thinking (*wonder*).
- **Acceptance** — positive self-regard, avoidance of criticism and rejection (*self-confidence*).
- **Order** — structure, cleanliness, stability, ritual, predictability (*stability*).
- **Saving** — collecting, frugality, keeping and accumulating things (*ownership*).
- **Honor** — loyalty to parents, heritage, moral code, group; character and duty (*loyalty / integrity*).
- **Idealism** — social justice, fairness, improving society, altruism toward strangers (*compassion*).
- **Social Contact** — friendship, companionship, fun, being among peers (*belonging / fun*).
- **Family** — raising and being with one's own children and kin (*love*).
- **Status** — prestige, social standing, wealth as rank, attention, reputation (*self-importance*).
- **Vengeance** — competition, winning, getting even, retaliation (*vindication*).
- **Romance** — sex, beauty, courtship, aesthetic and erotic experience (*lust / rapture*).
- **Eating** — food, the pleasure of consumption (*satiation*).
- **Physical Activity** — exercise, movement, muscular exertion (*vitality*).
- **Tranquility** — safety, calm, freedom from anxiety, fear, and pain (*relaxation / safety*).

**Vivid exemplars.** Because it's a *profile*, its power is in unusual *combinations*: high **Vengeance** + high **Honor**, low **Tranquility** = the vendetta-driven avenger who will not let a slight go and fears nothing (Inigo Montoya, the Count of Monte Cristo). High **Curiosity** + high **Independence** + low **Social Contact** = the reclusive genius (Newton, Sherlock). High **Family** + high **Order** + high **Tranquility** = the home-making, peace-keeping, routine-loving parent whose whole world is the household. High **Status** + high **Saving** + high **Power**, low **Idealism** = the acquisitive magnate (Ebenezer Scrooge, pre-ghosts). The satiation mechanic is writable: the high-**Vengeance** character gets their revenge and feels the vindication *fade* — and needs another target, which is the tragedy of the monomaniac.

**What it gives you for character depth.** The **broadest empirically-derived desire menu** in this file, and the one closest in *form* to the repo's model (`docs/values-and-stakes.md` explicitly cites Reiss: "motivation is an individual profile of desire-strengths over a shared menu — exactly the model concept"). Sixteen dials give combinatorial room for a distinctive signature, and Reiss's insistence that these are **intrinsic ends** (not means) matches the drives schema's terminal-goal layer. The **satiation/set-point** idea is a genuinely useful mechanic the value theories lack: a desire is a *recurring hunger*, not a fixed weight — feed it and it quiets and returns, which explains cyclical behaviour (the workaholic who rests and is soon restless again). And several desires (Saving, Vengeance, Eating, Physical Activity, Tranquility, Order) name concrete motivators the abstract value theories omit.

**Combines-with.** Maps richly onto everything: Power/Status ≈ Schwartz power + McClelland nPow; Independence ≈ SDT autonomy + Schwartz self-direction; Curiosity ≈ Schwartz stimulation + Openness; Acceptance/Status ≈ Maslow esteem; Social Contact/Family ≈ SDT relatedness + benevolence; Honor ≈ Moral Foundations loyalty/authority + Schwartz tradition/conformity; Idealism ≈ universalism + care/fairness; Tranquility ≈ Maslow safety + Panksepp low-FEAR set-point; Vengeance ≈ RAGE. In the drives schema (`docs/drives-schema.md`) each strong desire is a standing *goal-family* with triggers; the satiation point is a state-layer decay. Order and Tranquility carry a trait flavour (Conscientiousness, low Neuroticism) — a reminder the layers overlap.

**Cautions.** (1) The **RMP is proprietary and commercial**, and independent replication of the exact 16-factor structure is thin — treat the sixteen as a rich *menu*, not a validated dimensional model (same status as Reiss himself is honest about: profiles, not a theory of dynamics). (2) Some "desires" are trait-flavoured (Order, Tranquility) and some are physiological (Eating), mixing levels — that breadth is useful for authoring but sloppy as taxonomy. (3) It says *what* is wanted and *how strongly*, never *which wins in conflict* — that is still the Model's job (`docs/generative-model.md`). (4) Don't author all 16 — pull the salient handful (the repo's sparse-authoring discipline).

**Sources.** Reiss, *Who Am I?* (2000); Reiss, "Multifaceted nature of intrinsic motivation: the theory of 16 basic desires" (*Review of General Psychology*, 2004); Reiss, *The Normal Personality* (2008).

## Schwartz's Theory of Basic Human Values — the value circumplex (repo value spine)

**Lineage.** Shalom **Schwartz** (Hebrew University), "Universals in the content and structure of values" (*Advances in Experimental Social Psychology*, 1992), refining Milton Rokeach's value work into a *structural* theory validated in 80+ countries via the Schwartz Value Survey (SVS) and Portrait Values Questionnaire (PVQ, used in the European Social Survey). Refined into **19 values** by Schwartz et al., "Refining the theory of basic individual values" (*JPSP*, 2012). **This is the repo's value spine** (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`).

**Core idea.** Values are **trans-situational goals, ordered by importance, that serve as guiding principles** in a person's life. The theory's decisive insight is not the *list* of values but their **structure**: values form a **circular motivational continuum** (a *circumplex*), and — because pursuing some values inherently conflicts with pursuing others — **adjacent values are compatible and opposite values conflict**. The map *itself encodes where every trade-off is*. A person's value system is their *priority ordering* around this circle; the conflicts are built into the geometry, not invented per scene.

**Structure in full.**

*The 10 basic values (the classic model), each with its defining motivational goal:*
- **Self-Direction** — independent thought and action; choosing, creating, exploring.
- **Stimulation** — excitement, novelty, challenge, a varied life.
- **Hedonism** — pleasure and sensuous self-gratification.
- **Achievement** — personal success through demonstrating competence by social standards.
- **Power** — social status and prestige, control or dominance over people and resources.
- **Security** — safety, harmony, and stability of society, relationships, and self.
- **Conformity** — restraint of actions and impulses likely to upset or harm others or violate norms.
- **Tradition** — respect, commitment, and acceptance of the customs and ideas of one's culture or religion.
- **Benevolence** — preserving and enhancing the welfare of those close to one (the in-group).
- **Universalism** — understanding, tolerance, appreciation, and protection for *all* people and for nature.

*The two bipolar dimensions (the circle's structure):*
- **Openness to Change ↔ Conservation** — self-direction and stimulation (and hedonism) *vs* security, conformity, and tradition. The axis of *novelty and autonomy vs order and stability*.
- **Self-Enhancement ↔ Self-Transcendence** — power and achievement (and hedonism) *vs* universalism and benevolence. The axis of *self-interest vs concern for others*.

*The refined 19 values (Schwartz et al. 2012 — the same circle, finer partitions):*
- **Self-Direction–Thought** — freedom to cultivate one's own ideas and abilities.
- **Self-Direction–Action** — freedom to determine one's own actions.
- **Stimulation** — excitement, novelty, and change.
- **Hedonism** — pleasure and sensuous gratification.
- **Achievement** — success according to social standards.
- **Power–Dominance** — power through exercising control over people.
- **Power–Resources** — power through control of material and social resources.
- **Face** — security and power through maintaining one's public image and avoiding humiliation. *(Interstitial, between power and security.)*
- **Security–Personal** — safety in one's immediate environment.
- **Security–Societal** — safety and stability in the wider society.
- **Tradition** — maintaining and preserving cultural, family, or religious traditions.
- **Conformity–Rules** — compliance with rules, laws, and formal obligations.
- **Conformity–Interpersonal** — avoidance of upsetting or harming other people.
- **Humility** — recognising one's insignificance in the larger scheme of things. *(Interstitial, between conformity/tradition and universalism.)*
- **Benevolence–Dependability** — being a reliable and trustworthy member of the in-group.
- **Benevolence–Caring** — devotion to the welfare of in-group members.
- **Universalism–Concern** — commitment to equality, justice, and protection for all people.
- **Universalism–Nature** — preservation of the natural environment.
- **Universalism–Tolerance** — acceptance and understanding of those who differ from oneself.

*Two further organising principles running orthogonally around the circle:* **personal focus** (values serving one's own interests — self-direction, stimulation, hedonism, achievement, power, face) vs **social focus** (serving others / the collective — universalism, benevolence, conformity, tradition, security, humility); and **growth / anxiety-free** (self-expansion — self-direction, stimulation, universalism, benevolence) vs **self-protection / anxiety-avoidance** (coping with threat — security, power, face, conformity, tradition, achievement). The self-protection cluster sits on the *bottom* of the circle, growth on the *top*.

**Vivid exemplars.** Whole characters are legible as a *point on the circle* with a *rigidity*: **Conservation** incarnate — Marilla Cuthbert, Mr. Bennet's world before Elizabeth disrupts it, the traditionalist patriarch (security + conformity + tradition maxed, self-direction near zero). **Openness to Change** — Auntie Mame ("life is a banquet"), the wandering artist, Ishmael going to sea (self-direction + stimulation). **Self-Enhancement** — Gordon Gekko, Macbeth once ambition takes him (power + achievement crushing benevolence). **Self-Transcendence** — Dorothea Brooke (*Middlemarch*), Mother-figure saints, the humanitarian (universalism + benevolence). The circumplex *predicts the conflicts*: a Self-Enhancement character (power) and a Self-Transcendence character (universalism) are diametrically opposed and will clash on every resource decision; a Conservation parent and an Openness child are the archetypal generational war (every "leaving the family tradition" story). **Face** (the interstitial value) is the engine of honour-cultures and of every character who would rather be destroyed than *humiliated* — a huge dramatic lever.

**What it gives you for character depth.** The **conflict geometry** is the singular gift: unlike a list of needs you can max out together, Schwartz's circle *guarantees* that valuing one thing highly means valuing its opposite less — so a fully specified value profile is *automatically* a set of vulnerabilities and trade-offs. This is precisely why the repo adopts it as the spine (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`, "the map already encodes where the trade-offs are"). It gives you a *coordinate system* for a whole worldview in two axes, a finer 19-value grain for texture (Power-Dominance vs Power-Resources distinguishes the tyrant who wants to *rule people* from the oligarch who wants to *own things*; Universalism-Nature vs -Concern vs -Tolerance distinguishes three kinds of idealist), and — via the growth/self-protection principle — a bridge to *state*: threat pushes a person toward the self-protection (bottom) values, safety frees them toward growth (top). A frightened universalist becomes a security-clinger; this is characterisation under pressure, mechanised.

**Combines-with.** **This is the repo's value spine** (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`); everything else in this file is a lens on or complement to it. **Schwartz ↔ Moral Foundations** (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`): MFT adds the loyalty/authority/sanctity axes Schwartz's circle under-weights — conservation ≈ binding foundations, self-transcendence ≈ individualizing foundations. **Schwartz ↔ HEXACO** (`trait-models.md`): Honesty-Humility maps onto self-transcendence (high H) vs self-enhancement (low H) at facet level (Lee & Ashton showed this); Openness-to-Change ↔ trait Openness; Conservation ↔ Conscientiousness-Orderliness + low Openness. **↔ SDT:** self-direction = autonomy, achievement = competence, benevolence = relatedness. **↔ the drives schema** (`docs/drives-schema.md`): drives `serve` Schwartz values via their tags — the schema stores the goal, the Model stores the value weighting, the circle says which goals collide. The two axes are the repo's chosen analogue for the Panksepp basis's principled 2-axis structure (`docs/generative-model.md`).

**Cautions.** (1) **Values are the *espoused* good** — everyone rates benevolence high; the discriminating signal is the *relative ordering* and *what a person sacrifices for what*, not the raw ratings (social desirability inflates the prosocial values universally). Author the *priority under conflict*, not the flat profile. (2) A value is not a behaviour — a person who *values* self-direction may *act* conformist under a controlling regime (the value/action gap is drama, per `docs/drives-schema.md` `view: self`). (3) The circle's *pan-cultural structure* is robust, but the *content* of, e.g., tradition or face varies enormously by world (`docs/world-model.md`) — port the structure, localise the content. (4) Don't confuse the value layer (what's worth-oriented) with the trait layer (behavioural style) or the drive layer (concrete goal) — the repo keeps these as distinct layers for good reason (`docs/character-anatomy.md`). (5) 19 values is authoring-heavy; track the 10 (or the 4 higher-order quadrants) for most, refine to 19 only for principals.

**Sources.** Schwartz, "Universals in the content and structure of values" (*Advances in Experimental Social Psychology*, 1992); Schwartz et al., "Refining the theory of basic individual values" (*JPSP*, 2012); Schwartz, "An overview of the Schwartz theory of basic values" (*Online Readings in Psychology and Culture*, 2012); Rokeach, *The Nature of Human Values* (1973, the direct ancestor).

## Rokeach's Value Survey — terminal and instrumental values (the ancestor)

**Lineage.** Milton **Rokeach**, *The Nature of Human Values* (1973), the Rokeach Value Survey (RVS). The direct predecessor Schwartz built on and superseded — but its **terminal/instrumental distinction** remains uniquely useful.

**Core idea.** Values are *enduring beliefs that a specific mode of conduct or end-state is personally or socially preferable*, organised into a **hierarchy of importance**. Rokeach's lasting contribution is the split between **ends** and **means**: **terminal values** (desirable *end-states of existence* — the destinations) and **instrumental values** (desirable *modes of conduct* — the ways of travelling). A person's value system is a *rank-ordering* of each set.

**Structure in full.**

*18 Terminal values (end-states):* a comfortable life, an exciting life, a sense of accomplishment, a world at peace, a world of beauty, equality, family security, freedom, happiness, inner harmony, mature love, national security, pleasure, salvation, self-respect, social recognition, true friendship, wisdom.

*18 Instrumental values (modes of conduct):* ambitious, broad-minded, capable, cheerful, clean, courageous, forgiving, helpful, honest, imaginative, independent, intellectual, logical, loving, loyal, obedient, polite, responsible, self-controlled.

**Vivid exemplars.** The terminal/instrumental gap is a character engine: a person whose *terminal* value is "a world at peace" but whose top *instrumental* value is "obedient" behaves utterly differently from one who shares the terminal value but ranks "courageous" or "independent" highest — same destination, opposite road, and they will clash bitterly as allies (the reformer vs the revolutionary who want the same world). A character can also hold a **terminal value they betray by their means**: values "salvation" (terminal) but ranks "ambitious" over "honest" (instrumental) — the corrupt priest, the ends-justify-means idealist.

**What it gives you for character depth.** The **ends/means distinction** is the gift, and it maps *exactly* onto the drives schema's **terminal/instrumental goal chain** (`docs/drives-schema.md`). It lets you separate *what a character is ultimately for* from *how they are willing to get there* — and the richest conflicts (internal and external) live in that gap: the person whose means corrupt their ends, the ally who shares your destination but not your methods.

**Combines-with.** Schwartz absorbed and structured Rokeach's content (Schwartz's 10 values reorganise these 36 by motivational goal). Terminal ↔ instrumental = drives-schema terminal ↔ instrumental goals (`docs/drives-schema.md`) — the single cleanest cross-link. Instrumental values (honest, loyal, courageous, self-controlled) are largely *trait-expressions* (`trait-models.md`) — the bridge between the value and trait layers.

**Cautions.** (1) **Superseded by Schwartz** as a structural theory — the rank-ordering method is crude and the 36 items overlap; use it for the *terminal/instrumental idea*, not as your primary value taxonomy. (2) The lists are culturally dated (1970s American). (3) Forced-ranking assumes values are commensurable, which under conflict they often aren't.

**Sources.** Rokeach, *The Nature of Human Values* (1973); Rokeach, *Beliefs, Attitudes, and Values* (1968).

## Moral Foundations Theory — the moral taste buds (repo ethics lens)

**Lineage.** Jonathan **Haidt**, Craig **Joseph**, and Jesse **Graham**; Haidt & Joseph (2004), Graham, Haidt & Nosek, "Liberals and conservatives rely on different sets of moral foundations" (*JPSP*, 2009), and Haidt's *The Righteous Mind* (2012). Refined into the six-factor **MFQ-2** by Atari, Haidt et al. (2023). **This is the repo's ethics lens** (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`), adding the loyalty/authority/sanctity axes Schwartz under-weights.

**Core idea.** Moral judgment is **fast, intuitive, and emotional** ("the emotional dog and its rational tail" — *social intuitionism*: we feel the verdict first and reason to justify it after). These intuitions run on a small set of innate, evolutionarily-prepared **foundations** — "moral taste buds" — and, crucially, **different people and cultures weight the foundations differently**, which is why moral disagreement is so intractable: it is not that one side is immoral, but that they are *tasting different flavours*. This is *literally the repo's model* (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`): a loyalty-weighted person and a fairness-weighted person clash *even when both are good*.

**Structure in full.**

*The five classic foundations (each a virtue/vice pair, with its evolutionary trigger):*
- **Care / Harm** — sensitivity to suffering; compassion, kindness, protection of the vulnerable. *Adaptive challenge: caring for offspring.* Violated by cruelty.
- **Fairness / Cheating** — reciprocity, justice, rights, proportionality. *Adaptive challenge: reaping the rewards of cooperation.* Violated by cheating, exploitation, free-riding.
- **Loyalty / Betrayal** — group solidarity, patriotism, "us," self-sacrifice for the tribe. *Adaptive challenge: forming coalitions.* Violated by betrayal, treason.
- **Authority / Subversion** — respect for legitimate hierarchy, tradition, duty, deference. *Adaptive challenge: forging hierarchies.* Violated by disobedience, disrespect.
- **Sanctity / Degradation** — purity, the sacred, disgust at contamination, elevation, the body as temple. *Adaptive challenge: avoiding pathogens (and, elevated, the noble).* Violated by defilement, degradation.

*The proposed sixth (Haidt, *The Righteous Mind*):*
- **Liberty / Oppression** — resentment of domination and coercion, the drive to band against a bully or tyrant, reactance against restraint. (The repo lists this as the sixth, `docs/values-and-stakes.md`.)

*Two clusters (a key structural claim):*
- **Individualizing foundations** — **Care** and **Fairness** — protect the individual; weighted heavily by political liberals, and by WEIRD cultures generally.
- **Binding foundations** — **Loyalty, Authority, Sanctity** — bind individuals into groups and constrain the self; political conservatives (and most traditional/collectivist cultures) weight all foundations more *evenly*, giving the binding three far more moral force than liberals do.

*The MFQ-2 refinement (Atari, Haidt et al., 2023):* splits **Fairness** into **Equality** (equal outcomes and treatment — *individualizing*) and **Proportionality** (rewards in proportion to merit and contribution — reclassified toward the *binding* side), yielding six: **Care, Equality, Proportionality, Loyalty, Authority, Purity**. (Note: MFQ-2 does *not* include Liberty — a reminder that "the six foundations" names two *different* six-lists depending on source. The repo's six = the classic five + Liberty; the psychometric six = Care/Equality/Proportionality/Loyalty/Authority/Purity.)

**Vivid exemplars.** The theory's whole power is the **binding-vs-individualizing collision between two sympathetic characters**: *Antigone* is the ur-example — Antigone weights **Sanctity/Loyalty** (her brother *must* be buried, the sacred rite, kin) against Creon's **Authority/Loyalty-to-the-state** (the law of the city must hold), and *both are right within their foundation*. A Care-driven character (weights harm-avoidance above all) and a Loyalty-driven one clash over the whistle-blower question: *do you expose your own corrupt unit (fairness/care to outsiders) or protect your brothers (loyalty)?* — the moral spine of every "rat vs stand-up-guy" story. Sanctity incarnate: characters for whom some things are *not for sale at any price* (defiling a temple, eating the forbidden thing, the taboo) — the disgust-driven refusal a pure cost-benefit character can't feel. Javert is **Authority** maxed against Valjean's **Care/Fairness**; Marvel's Captain America (individualizing, will defy any order to prevent harm) vs a loyalist soldier. **Liberty/Oppression** drives every rebel-against-the-tyrant arc (Spartacus, V, Katniss).

**What it gives you for character depth.** MFT's distinct contribution is a **moral taxonomy that makes "two good people at war" mechanical and principled** — the repo's central dramatic engine (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`). Schwartz gives you *what a person values*; MFT gives you *what a person finds morally outrageous* — and the binding foundations (loyalty, authority, sanctity) name moral force that the Care/Fairness-centric modern reader (and the naive writer) tends to *dismiss as mere prejudice*, which is exactly why an honour-killing, a blasphemy, or a betrayal-of-the-clan can be written from *inside* as a genuine moral horror rather than a cartoon. It converts villains-of-conviction into people. And — the repo's elegant reuse — MFT is *also a harm taxonomy*: the foundations name what a moral act *violates* (care/**harm**, fairness/**cheating**, loyalty/**betrayal**, sanctity/**degradation**), so a character's moral weights *are* their moral vulnerabilities — "lying" is a care+fairness violation *by definition*, no per-act authoring (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`, `severity(e)`).

**Combines-with.** **The repo's ethics lens on top of the Schwartz spine** (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`): the binding foundations ≈ Schwartz conservation (tradition/conformity/security) + in-group benevolence; individualizing ≈ universalism + care-benevolence. **↔ HEXACO** (`trait-models.md`): low Honesty-Humility predicts weighting care/fairness *lightly* relative to self-interest; the Dark-Triad villain's moral weights collapse toward zero with *moral disengagement* as the justification machinery. **↔ the harm/severity computation** (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`, `docs/state-engine.md`): the foundations double as the social/moral harm-vector. **↔ Panksepp** (`docs/generative-model.md`): care ↔ CARE, loyalty ↔ CARE-for-group, authority/subversion ↔ RAGE at disrespect. *Note:* the repo's 7-primary basis (SEEKING·FEAR·RAGE·CARE·PANIC/GRIEF·LUST·PLAY) has **no disgust primary** — Panksepp treated disgust as a reflexive sensory affect, not one of the seven emotional-operating-systems — so sanctity/degradation has no clean primary and is best modelled as a *derived contamination response* (a learned appraisal, often RAGE- or FEAR-tinged), the one MFT foundation the affective basis under-covers. **↔ world-model** (`docs/world-model.md`): the *foundation weighting of a whole culture* is a worldbuilding dial (an honour culture maxes loyalty/sanctity/authority).

**Cautions.** (1) **"The six foundations" is ambiguous** — the repo/Haidt six (classic 5 + Liberty) ≠ the MFQ-2 psychometric six (Care/Equality/Proportionality/Loyalty/Authority/Purity). State which you mean. (2) The **liberal/conservative mapping is US-politics-specific and time-bound** — port the *foundations*, not the American partisan alignment, into other worlds. (3) The foundation *count and independence* is contested (some argue Care/Fairness/Liberty collapse, or that the whole set reduces to fewer dimensions); treat six as a strong, useful carving, not settled fact. (4) Social intuitionism (feel-then-justify) is the mechanism, but people *do* sometimes reason to a moral conclusion — don't make every character purely intuitive. (5) The cliché trap: writing binding-foundation morality (honour, purity, loyalty) as *automatically* backward or villainous — the theory's whole point is that it is *felt as sacred from inside*; write it that way or you've missed it.

**Sources.** Graham, Haidt & Nosek, "Liberals and conservatives rely on different sets of moral foundations" (*JPSP*, 2009); Haidt, *The Righteous Mind: Why Good People Are Divided by Politics and Religion* (2012); Graham et al., "Moral Foundations Theory: the pragmatic validity of moral pluralism" (*Advances in Experimental Social Psychology*, 2013); Atari, Haidt et al., "Morality beyond the WEIRD" / "Moral Foundations Questionnaire-2" (*JPSP* / validation studies, 2023).

## Regulatory Focus Theory — promotion and prevention

**Lineage.** E. Tory **Higgins**, "Beyond pleasure and pain" (*American Psychologist*, 1997) and *Promotion and Prevention* (1998), Columbia. Grows out of Higgins's earlier **Self-Discrepancy Theory** (ideal self vs ought self, 1987).

**Core idea.** People pursue goals through two distinct **self-regulatory systems** with different concerns, strategies, and emotional signatures. It is not *what* you want but the *orientation of the wanting* — toward attaining gains, or toward avoiding losses. The two systems trace to two kinds of self-guide: the **ideal self** (hopes, aspirations — promotion) and the **ought self** (duties, obligations, responsibilities — prevention).

**Structure in full.**

- **Promotion focus** — concerned with **advancement, growth, aspirations, ideals, gains**. Strategy: *eagerness* — pursue all means to hits, take chances, ensure against errors of *omission* (missing a gain). Emotional range: success → **joy/elation**; failure → **dejection/sadness**. Sensitive to the *presence and absence of positive* outcomes.
- **Prevention focus** — concerned with **security, safety, duties, obligations, responsibilities, the avoidance of loss**. Strategy: *vigilance* — be careful, avoid mistakes, ensure against errors of *commission* (a costly wrong move). Emotional range: success → **calm/relief/quiescence**; failure → **anxiety/agitation**. Sensitive to the *presence and absence of negative* outcomes.
- **Regulatory fit** — when the *manner* of pursuit matches the focus (eager pursuit of a promotion goal, vigilant pursuit of a prevention goal), motivation and felt-rightness rise. A mismatch drains it.

**Vivid exemplars.** The **promotion** hero leans in and reaches — the entrepreneur, the explorer, the gambler who plays to win and grieves (not panics) at a loss; Icarus is promotion focus fatal (all reach, no vigilance). The **prevention** character plays not to lose — the careful steward, the security chief, the parent whose every decision is *what could go wrong*, the character who feels *relief* rather than joy when it works out and *dread* rather than sadness when it doesn't. A promotion partner and a prevention partner in the same crisis are a scene by themselves: one wants to *seize the opportunity*, the other to *not make it worse* — and each reads the other as reckless or as cowardly.

**What it gives you for character depth.** It supplies the **emotional and strategic *signature* of goal-pursuit** that the value theories omit — two characters with identical goals (both want the child safe, both want the deal) will *pursue, feel, and fail differently* by focus: the promotion one takes the bold play and is crushed-sad if it fails; the prevention one hedges and is anxious-relieved. It cleanly separates **two flavours of the same emotion** (dejection vs agitation as failure-responses; joy vs relief as success-responses) — useful precision for rendering interiority. And ideal-self vs ought-self is a direct handle on the drives schema: a promotion goal serves an *ideal* (aspiration); a prevention goal serves an *ought* (duty/fear).

**Combines-with.** Prevention ↔ Schwartz conservation + self-protection cluster + Panksepp FEAR + SDT introjected "ought" regulation; promotion ↔ openness-to-change + growth cluster + SEEKING + intrinsic/identified motivation. Directly maps to the drives schema's `coping_engagement` (approach/avoid) and to McClelland (nAch has a promotion flavour; fear-of-failure a prevention flavour). Regulatory focus ≈ Gray's BAS (promotion) / BIS-FFFS (prevention) at the trait level (`trait-models.md`). The ideal/ought split ↔ self-image and self-guides (`docs/self-and-perception.md`).

**Cautions.** (1) It is an *orientation*, not a full motive theory — pair it with a value/need model for the *content*. (2) Focus is partly **situationally induced** (framing a task as gain vs loss shifts it), so it's both a trait and a state — hold the (mean, variability) distinction (`trait-models.md`). (3) Don't over-tidy the emotions — real people feel blended dejection-and-agitation; the clean split is a tendency.

**Sources.** Higgins, "Beyond pleasure and pain" (*American Psychologist*, 1997); Higgins, "Self-discrepancy: a theory relating self and affect" (*Psychological Review*, 1987); Higgins, "Promotion and prevention: regulatory focus as a motivational principle" (*Advances in Experimental Social Psychology*, 1998).

## Achievement Goal Theory & Mindset — why two strivers break differently

**Lineage.** A convergent tradition on *the aims inside achievement*: John **Nicholls**, Carol **Dweck**, and Carol **Ames** (1980s) split achievement goals into **mastery** vs **performance**; Andrew **Elliot** & Judith **Harackiewicz** crossed that with **approach vs avoidance** (Elliot & Church, "A hierarchical model of approach and avoidance achievement motivation," *JPSP*, 1997 — the trichotomous model; Elliot & McGregor, "A 2×2 achievement goal framework," *JPSP*, 2001 — the four-cell grid). Running alongside it, Dweck's **implicit theories / mindset** work (*Self-Theories*, 1999; *Mindset*, 2006) — do you construe ability as **fixed** (entity theory) or **growable** (incremental theory)? The direct descendant of McClelland/Atkinson's achievement-motivation line, refined from one drive into a *structure of aims*.

**Core idea.** nAch is not one thing. *What a striver is actually pursuing* — and *what they're fleeing* — determines how they choose tasks, interpret feedback, and hold up under failure. Two axes cross: the **definition** of success (mastering the task vs out-performing others) and the **valence** of the pursuit (moving *toward* competence vs *away from* incompetence). Underneath sits the mindset that makes the whole thing load-bearing: if ability is *fixed*, every failure is a verdict on your worth (and effort itself is damning — "if I had it I wouldn't have to try"); if ability is *growable*, failure is information and effort is the path.

**Structure in full.**

*The 2×2 achievement-goal grid (Elliot & McGregor):*
- **Mastery-approach** — strive to *learn, improve, master the task, exceed one's own past best.* The self-referential craftsman. Resilient; seeks challenge and feedback; effort-valuing.
- **Mastery-avoidance** — strive *not to lose one's skill, not to misunderstand, not to fall short of one's own standard.* The perfectionist and the declining expert ("I must not get worse"); anxious, self-monitoring.
- **Performance-approach** — strive to *out-do others, prove superiority, win the comparison.* The competitor; can be highly effective but brittle when the ranking turns against them.
- **Performance-avoidance** — strive to *not look stupid, not be last, avoid public failure.* The most corrosive cell: self-handicapping, effort-withdrawal ("I didn't really try"), the student who won't raise their hand. Driven by **fear of failure** (Atkinson's *motive to avoid failure*), not the pursuit of success.

*The mindset substrate (Dweck):* **entity/fixed** — ability is a fixed trait to be *proven*, so challenge is threat, effort is evidence of deficiency, and failure indicts the self → tilts toward performance and avoidance goals. **Incremental/growth** — ability is malleable and *developed*, so challenge is opportunity, effort is virtue, and failure is feedback → tilts toward mastery-approach.

**Vivid exemplars.** **Mastery-approach:** the potter who throws the same bowl a thousand times to get it right, indifferent to the competition (the *Whiplash* ideal Fletcher perverts). **Performance-approach:** Amadeus's *Salieri wanting to be seen as the greatest*; the athlete who only enjoys winning. **Performance-avoidance / fear-of-failure:** the gifted child paralysed by a reputation for brilliance who stops trying so that failure can be blamed on not-trying rather than not-having-it — the fixed-mindset trap in one character; the promising figure who *sabotages themselves* to keep the verdict ambiguous. **Mastery-avoidance:** the ageing master terrified of the day his hand slips — the champion who plays not to be dethroned. The clash: a mastery-approach mentor ("fail, that's how you learn") and a performance-avoidance protégé ("if I fail I'm nothing") talk straight past each other — a whole teaching-relationship arc.

**What it gives you for character depth.** It carves *striving itself* into four textures the single nAch dial can't — and pins each to a **feedback-and-failure signature** that is pure interiority: how a character reads a setback (data vs verdict), whether they seek or dodge hard tasks, and *why* a talented person underperforms (performance-avoidance + fixed mindset → the self-handicap). **Fear of failure is the key hook** — it is a drives-schema *fear* (`docs/drives-schema.md`) with a ready-made defense (withdraw effort, self-handicap, perfectionist over-control), and it names the engine of the "wasted talent" and "paralysed prodigy" characters directly. Mindset gives a *changeable* variable — a fixed→growth shift is a legible arc (the whole point of the coming-of-age story where the character learns effort isn't shameful).

**Combines-with.** Refines **McClelland nAch** into its aims; performance goals ↔ Schwartz *achievement* + external/status motivation, mastery goals ↔ SDT *competence* + intrinsic motivation. Approach/avoidance ↔ **Regulatory Focus** (mastery-approach ~ promotion; performance-avoidance ~ prevention) and ↔ Gray's BAS/BIS (`trait-models.md`). Fear-of-failure = a drives-schema fear+defense; performance-avoidance self-handicapping is a `view: self` gap (the stated goal masks the fear, `docs/drives-schema.md`). Fixed vs growth mindset ↔ the self-image / self-theory layer (`docs/self-and-perception.md`); the entity-theorist's *fragile* self-worth is contingent-self-esteem (SDT introjection). In Panksepp terms mastery-approach rides **SEEKING**; performance-avoidance rides **FEAR + PANIC** (fear of the shame of exposure).

**Cautions.** (1) The **mindset literature is contested** — the original effects were large but several growth-mindset *interventions* have produced small or null effects at scale (the 2019 National Study of Learning Mindsets found modest, subgroup-specific gains); use fixed/growth as a *characterological lens on how a person construes ability*, not as a proven lever you can pull. (2) The 2×2 cells are *tendencies*, not types — real strivers mix goals and shift by domain (mastery in the craft, performance in public). (3) Performance goals are not simply "bad" — performance-approach can drive excellent, sustained achievement; the corrosive cell is avoidance. (4) It is an *achievement-domain* theory — don't stretch it past competence-relevant situations.

**Sources.** Dweck, "Motivational processes affecting learning" (*American Psychologist*, 1986); Dweck, *Self-Theories* (1999); Dweck, *Mindset* (2006); Elliot & Church, "A hierarchical model of approach and avoidance achievement motivation" (*JPSP*, 1997); Elliot & McGregor, "A 2×2 achievement goal framework" (*JPSP*, 2001); Nicholls, "Achievement motivation: conceptions of ability, subjective experience, task choice, and performance" (*Psychological Review*, 1984).

## Adjacent (morals): the moral-reasoning traditions MFT reacted against

**Lineage & core idea.** MFT's "the emotional dog and its rational tail" is a deliberate strike at a **rationalist-developmental** tradition that dominated moral psychology for decades — and that tradition is still live-material, because it carves morality along axes MFT sets aside. Track it as the *reasoning* counterpart to MFT's *intuition*.
- **Lawrence Kohlberg — stages of moral development** (*Essays on Moral Development*, 1981; building on Piaget). Moral reasoning matures through three levels, six stages: **preconventional** (1: punishment-avoidance; 2: instrumental self-interest, "what's in it for me"), **conventional** (3: good-boy/good-girl, approval and relationships; 4: law-and-order, duty to the social system), **postconventional** (5: social contract, rights and the greater good; 6: universal ethical principles, conscience above law). Scored via responses to dilemmas (the **Heinz dilemma** — steal the drug to save your wife?): the *stage is in the reasoning, not the verdict*. Higher stages are rarer; most adults reason at stages 3–4.
- **Carol Gilligan — the ethic of care** (*In a Different Voice*, 1982). A frontal critique of Kohlberg: his justice-and-rights ladder privileged an *ethic of justice* (abstract, principled, "male-coded" in her data) over an equally mature **ethic of care** (relational, contextual, responsibility-to-particular-others) that his scoring mis-ranked as lower. Two moral orientations, not one ladder — *justice* (fairness, rights, impartiality) vs *care* (attachment, need, not-abandoning).
- **Elliot Turiel — domain theory** (*The Development of Social Knowledge*, 1983). Children distinguish, early and cross-culturally, the **moral** domain (harm, fairness, rights — obligatory, universal) from the **conventional** domain (etiquette, custom, authority-set norms — alterable, local) from the **personal** domain (private preference, none-of-your-business). A rival to MFT: Turiel argues much of what Haidt calls *binding-foundation morality* (purity, authority) is really *convention*, not morality proper — a genuine live dispute worth knowing.

**Vivid exemplars.** Kohlberg is a **stage-ladder for a character's moral voice**: a stage-2 character weighs every choice as a trade ("what do I get"); a stage-4 character (Javert) cannot conceive of right *above* the law; a stage-6 character (Antigone, Thoreau, the conscientious objector) breaks the law *for a principle above it* — and stage-4-vs-stage-6 is the whole civil-disobedience drama, two moral maturities colliding. Gilligan's split is the **justice-vs-care collision** rendered as gendered-but-not-really moral languages: the character who does the *principled* thing (turn in the guilty friend — justice) against the one who does the *relational* thing (stand by them — care), each hearing the other as cold or as corrupt. Turiel's moral/conventional line is the engine of the *is-this-actually-wrong-or-just-not-done* argument (the taboo-breaker who insists the taboo is mere convention vs the one who feels it as sacred — which is exactly the MFT sanctity dispute from the other side).

**What it gives you for character depth.** A **developmental axis MFT lacks** — a character can be *morally serious yet reasoning at a low stage*, or grow up the ladder across an arc (the coming-of-age from stage-2 self-interest to stage-4 duty to stage-5/6 principle). Gilligan hands you the **justice↔care axis** as two whole moral *voices* (it prefigures MFT's care-vs-fairness but frames it as a mature either/or, not a taste-weighting) — and it re-genders the moral-conflict scene usefully. Turiel gives the **is-it-moral-or-merely-conventional** dispute a name, which is the crux of every generational and cross-cultural values fight.

**Combines-with.** Kohlberg's conventional level (stages 3–4: approval, law-and-order, duty) ↔ Schwartz *conformity/tradition* + MFT *authority/loyalty* (the **binding** cluster); postconventional (5–6: rights, universal principle) ↔ Schwartz *universalism* + MFT *care/fairness* (**individualizing**) — so Kohlberg's ladder is *roughly* a developmental climb from binding-embedded to individualizing-principled morality, which is exactly the axis MFT flattens into a taste-profile. Gilligan's *care* ↔ MFT Care + Schwartz benevolence + SDT relatedness; *justice* ↔ MFT Fairness + Schwartz universalism-concern. Turiel's moral/conventional/personal split ↔ the repo's harm-vector (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`): "moral" violations carry harm-vectors, "conventional" ones are *world-local* norms (`docs/world-model.md`), "personal" ones carry no harm at all — a useful gate on *what even counts as a transgression* in a given world.

**Cautions.** (1) **Kohlberg is empirically battered** — stage 6 barely appears in data (Kohlberg himself dropped it as a separate scored stage), the stages aren't cleanly sequential-invariant, and the dilemmas are culturally narrow; use the *ladder as a scale of moral voice*, not a validated developmental fact. (2) Gilligan's *empirical* gender claim (women reason care, men justice) largely **did not replicate** — the *two-orientations* idea survived; the sex difference did not. Keep the justice/care axis, drop the gender essentialism. (3) The whole tradition is **rationalist** — it locates morality in reasoning, which MFT's evidence (moral dumbfounding, fast intuition) substantially undercuts; hold both, and let most characters *feel* the verdict and *reason* to justify it (MFT), while a few genuinely deliberate up the ladder (Kohlberg). (4) These are *reasoning/development* frames, not a weighting menu — pair with Schwartz/MFT for the *content* of what's valued.

**Sources.** Kohlberg, *Essays on Moral Development, Vol. I: The Philosophy of Moral Development* (1981); Gilligan, *In a Different Voice* (1982); Turiel, *The Development of Social Knowledge: Morality and Convention* (1983); Rest et al., *Postconventional Moral Thinking* (1999, the neo-Kohlbergian DIT revision).

## Adjacent: existential motivation — the will to meaning, and the denial of death

**Lineage & core idea.** A tradition that names a driver the needs/values models under-weight: the pull toward **meaning** and the push *away from* mortality.
- **Viktor Frankl — logotherapy / the will to meaning.** *Man's Search for Meaning* (1946), from Frankl's survival of the camps. The primary human motivation is not Freud's *will to pleasure* nor Adler's *will to power* but the **will to meaning** — the need to find purpose. Meaning is found three ways: through *creative* work/deeds, through *experiential* value (love, beauty), and — the deepest — through the *attitude* one takes toward *unavoidable suffering*. The "**existential vacuum**" (meaninglessness, boredom) is the modern pathology. Frankl's line: those who have a *why* to live can bear almost any *how*.
- **Terror Management Theory (TMT).** Greenberg, Pyszczynski & Solomon (1986), building on Ernest Becker's *The Denial of Death* (1973). Humans are uniquely aware of their own mortality, which creates potential paralysing terror; we manage it by investing in a **cultural worldview** (a meaning-system that promises symbolic or literal immortality) and by pursuing **self-esteem** (being a valuable contributor *within* that worldview). **Mortality salience** (reminders of death) intensifies worldview-defence, in-group loyalty, and hostility to those who threaten the worldview.

**Vivid exemplars.** The will to meaning: any character who *chooses their attitude* toward unchosen suffering — the prisoner who keeps his dignity, the terminally ill character who finds their purpose in their last months, Frankl himself. The existential vacuum: the successful-but-empty midlife character (the *American Beauty* / *Fight Club* protagonist), whose crisis is *meaninglessness*, not deprivation. TMT: the character whose brush with death sends them *harder* into their tribe, their religion, their legacy-project (building the monument, having the child, writing the book so *something* survives them) — and the flip, the character who defends their worldview with lethal fury precisely because a heretic threatens the immortality-story that holds their terror at bay (the inquisitor, the zealot).

**What it gives you for character depth.** These name the **highest-stakes motive** — the one people will trade survival itself for (the repo's "sacrifice the floor for a higher stake," `docs/values-and-stakes.md`). Meaning-seeking is the driver behind self-transcendence, legacy, martyrdom, and the mid-story crisis of the person who has *everything and nothing*. TMT gives a mechanism for *why worldviews are defended so violently* and why mortality (a death in the story) reliably intensifies loyalty, faith, and legacy-drives — a usable lever: put death in the scene and watch the binding foundations and the meaning-projects flare.

**Combines-with.** Meaning ↔ Maslow self-transcendence, Schwartz universalism, SDT integrated regulation, the drives schema's terminal-goal layer (a *why* is the ultimate terminal goal). TMT worldview-defence ↔ Moral Foundations binding cluster + Schwartz conservation under threat + the growth→self-protection shift. Mortality salience is a *state-layer* buff (`docs/state-engine.md`) that reweights the whole value profile toward the protective/meaning cluster.

**Cautions.** (1) Frankl's logotherapy is a *clinical philosophy*, not a measured construct — use it for its truth about meaning-as-driver, not as psychometrics. (2) TMT is empirically contested — several mortality-salience effects have failed to replicate in large multi-lab studies; treat the *mechanism* as an evocative, plausible lever, not established fact. (3) Don't make every character an existential seeker — most people, most days, run on the ordinary needs; meaning-motivation *spikes* at thresholds (death, failure, the empty summit).

**Sources.** Frankl, *Man's Search for Meaning* (1946); Becker, *The Denial of Death* (1973); Greenberg, Pyszczynski & Solomon, "The causes and consequences of a need for self-esteem: a terror management theory" (in *Public Self and Private Self*, 1986); Solomon, Greenberg & Pyszczynski, *The Worm at the Core* (2015).

## Adjacent (craft tool): the Enneagram — nine cores of desire and fear

**Lineage.** A modern synthesis (Oscar **Ichazo**, 1960s; Claudio **Naranjo**, who added psychological content; popularised by Don Riso & Russ Hudson and by Helen Palmer, 1980s–90s) drawing on older contemplative sources. **Not an empirically validated personality model** — but one of the most *useful writer's tools* in circulation, because unlike the trait models it is built directly around a **core fear paired with a core desire** — exactly the drives-schema shape (`docs/drives-schema.md` goals + fears/wounds).

**Core idea.** Nine personality "types," each organised around a **basic fear**, a corresponding **basic desire**, a **core motivation**, and a characteristic **vice/passion** and defensive strategy. The type is *what you're running from and toward* — a motivational core, not a behavioural style. Types have **wings** (adjacent flavours), **arrows** (how each shifts under stress/*disintegration* and growth/*integration*), and cluster into three **centres**: **Gut/Body** (8, 9, 1 — core emotion *anger*, concern *control/autonomy*), **Heart** (2, 3, 4 — core emotion *shame*, concern *image/worth*), **Head** (5, 6, 7 — core emotion *fear*, concern *security/guidance*).

**Structure in full** (type: basic fear → basic desire → the vice):

1. **The Reformer / Perfectionist** — fear of being *corrupt/defective/bad* → desire to be *good, right, with integrity*; vice: *anger* (resentment at imperfection). *Javert, Atticus, the rigid idealist.*
2. **The Helper / Giver** — fear of being *unloved/unwanted* → desire to *be loved*; vice: *pride* (in being indispensable). *The self-sacrificing caretaker, Molly Weasley, the smothering mother.*
3. **The Achiever / Performer** — fear of being *worthless without achievement* → desire to *be valuable, admired*; vice: *deceit* (becoming the image). *Gatsby, Don Draper, the golden-child striver.*
4. **The Individualist / Romantic** — fear of *having no identity/significance* → desire to *be uniquely themselves*; vice: *envy* (of what others have). *The tortured artist, Holden Caulfield, the melancholic aesthete.*
5. **The Investigator / Observer** — fear of being *useless, overwhelmed, depleted* → desire to *be capable and self-sufficient through knowledge*; vice: *avarice* (of energy, resources, privacy). *Sherlock, the reclusive scholar, the detached expert.*
6. **The Loyalist / Skeptic** — fear of being *without support/guidance* → desire for *security and certainty*; vice: *fear/anxiety* (scanning for threat). *The dutiful soldier, the anxious loyal friend, the conspiracy-minded doubter.*
7. **The Enthusiast** — fear of being *trapped in pain/deprivation* → desire to *be satisfied, free, happy*; vice: *gluttony* (for experience). *The manic adventurer, Peter Pan, the pleasure-chasing escapist.*
8. **The Challenger / Protector** — fear of being *controlled/harmed/vulnerable* → desire to *be in control, protect themselves and their own*; vice: *lust/excess* (intensity, dominance). *The strongman, Tony Soprano, the fierce guardian.*
9. **The Peacemaker** — fear of *loss/separation, conflict, fragmentation* → desire for *inner and outer peace*; vice: *sloth* (of self, self-forgetting). *The conflict-avoidant mediator, the go-along-to-get-along everyman.*

**Vivid exemplars.** (Woven above.) The Enneagram's craft power is in **the fear→desire→defensive-behaviour chain** and the **stress/growth arrows**: a Type 3 (Achiever) under stress "disintegrates" toward the disengaged apathy of an unhealthy 9; a Type 1 (Reformer) under stress toward the moodiness of a 4 — giving a *predicted direction of decompensation* under pressure that is genuinely useful for arc-writing.

**What it gives you for character depth.** It is the framework here that most directly hands you a **wound-fear-desire-defense package** — the repo's fears/wounds family (`docs/drives-schema.md`) in ready-made form: each type *is* a "protects [self-image] via [defense]" spec plus the goal it chases. The stress/growth arrows give a *causal direction of change* for arcs (what this person becomes when they crack, and what integration looks like). For rapidly sketching a *coherent* motivational core — one fear generating one desire generating one habitual defensive strategy — it is faster and more character-shaped than any trait model.

**Combines-with.** Maps loosely onto Big Five/HEXACO (Type 1 ≈ high Conscientiousness; 7 ≈ high Extraversion/Openness; 5 ≈ high Openness/introversion; 6 ≈ high Neuroticism; 8 ≈ low Agreeableness/high assertiveness) but *not* cleanly — its value is *orthogonal* to traits: it carves by *motivational core*, which is the drives/values layer, not the trait layer. Each type's basic fear ↔ a drives-schema wound+fear; basic desire ↔ a terminal goal serving a Schwartz value (1→conformity/universalism, 3→achievement, 8→power/security, 2→benevolence). The three centres (anger/shame/fear) ↔ Panksepp RAGE / a shame-blend / FEAR.

**Cautions.** (1) **It has no empirical validity** — factor analyses do not recover nine types; test-retest and construct validity are poor. *Use it as a generative craft scaffold, never as a claim about real psychology*, and never let a character be *only* a type (the "she's a Type 4" flatness is as bad as any horoscope). (2) It **typologises** (nine boxes) where the repo *dimensionalises* (continuous weightings) — convert a type into a *fear+desire+defense triple you weight and blend*, don't inherit the box. (3) The system is sprawling and semi-mystical (wings, arrows, subtypes, levels of health) — take the fear→desire core and the stress-direction; leave the cosmology. (4) Cliché trap: the arrows and vices can become deterministic ("all 8s are bullies") — they are *leans with tails*, like any disposition (`trait-models.md`).

**Sources.** Riso & Hudson, *The Wisdom of the Enneagram* (1999) and *Personality Types* (1996); Palmer, *The Enneagram* (1988); Naranjo, *Character and Neurosis* (1994). *(Cite as a craft framework; there is no strong peer-reviewed validation literature to point to — which is itself the caution.)*

## Value conflict as the engine of character

Everything above is raw material for one thing: **the forced choice between two things a person genuinely cares about.** A menu you can satisfy all at once produces a wish-list, not a character. The frameworks in this file are chosen because each exposes *built-in oppositions* — and those oppositions, instantiated by a scene, are the drama the repo's decision engine exists to resolve (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`, `docs/decision-engine.md`, `docs/drives-schema.md`). This section is the master key; read it as the *point* of the rest.

**Where the conflicts are built in (the tension map).** You do not have to invent trade-offs — the menus encode them:
- **Survival vs principle** — Maslow's floor (physiological/safety) against everything above it. The oldest choice: eat the forbidden thing / betray the cause / abandon the person, or starve. When a character sacrifices the *floor* for a higher stake, that is the repo's "dramatic gold."
- **Self-enhancement vs self-transcendence** — Schwartz's vertical axis: my power/achievement against others' welfare. Macbeth's whole fall. Every "sell your soul for the throne."
- **Openness vs conservation** — Schwartz's horizontal axis: my freedom/novelty against the security and order of the group. Every leaving-home, every generational war, every "the family expects."
- **Autonomy vs relatedness** — SDT's two needs pulling apart: to be my own author vs to belong. Every doomed love-vs-duty, every child of a demanding family.
- **Care vs loyalty** — Moral Foundations: harm to an outsider vs betrayal of my own. The whistle-blower's agony; *do I turn in my brother?*
- **Liberty vs authority** — Moral Foundations: resistance to domination vs respect for legitimate order. Antigone vs Creon; the rebel vs the institution they half-believe in.
- **Fairness (equality) vs proportionality/loyalty** — should the reward go to the neediest, the most deserving, or my own? Every distribution scene.
- **Justice vs care** (Gilligan) — the *impartial principle* (turn in the guilty friend; the rule is the rule) against the *relational responsibility* (stand by them; don't abandon the particular person in front of you). Two mature moral voices, each hearing the other as cold or as corrupt.
- **Ideal vs ought** (Regulatory Focus) / **terminal vs instrumental** (Rokeach) — the aspiration I chase vs the duty I owe; the destination I want vs the means I'll allow myself.
- **Promotion vs prevention** — seize the gain vs avoid the loss; the same crisis pulls two partners opposite ways.

**Two good people, one menu, different weights.** The most fertile conflict is not good-vs-evil but **good-vs-good** — two sympathetic characters who weight the *same* menu differently and therefore *cannot both be satisfied*. A loyalty-weighted person and a fairness-weighted person, both decent, tear a family apart over whether to shield a guilty relative. This is the repo's explicit design (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`): the Model is the *weighting*, the conflict is the *menu's built-in tension*, and the decision is *which stake wins for this person when the scene forces it*. Author the divergence in the **weights on the subset the situation activates** — as the son-dies worked example shows (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`), two people identical on surface traits react oppositely because the loss-governing weights (what the son *represented*, the moral construal, the locus of control) were never the same.

**Internal conflict: the drive against itself.** The same map runs *inside* one person. A goal pulls toward the very thing a fear guards against (the drives schema's opposability, `docs/drives-schema.md`): Maelle's goal *free my brother* (serves benevolence + attachment) collides with her fear *be caught and erased* (protects survival) — same person, opposite pulls, the Model resolves. And the value/action gap is its own interior drama: the person who *values* self-direction but *acts* conformist, the one whose *ought self* overrides their *ideal self* and calls it maturity — self-image ≠ true want (`docs/drives-schema.md` `view: self`, the self-deception engine).

**How a scene turns abstract weights into a forced choice.** The abstract weighting is a *prior*; the scene supplies **concrete stakes** — *this* child, *this* danger, *this* promise, *this* public humiliation (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`). The decision *weighs the concrete instantiations under the priors*. Conflict becomes *forced* when the situation puts two menu-items on the table such that one must be sacrificed — the relevance gate surfaces the colliding pair (`docs/relevancy-gate.md`), the Model resolves *narratively, never by a code-side sum* (`docs/decision-engine.md`, `docs/generative-model.md`), and the `{thought}` stream names the pulls it is trading off. **Flip one weight and the same drives resolve the other way** — the coward and the hero feel the identical fear (`docs/generative-model.md`); the divergence is the weighting, and that weighting *is* the character.

**The craft caution.** A conflict is only dramatic if *both* sides are genuinely weighted — a "choice" between something the character cares about and something they don't is not a choice. The forced choice must cost *whichever way it goes*. And the resolution must be *surprising-given-the-lean yet inevitable-given-the-motivation* (the repo's realism test, `docs/generative-model.md`): the timid character's brave act is a caused tail-sample where a Level-2/3 value overrode the trait. Load the menu with real opposing weights, let the scene force the collision, and the character-defining moment falls out — caused, not authored.

## How to reach into this file

Start from what the moment needs, not from a favourite taxonomy:

- **Authoring a character's core worth-weighting (the "model")** → **Schwartz's values** (repo spine, `docs/values-and-stakes.md`) as the coordinate system — two axes for a whole worldview, the 10 for most characters, the **19** for principals. Add **Moral Foundations** (repo ethics lens) for the loyalty/authority/sanctity/liberty weights Schwartz under-carves. Weight the *priority under conflict*, not the flat (socially-desirable) profile.
- **The needs substrate under the values** → **Maslow's floor** (physiological/safety) + **SDT's three** (autonomy/competence/relatedness) — the repo default. Reach for **ERG** when you need *frustration-regression* (why a blocked character over-pursues a substitute).
- **A compact motivational signature** → **McClelland** (achievement / affiliation / personalized-vs-socialized power — the sharpest single villain-vs-statesman dial) or, for breadth, **Reiss's 16 desires** (the empirical menu closest to the repo's profile form, with the useful *satiation* mechanic).
- **A specific hunger the big theories skip** → **Murray's psychogenic needs** (succorance, abasement, infavoidance, counteraction) — a vocabulary, one texture at a time.
- **The *quality* and *emotional signature* of goal-pursuit** → **SDT's OIT continuum** (coerced → introjected/guilty → identified → intrinsic — the same act with a different soul) and **Regulatory Focus** (promotion/eager/joy-or-dejection vs prevention/vigilant/relief-or-anxiety).
- **The flavour of striving — and *why a talented character stalls*** → **Achievement Goal Theory** (mastery vs performance × approach vs avoidance) + **Dweck's fixed/growth mindset**: performance-avoidance + a fixed mindset = the self-handicapping, effort-withdrawing paralysed prodigy; fear-of-failure is a ready-made drives-schema fear+defense.
- **The developmental / reasoning axis of morality (a moral *voice*, not a taste-weighting)** → **Kohlberg's stages** (self-interest → duty/law → principle), **Gilligan's justice-vs-care**, **Turiel's moral-vs-conventional** line — the rationalist tradition MFT reacted against; use for a character who *deliberates* up the ladder rather than only feeling the verdict.
- **A ready-made fear→desire→defense core for a fast, coherent character** → the **Enneagram** — as a *craft scaffold only*, converted into a weighted fear+desire+defense triple, never as a validity claim or a box.
- **The highest-stakes motive (what they'll trade survival for)** → **Frankl's will to meaning** and **TMT** (worldview-defence under mortality) — self-transcendence, legacy, martyrdom, the empty-summit crisis.
- **Separating ends from means** → **Rokeach's terminal/instrumental** split — maps straight onto the drives schema's terminal/instrumental goal chain (`docs/drives-schema.md`).
- **Building the drama** → the **value-conflict section above**. Load the menu with real *opposing* weights (the maps are pre-drawn — Schwartz's axes, Maslow's floor, SDT's autonomy-vs-relatedness, MFT's care-vs-loyalty), let the scene instantiate concrete stakes that force one to be sacrificed, and resolve *narratively*. Two good people who weight the same menu differently is the richest engine in the well.
