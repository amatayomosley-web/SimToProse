# The Shadow — Clinical, Dark and Wounded

The darker and wounded registers: the frameworks you reach for when a character is a villain, an antihero, a predator, or a person the world has broken. Where `trait-models.md` maps the *normal-range* dark region — the low-Honesty-Humility corner, the subclinical Dark Triad/Tetrad, the D core — this file goes one floor down, into **clinical pathology, trauma, and self-deception**: the DSM-5 personality-disorder categories and their signatures, the dimensional alternative (the maladaptive PID-5 trait domains), the deep structure of narcissism and psychopathy, the body's trauma responses (fight/flight/freeze/fawn) and the PTSD/C-PTSD wound-signatures they harden into, and the machinery of self-deception that lets every villain be the hero of his own story. The repo's spine already contains most of what you need to *build* these people without a bolt-on system: villains are the **low-H trait region plus a named overlay** (`docs/trait-theory.md`), cruelty is **RAGE unchecked by CARE** and cowardice is **FEAR winning its collision** (`docs/generative-model.md`), the wound → fear → defense chain is a first-class Layer-3 structure (`docs/drives-schema.md`, `defense: withdraw|control|attack|please|perform`), and self-deception is the **`view: self` split** where a truth-fear is absent from self-image (`docs/self-and-perception.md`, `docs/drives-schema.md`). Those are the reference defaults. This file is the clinical well they draw on — richer vocabulary for the shadow, and a standing warning that clinical labels are the *cliché trap*, not the craft. A diagnosis is a checklist an institution uses to bill; a character is a caused, particular person. The frameworks below are lenses for building *behaviour with provenance*, never costumes to hang on "the crazy one." Read them that way, and read the ethics section before you write a single symptom.

## DSM-5 categorical personality disorders — the ten named patterns, three clusters

**Lineage.** The *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders*, American Psychiatric Association — the categorical model runs from DSM-III (1980, which put personality disorders on their own "Axis II") through DSM-IV (1994) to **DSM-5 (2013)** and **DSM-5-TR (2022)**, which retained the ten categories essentially unchanged in the main text while relegating a dimensional alternative to Section III (below). The ICD-11 (WHO, 2022) broke sharply the other way, abolishing the categories entirely for a severity-plus-traits dimensional scheme.

**Core idea.** A personality disorder is an *enduring, inflexible, pervasive pattern* of inner experience and behaviour that deviates markedly from cultural expectation, is stable across time and situations, begins by adolescence/early adulthood, and causes distress or impairment. The key word is **pervasive**: not a state, not a bad week, but the person's default operating system across contexts. DSM-5 sorts the ten into three clusters by surface resemblance — a crude but memorable grouping ("odd, dramatic, anxious").

**Structure in full.**

**Cluster A — the odd / eccentric cluster** (share a withdrawn, mistrustful, reality-loosened quality; genetically adjacent to the schizophrenia spectrum):

- **Paranoid PD** — a pervasive distrust and suspiciousness; reads benign acts as demeaning or threatening, doubts loyalty, bears grudges, suspects infidelity without grounds, is preoccupied with hidden malevolent motives. *The person for whom everyone is an angle.*
- **Schizoid PD** — detachment from social relationships and a *restricted* emotional range; a genuine loner who neither desires nor enjoys close relationships, takes pleasure in few activities, appears cold and flat. Not shy (that is avoidant) — *indifferent*. The lighthouse keeper who prefers the lighthouse.
- **Schizotypal PD** — acute social discomfort *plus* cognitive-perceptual distortions and eccentricity: odd beliefs and magical thinking, ideas of reference, unusual perceptual experiences, odd speech and affect, paranoid ideation. The eerie recluse whose inner world has slipped its moorings. (Sits on the schizophrenia spectrum.)

**Cluster B — the dramatic / emotional / erratic cluster** (share emotional volatility, impulsivity, and interpersonal turbulence; the *antagonist cluster* of fiction):

- **Antisocial PD (ASPD)** — pervasive disregard for and violation of others' rights since age 15: deceit, impulsivity, aggression, reckless disregard for safety, irresponsibility, *lack of remorse*. The behavioural, criminological cousin of psychopathy (they are **not** identical — see the psychopathy section). Diagnosable only in adults, with childhood conduct-disorder evidence.
- **Borderline PD (BPD)** — pervasive instability of relationships, self-image, and affect, plus marked impulsivity: frantic efforts to avoid abandonment, idealisation↔devaluation ("splitting"), identity disturbance, self-damaging impulsivity, recurrent suicidal/self-harm behaviour, chronic emptiness, intense inappropriate anger, transient stress-related paranoia or dissociation. The most *interior* and most sympathetic of the cluster — usually rooted in trauma; it overlaps heavily with C-PTSD.
- **Histrionic PD** — excessive emotionality and attention-seeking: uncomfortable when not the centre of attention, seductive/provocative, shifting shallow emotions, uses appearance to draw notice, theatrical, suggestible, reads relationships as more intimate than they are. The person who performs feeling rather than has it.
- **Narcissistic PD (NPD)** — grandiosity (in fantasy or behaviour), need for admiration, lack of empathy: sense of superiority and specialness, entitlement, exploitativeness, envy, arrogance, fragility beneath (see the narcissism section). The self-worshipper whose worship is load-bearing.

**Cluster C — the anxious / fearful cluster** (share a driving fear — of judgement, of abandonment, of chaos):

- **Avoidant PD** — pervasive social inhibition, feelings of inadequacy, hypersensitivity to negative evaluation: *wants* connection but avoids it for fear of shame/rejection, feels inept, restrained in intimacy. Distinguished from schizoid by the *longing* — avoidant hurts to be alone, schizoid does not care.
- **Dependent PD** — a pervasive, excessive need to be taken care of, leading to submissive clinging and fear of separation: cannot make ordinary decisions without reassurance, needs others to assume responsibility, goes to great lengths for support, feels helpless alone, urgently seeks a new relationship when one ends.
- **Obsessive-Compulsive PD (OCPD)** — preoccupation with orderliness, perfectionism, and control at the expense of flexibility and openness: rules and lists over the point of the activity, perfectionism that prevents completion, workaholism, over-conscientiousness, rigidity, miserliness, inability to delegate or discard. *Distinct from OCD* (which involves ego-dystonic obsessions/compulsions the person wants to be rid of); OCPD is ego-syntonic — the person thinks they are simply *right*.

**Vivid exemplars.** Paranoid: Captain Queeg (*The Caine Mutiny*), Richard Nixon as dramatized. Schizoid: Bartleby, Meursault (*The Stranger*), Lisbeth Salander (with trauma overlay). Schizotypal: Luna Lovegood dialled dark, the eerie hermit-seer. Antisocial: Alex (*A Clockwork Orange*), Tom Ripley (with narcissism). Borderline: Clementine (*Eternal Sunshine*), often read onto Anakin Skywalker's abandonment terror; Susanna (*Girl, Interrupted*). Histrionic: Blanche DuBois (*Streetcar*), Scarlett O'Hara. Narcissistic: Jay Gatsby (aspirational form), Miranda Priestly, Jordan Belfort. Avoidant: Prufrock, Fanny Price. Dependent: Of Human Bondage's Philip toward Mildred, the perpetually-rescued spouse. OCPD: Sherlock Holmes (functional), Melvin Udall (*As Good as It Gets*), Javert (rigid moral order over mercy — the tragic OCPD structure).

**What it gives you for character depth.** A **named library of coherent extremes** — ten internally-consistent ways a personality can organise itself rigidly around one fear or one need, each with a full behavioural signature you can render. Used as *silhouettes and pressure-points* (not diagnoses) they give an antagonist or a broken protagonist a spine: the paranoid's grudge-ledger, the borderline's idealise-then-devalue oscillation, the avoidant's ache-and-flinch, Javert's OCPD collapse when mercy breaks his rulebook. The cluster grouping is a fast triage — *odd, dramatic, or anxious?* — that points you at the register.

**Combines-with.** Every category **projects onto the HEXACO trait space** the repo commits to (`docs/trait-theory.md`) — it is the *extreme, rigid, pervasive tail* of a trait profile, which is exactly why the Alternative Model (next) reframes them as trait combinations. Cluster A ≈ high Detachment / low Extraversion (+ Psychoticism for schizotypal); Cluster B ≈ low Honesty-Humility + low Agreeableness + high Negative-Affectivity/Disinhibition (the low-H region of `trait-models.md`); Cluster C ≈ high Emotionality/Neuroticism (+ low X for avoidant). Each maps to the drives schema's **wound → fear → defense** chain (`docs/drives-schema.md`): the disorder *is* one defense pattern run rigidly (paranoid = `attack`/vigilance; dependent/histrionic = `please`/`perform`; OCPD = `control`; avoidant/schizoid = `withdraw`). And each biases the Panksepp basis (`docs/generative-model.md`): paranoid = chronic FEAR+RAGE, schizoid = flattened CARE/PLAY, borderline = PANIC-GRIEF (abandonment) whiplashing with RAGE.

**Cautions.** (1) **The diagnosis-as-character cliché is the cardinal sin of this whole file.** A category is a *statistical family resemblance an insurer recognises*, not a person; writing "she has BPD" as characterisation produces a symptom-list in a wig. Build the caused particular (this wound, this fear, this defense) and let a reader-observer *maybe* recognise a pattern — never start from the label. (2) **Comorbidity and hybridity are the rule** — real people meeting criteria for one PD usually meet partial criteria for several; the pure-type is a textbook fiction. (3) The categories have **poor discriminant validity and arbitrary thresholds** (why *five* of nine criteria?) — the field's own dissatisfaction is what produced the Alternative Model. (4) **Cluster B is over-represented in fiction and under-represented as suffering** — BPD especially is a trauma-driven agony written far more often as "the crazy ex" than as the person drowning. (5) OCPD≠OCD, ASPD≠psychopathy, schizoid≠schizophrenia≠avoidant — the near-homonyms are constantly conflated. (6) Ego-syntonic vs ego-dystonic matters for interiority: the OCPD/narcissist thinks they are *correct* (no inner conflict to render), the avoidant/dependent *suffers their pattern* (rich inner conflict).

**Sources.** APA, *DSM-5-TR* (2022), Section II Personality Disorders; Millon, *Disorders of Personality* (3rd ed., 2011); Livesley (ed.), *Handbook of Personality Disorders* (2018); Paris, *A Concise Guide to Personality Disorders* (2015).

## The Alternative DSM-5 Model (AMPD) and PID-5 — pathology as maladaptive trait dimensions

**Lineage.** Developed for DSM-5 by the Personality and Personality Disorders Work Group and placed in **Section III** ("Emerging Measures and Models," 2013) as an alternative to the categorical system it could not politically replace. The trait side (Criterion B) is measured by the **PID-5** (Personality Inventory for DSM-5; Krueger, Derringer, Markon, Watson & Skodol, 2012). The ICD-11 (2022) adopted a very similar dimensional trait scheme, making this the direction of official travel.

**Core idea.** Personality pathology is not ten discrete illnesses but **the maladaptive extreme of the same continuous trait dimensions everyone has** — the Big Five/HEXACO turned up to dysfunction. A diagnosis has two parts: **Criterion A**, the *severity* of impairment in personality functioning (how broken the self and relationships are), and **Criterion B**, the *style* of that impairment (which maladaptive traits). Severity × style — how bad, and what flavour. This is the model most *congenial to the repo*, because it says exactly what `docs/trait-theory.md` says: villains and the disordered are a *region of the ordinary trait space*, not a separate species.

**Structure in full.**

**Criterion A — the Level of Personality Functioning Scale (LPFS).** Impairment, rated 0 (none) to 4 (extreme), across four capacities in two spheres:
- *Self:* **Identity** (a coherent, bounded, stable sense of self; regulated self-esteem) and **Self-direction** (coherent goals, prosocial internal standards, capacity to reflect productively).
- *Interpersonal:* **Empathy** (comprehending others' experiences and their impact; tolerating differing perspectives) and **Intimacy** (depth and duration of connection; desire and capacity for closeness; mutuality). Moderate impairment (≈ level 2) is the threshold for a PD. *This is the severity dial — the same four capacities the repo tracks as identity/self-image (Layer 3/9), goals (Layer 3), and relationship capacity (`docs/relationships.md`).*

**Criterion B — five maladaptive trait domains, 25 facets** (each domain is the pathological pole of an FFM/HEXACO dimension):

- **Negative Affectivity** (≈ maladaptive high Neuroticism / HEXACO Emotionality): frequent intense negative emotions. Facets: *Emotional Lability, Anxiousness, Separation Insecurity* (primary) + *Submissiveness, Hostility, Perseveration, Restricted Affectivity* (the last as its **low** pole — its absence loads here).
- **Detachment** (≈ maladaptive low Extraversion): withdrawal from and avoidance of others and of experience. Facets: *Withdrawal, Anhedonia, Intimacy Avoidance* (primary) + *Depressivity, Restricted Affectivity, Suspiciousness* (cross-loaders).
- **Antagonism** (≈ maladaptive low Agreeableness + low Honesty-Humility — **the villain domain**): putting oneself against others. Facets: *Manipulativeness, Deceitfulness, Grandiosity, Attention Seeking, Callousness* (primary) + *Hostility* (cross-loads from Negative Affectivity).
- **Disinhibition** (≈ maladaptive low Conscientiousness): impulsive pursuit of immediate reward, heedless of consequence. Facets: *Irresponsibility, Impulsivity, Distractibility, Risk Taking* (primary) + *Rigid Perfectionism* as the **opposite** pole (compulsivity — its high end anchors OCPD).
- **Psychoticism** (≈ maladaptive high Openness): oddity, eccentricity, reality-loosening. Facets: *Unusual Beliefs & Experiences, Eccentricity, Cognitive & Perceptual Dysregulation*.

**How the categories re-express as trait profiles** (the model's payoff — each old PD becomes a required-domain recipe): Antisocial ≈ Antagonism + Disinhibition; Borderline ≈ Negative Affectivity + Disinhibition + Antagonism (emotional lability, anxiousness, separation insecurity, impulsivity, risk-taking, hostility); Narcissistic ≈ Antagonism (grandiosity + attention-seeking); Avoidant ≈ Detachment + Negative Affectivity (withdrawal, anhedonia, intimacy-avoidance, anxiousness); Obsessive-Compulsive ≈ rigid-perfectionism + perseveration + restricted-affectivity; Schizotypal ≈ Psychoticism + Detachment.

**Vivid exemplars.** The model shines at *odd hybrids the ten categories can't name*: a character who is high-Antagonism (cold, grandiose, manipulative) but *low*-Disinhibition (patient, controlled) is the composed schemer (Littlefinger) — neither ASPD nor NPD cleanly, but a precise trait silhouette. High-Detachment + high-Psychoticism = the eerie seer. High-Negative-Affectivity + high-Antagonism = the wounded lasher-out (borderline rage). You author a *dial-profile*, not a diagnosis.

**What it gives you for character depth.** A **continuous, combinable, repo-native** way to write pathology: severity (Criterion A) and style (Criterion B) as separate knobs, so you can write a *mildly* impaired grandiose narcissist and a *severely* impaired one who differ in degree, not kind. Because the five domains are just the maladaptive poles of the HEXACO the repo already stores as **(mean, variability)** (`docs/trait-theory.md`), a disordered character is *the same data structure with extreme means and often low variability* — pathology as **rigidity** (a narrow, situation-insensitive distribution), which is precisely DSM's "inflexible and pervasive." That reframing — disorder = extreme mean + collapsed variability — is the single most useful idea in this file for the engine.

**Combines-with.** It **is** the bridge between the whole categorical section above and the trait spine of `trait-models.md`: Antagonism = the low-H/Dark-Triad region made clinical; Negative Affectivity = HEXACO Emotionality's pathological tail; Detachment = low-X's. Criterion A maps onto the repo's Layer-3 identity/self-direction and `docs/relationships.md` intimacy/empathy capacity. Rigid Perfectionism and Restricted Affectivity as *reversed poles* is exactly the repo's bipolar-continuum storage. Psychoticism connects to the Openness facet *Unconventionality* pushed to reality-distortion.

**Cautions.** (1) Psychoticism as "maladaptive Openness" is the **weakest link** — the mapping is contested; oddity of belief is not obviously the same axis as aesthetic curiosity. (2) The trait profile captures *style* but under-weights the *developmental story* — a PID-5 vector tells you a borderline pattern but not the childhood that built it; pair it with the trauma section for provenance. (3) It is still a clinician's instrument — do not narrate a character's PID-5 scores; use the domains to *shape behaviour*, then throw the scaffolding away. (4) Same cliché risk as the categories if used as a label rather than a mechanism.

**Sources.** APA, *DSM-5-TR* (2022) Section III; Krueger et al., "Initial construction of a maladaptive personality trait model and inventory for DSM-5" (*Psychological Medicine*, 2012); Widiger & Crego, "The Alternative DSM-5 Model" (*World Psychiatry*, 2019); Sharp et al., AMPD validity review (*World Psychiatry*, 2025); Bach & First, on ICD-11 trait convergence.

## The Dark Factor of personality (D) — the aversive core (repo cross-link)

**Lineage & core idea.** Moshagen, Hilbig & Zettler (2018): beneath every dark trait sits one general disposition, **D** — *the tendency to maximise one's own utility at the expense of others, accompanied by beliefs that serve as justifications.* Egoism, Machiavellianism, moral disengagement, narcissism, entitlement, psychopathy, sadism, self-interest, and spitefulness are its facets. **This is already covered in full in `trait-models.md`** (subclinical, trait-level) and is the theoretical warrant for the repo's design — the dark region has a *centre*, and any villain sits on a continuum with ordinary self-interest. The clinical relevance here: D's defining second clause — *"accompanied by beliefs that serve as justifications"* — is the **self-deception engine** (last section). Malevolence is not experienced by its bearer as malevolence; it comes pre-wrapped in justification. That built-in rationalisation is what separates the psychologically real villain from the moustache-twirler, and it is why the self-deception section is the most important craft content in this file.

**Combines-with / cross-link.** See `trait-models.md` (Dark Triad/Tetrad + D core) for the structure; `docs/trait-theory.md` for the low-H region and named overlays; the *justification* clause connects to Bandura's moral disengagement and the `view: self` split (below).

**Sources.** Moshagen, Hilbig & Zettler, "The dark core of personality" (*Psychological Review*, 2018); Moshagen et al., "Measuring the dark core" (*Psychological Assessment*, 2020).

## Narcissism in depth — the grandiose mask and the wound beneath

**Lineage.** From Freud ("On Narcissism," 1914) through the two great post-war clinical rivals — **Heinz Kohut** (self-psychology: narcissism as an *arrested developmental need* for mirroring, healable through empathy) and **Otto Kernberg** (object-relations: a *pathological grandiose self* defending against primitive envy and rage, requiring confrontation) — to the DSM's NPD and the modern research split into **grandiose vs vulnerable** narcissism (Pincus & Lukowitsky, 2010; Miller & Campbell). Theodore Millon supplied clinical subtypes.

**Core idea.** Narcissism is a *regulation disorder of self-esteem*: the self cannot maintain worth from within, so it is propped from outside — by admiration, superiority, control — and it swings between grandiosity and collapse. The grandiosity is not the problem; it is the **defense**. Beneath it is a self that experienced worth as conditional (on performance, on being special, on a parent's need) and never internalised a stable, unconditional sense of being enough. Every narcissistic behaviour is self-esteem *maintenance* — which is why the injury (a slight, a failure, exposure) produces such disproportionate rage: it is not vanity bruised, it is the load-bearing wall cracking.

**Structure in full.**

- **Grandiose narcissism** (the "thick-skinned," overt form): overt superiority, exhibitionism, charm, boldness, dominance, low anxiety, aggression when challenged, exploitativeness worn openly. High-agency, high-Extraversion, low-Agreeableness. Reads as *confident and magnetic* until crossed. — the bold predator-charmer.
- **Vulnerable narcissism** (the "thin-skinned," covert form): hypersensitivity, shame-proneness, resentful envy, defensiveness, a *quiet* conviction of specialness paired with chronic feeling that the world fails to recognise it; anxious, withdrawn, aggrieved. High-Neuroticism. Reads as *the perpetually slighted, self-pitying, secretly superior* — the martyr who is always owed. The two forms oscillate within one person and are unified by the shared **entitlement + fragile self-worth** core.
- **The narcissistic injury / rage** — when admiration is withdrawn or grandiosity is punctured, the collapse is met with rage (attack the source), devaluation (they were worthless anyway), or shame-flight. The bully who cannot survive being laughed at.
- **The false self** (Winnicott) — the polished persona built to earn love the true self never got; over time the person may lose contact with the true self entirely, which is the hollowness beneath the shine.
- **Kohut vs Kernberg (the writerly choice):** Kohut's narcissist is *woundable and reachable* (the arc toward being truly seen) — write this one for tragedy and possible redemption. Kernberg's is *organised around envy and armoured* (grandiosity defending against wanting-to-destroy-what-it-cannot-have) — write this one for the cold, un-healable antagonist.
- **Millon subtypes** (flavour palette): *elitist* (self-styled superior), *amorous* (seductive Don Juan), *unprincipled* (narcissist + antisocial = con artist), *compensatory* (vulnerable, building grandiosity over deep inferiority).
- **Malignant narcissism** (Fromm coined it, *The Heart of Man*, 1964; Kernberg gave it clinical shape, 1984) — the most dangerous narcissistic form and the **marquee tyrant / dark-lord profile**: a core narcissistic personality **fused with** antisocial behaviour, a **paranoid orientation** (the world is conspiring to take what is rightfully his), and **ego-syntonic sadism/aggression** (cruelty experienced as justified, even righteous — no inner conflict about it). Fromm, a survivor of the Nazi era, called it "the quintessence of evil" and reached for it to explain Hitler and Stalin. It sits *between* NPD and psychopathy and unifies four of this file's threads at once: unlike the ordinary narcissist (who wants to be *admired*), the malignant narcissist is *willing to be feared*; unlike the pure psychopath (harm from the mere *absence* of brakes), he is driven by grandiosity and grievance and *believes in* his cruelty. Not a DSM category (a hypothetical/experimental construct), but the single most useful lens for the despot, the cult-leader, the domestic tyrant — grandiose, paranoid, remorseless, and cruel *on principle*.

**Vivid exemplars.** Grandiose: Gaston (*Beauty and the Beast*), Jordan Belfort, Miranda Priestly. Vulnerable: Gollum (grievance, self-pity, thwarted specialness), Salieri (*Amadeus* — the connoisseur of his own mediocrity, poisoned by envy = Kernbergian narcissism made tragic), Uriah Heep (covert, "'umble"-masking resentment). The injury: Jay Gatsby's whole edifice collapsing when Daisy will not rewrite the past; Anakin/Vader's turn seeded in the terror of not being special enough to save what he loves. Malignant: Commodus (*Gladiator*) tipping grandiosity into paranoid fratricidal cruelty, Homelander (*The Boys* — grandiose, paranoid, sadistic, remorseless), Immortan Joe, the personified dark-lord tyrant whose cruelty is *doctrine*.

**What it gives you for character depth.** The single most *dramatically productive* pathology, because it is **an engine with a fault line**: grandiosity you can puncture, a wound you can reach, an oscillation you can stage. It resolves the flat-villain problem — the narcissist is frightening *and* pitiable, monstrous on the surface and starving underneath, which is the exact texture that makes an antagonist unforgettable (Salieri, Vader). The grandiose/vulnerable split gives you two opposite silhouettes from one core, and the Kohut/Kernberg fork lets you *choose the arc* (reachable → redemption; armoured → tragedy or defeat).

**Combines-with.** NPD = the **Antagonism domain** (grandiosity + attention-seeking) over impaired **identity** (Criterion A self-esteem dysregulation). In HEXACO: low H (entitlement, exploitation) + variable E (grandiose high-X vs vulnerable low-X) + low A. Grandiose ≈ the bold Dark-Triad narcissism of `trait-models.md`; vulnerable adds high Emotionality/Neuroticism. The injury→rage is `docs/generative-model.md`'s **RAGE**, triggered by a **severity-computed** threat to *self-image* (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`: self-image is a stake; puncturing it is high-severity *for this person*). The wound (conditional love) is a Layer-6 backstory generating a Layer-3 fear ("being ordinary / unseen / exposed"), `defense: perform | control | attack`. The false-self ↔ true-self gap is the **`view: self` self-deception** (`docs/self-and-perception.md`). **Malignant narcissism is the composite** — NPD's grandiosity + the **psychopathy** section's antagonistic core + the **paranoid PD** silhouette + the sadist's pleasure-in-harm (`trait-models.md`): four overlays stacked on one low-H spine, which is why it reads as the most complete portrait of a tyrant this file offers.

**Cautions.** (1) **"Narcissist" is the internet's most abused clinical word** — do not write the term into a character's mouth as an easy villain-stamp; write the *regulation-of-self-worth* mechanism instead. (2) Grandiose and vulnerable read *oppositely* (magnetic-bold vs aggrieved-withdrawn) — pick the dominant form; conflating them muddies the silhouette. (3) The competence halo again: fictional narcissists are often hyper-capable, but the disorder frequently *undermines* achievement (the grandiosity outruns the substance, the injury sabotages). (4) A pinch of narcissism is *normal and often adaptive* (leaders, performers, anyone who survives rejection) — reserve the pathological register for where the fault line actually cracks the person's life.

**Sources.** Kohut, *The Analysis of the Self* (1971); Kernberg, *Borderline Conditions and Pathological Narcissism* (1975) and *Severe Personality Disorders* (1984, malignant narcissism); Fromm, *The Heart of Man* (1964, coining "malignant narcissism"); Pincus & Lukowitsky, "Pathological narcissism and narcissistic personality disorder" (*Annual Review of Clinical Psychology*, 2010); Miller, Lynam, Campbell et al., grandiose/vulnerable research; Ronningstam, *Identifying and Understanding the Narcissistic Personality* (2005).

## Psychopathy in depth — the cold predator, the mask, and the missing fear

**Lineage.** **Hervey Cleckley**, *The Mask of Sanity* (1941) — the founding clinical portrait: the psychopath as an outwardly charming, intelligent, "sane"-seeming person hollowed of the affective machinery that makes conscience possible. **Robert Hare** operationalised it as the **PCL-R** (Psychopathy Checklist-Revised, 1991/2003), the forensic standard. **Benjamin Karpman** (1941) split *primary* from *secondary* psychopathy. **Christopher Patrick, Don Fowles & Robert Krueger** (2009) proposed the **Triarchic Model**. Note throughout: **psychopathy ≠ ASPD** — ASPD (DSM) is a *behavioural/criminal* diagnosis; psychopathy is a *personality* construct centred on the affective-interpersonal core ASPD largely misses.

**Core idea.** Psychopathy is a *deficit of the affective substrate of conscience* — shallow emotion, absent fear and anxiety, no genuine empathy or remorse — wrapped in a functional, often charming social surface (the "mask of sanity"). The predator is not driven by rage (that is another engine) but by *the absence of the brakes* — guilt, fear of consequence, and fellow-feeling that stop the rest of us. Its darkest signature is instrumentality: harm as a *tool*, executed without the friction of feeling.

**Structure in full.**

**Cleckley's 16 criteria** (abridged to the load-bearing): superficial charm and good intelligence; absence of delusions or nervousness; unreliability; untruthfulness and insincerity; lack of remorse or shame; inadequately motivated antisocial behaviour; poor judgement and failure to learn from experience; pathological egocentricity and incapacity for love; general poverty of major affective reactions; specific loss of insight; unresponsiveness in interpersonal relations; fantastic behaviour with/without drink; suicide rarely carried out; impersonal, trivial, poorly integrated sex life; failure to follow any life plan.

**Hare PCL-R — two factors, four facets** (the standard structure):
- **Factor 1 — Interpersonal/Affective** (the *core*, most distinctly psychopathic): *Interpersonal facet* — glibness/superficial charm, grandiose self-worth, pathological lying, conning/manipulativeness. *Affective facet* — shallow affect, callousness/lack of empathy, lack of remorse/guilt, failure to accept responsibility.
- **Factor 2 — Lifestyle/Antisocial** (the *behavioural*, overlaps ASPD): *Lifestyle facet* — need for stimulation/proneness to boredom, parasitic lifestyle, impulsivity, irresponsibility, lack of realistic goals. *Antisocial facet* — poor behavioural controls, early behaviour problems, juvenile delinquency, revocation of conditional release, criminal versatility.

**Primary vs secondary psychopathy** (Karpman — the crucial writerly fork):
- **Primary** — the *constitutional* form: low-fear, low-anxiety, cold, calm, calculated; the emotional deficit is inborn. The genuinely fearless predator (Chigurh). Maps to Factor 1.
- **Secondary** — the *acquired/reactive* form: anxious, impulsive, emotionally turbulent, often trauma-forged; aggression driven by distress and dysregulation rather than cold calculation. Closer to BPD/C-PTSD in flavour. Maps to Factor 2 + high Negative Affectivity. *This is the psychopath a childhood built* — the one with a wound.

**Triarchic Model** (Patrick et al. — three dispositional building blocks, dial independently):
- **Boldness** — fearless dominance, stress-immunity, social poise, venturesomeness, low anxiety. (The adaptive-looking face; overlaps "successful psychopathy," the fearless leader/surgeon/operator.)
- **Meanness** — callousness, cruelty, empathy-deficit, exploitativeness, contempt, active aggression. (The malevolent core.)
- **Disinhibition** — impulsivity, poor regulation, hostility, irresponsibility, difficulty delaying gratification. (The out-of-control face.)
Different psychopaths are different *blends*: bold+mean, low-disinhibited = the composed predator; mean+disinhibited, low-bold = the chaotic violent offender.

**The low-fear / amygdala account** (Lykken's low-fear hypothesis; James Blair's Violence Inhibition Mechanism / amygdala-dysfunction model): the primary psychopath's defining deficit is *impaired threat and distress processing* — they do not flinch at others' fear or at their own risk, so the aversive signals that build conscience and caution never land. **This is the cleanest repo mapping: psychopathy = chronically low Panksepp FEAR** (`docs/generative-model.md`), which is why the reckless-vs-brave distinction there matters — "a person with no fear at all is reckless, not brave (nothing registered to override)."

**Vivid exemplars.** Primary/cold: Anton Chigurh (fearless, instrumental, mask absent — pure Factor 1 + meanness), Hannibal Lecter (charm + immense control + sadism), Tom Ripley (charm, lying, no remorse, instrumental murder). Secondary/reactive: many trauma-forged killers, the volatile abused-into-predator. Boldness-heavy "successful": the ruthless CEO, the fearless field operative. The mask itself: Patrick Bateman's performed normalcy over an absence, Norman Bates' pleasant surface. The Joker is *not* clean psychopathy — the chaotic-nihilist variant, more disinhibition+meanness than cold Factor-1 calculation.

**What it gives you for character depth.** A precise vocabulary for *the villain without a hot motive* — harm as tool, not as passion — and the mechanisms to make it *frightening rather than cartoonish*: the **mask** (charm as camouflage, the horror of the ordinary surface), the **instrumentality** (feeling absent where the reader expects it — the flatness *is* the dread), and the **missing fear** (the character who does not flinch where anyone would). The primary/secondary fork is huge: it separates the un-reachable constitutional cold (no wound to heal, so no redemption — write the implacable force) from the trauma-forged reactive predator (a wound, a possible arc, a tragedy). Boldness lets you write the *functional* psychopath (surgeon, general, executive) who never sees a courtroom.

**Combines-with.** The affective core = **low Honesty-Humility + low Agreeableness + low Emotionality/FEAR** (`docs/trait-theory.md`, `trait-models.md` psychopathy mapping) — the fearlessness is the key clinical addition the trait file flags. Meanness = **RAGE/dominance uncoupled from CARE**, or more precisely *CARE absent so harm meets no internal resistance* (`docs/generative-model.md`: "cruelty = RAGE unchecked by CARE"; the psychopath adds *no fear-brake* either). Disinhibition = the PID-5 Disinhibition domain / low-C. Secondary psychopathy overlaps the **trauma section** (a wound-forged coldness) and BPD. Instrumentality vs the sadist's *pleasure* in pain is the same distinction `trait-models.md` draws: the psychopath hurts you to get something; the sadist because hurting is the point.

**Cautions.** (1) **Psychopathy ≠ ASPD ≠ "psychotic."** ASPD is behavioural (many prisoners qualify, few are true psychopaths); "psychotic" is a *different axis entirely* (loss of reality contact) constantly and wrongly used as a synonym — a psychopath is typically *perfectly reality-oriented*, that is the whole "mask of sanity." (2) **Most psychopaths are not serial killers** — they are over-represented among the reckless, the fraudulent, the exploitative, and (per "successful psychopathy" research) among some functional high-status operators; the fictional equation psychopath=slasher is a genre distortion. (3) The **charm is the tell you must actually write** — a snarling brute is not a psychopath; the terror is the pleasant surface, so render the mask, not the monster. (4) The genius-cannibal (Lecter) is a *fantasy* archetype; real psychopathy correlates with *poor* long-term judgement and failure-to-learn — the hyper-competent mastermind is a convention. (5) **Boldness is contested** — some argue it dilutes the construct toward "fearless but not malevolent"; a bold-only character is a daredevil, not a predator (meanness is the malevolent ingredient).

**Sources.** Cleckley, *The Mask of Sanity* (1941); Hare, *Without Conscience* (1993) and the PCL-R Manual (2003); Patrick, Fowles & Krueger, "Triarchic conceptualization of psychopathy" (*Development and Psychopathology*, 2009); Lykken, *The Antisocial Personalities* (1995); Blair, *The Psychopath: Emotion and the Brain* (2005); Karpman, "The myth of the psychopathic personality" (1941).

## Trauma and its responses — the body's survival modes and the wound they harden into

**Lineage.** **Walter Cannon** named the **fight-or-flight** response (1915/1929). The **freeze** (tonic immobility, defensive shutdown) was added from animal-defense research. **Pete Walker** coined and popularised **fawn** (*Complex PTSD: From Surviving to Thriving*, 2013). **Stephen Porges'** Polyvagal Theory (1994) supplied a nervous-system hierarchy. PTSD entered DSM-III (1980) from combat and rape research; **Judith Herman** (*Trauma and Recovery*, 1992) proposed **Complex PTSD** for prolonged, repeated, inescapable trauma; the **ICD-11** (2022) made C-PTSD an official diagnosis. **Bessel van der Kolk** (*The Body Keeps the Score*, 2014) popularised the somatic view.

**Core idea.** Under threat the nervous system runs pre-cognitive survival programs; when threat is *overwhelming, prolonged, or inescapable* — especially in childhood, especially at the hands of caregivers — those programs **fail to switch off** and become the person's default operating mode. Trauma is not the event; it is *the nervous system's unfinished response to the event*, carried forward. A traumatised character is not "sad about a bad thing" — they are running an old survival program against a present that no longer requires it, and every symptom is that program misfiring.

**Structure in full.**

**The four (five) trauma responses** — the survival repertoire (and its map onto the repo's `defense` field):
- **Fight** — mobilise aggression to repel the threat: rage, control, confrontation, domination. *Hardens into:* explosive anger, bullying, need for control, hypervigilant hostility. (≈ `defense: attack | control`.)
- **Flight** — escape: physical fleeing, and chronically, *anxiety, workaholism, perfectionism, busyness* as flight-from-feeling. *Hardens into:* panic, avoidance, obsessive doing, inability to be still. (≈ `defense: withdraw`.)
- **Freeze** — when neither fight nor flight is possible: immobility, dissociation, numbing, "playing dead," the mind leaving the body. *Hardens into:* dissociation, emotional shutdown, derealisation, procrastination-paralysis, the deer-in-headlights. (The one the repo's five-defense list under-covers — *freeze/dissociate* is a distinct output worth adding.)
- **Fawn** (Walker) — when the threat is a caregiver you cannot escape: *appease, please, merge with the aggressor's wishes, abandon your own needs to stay safe.* "Fawn types seek safety by merging with the wishes, needs, and demands of others." *Hardens into:* compulsive people-pleasing, no boundaries, codependency, loss of self, service as survival. (≈ `defense: please`.)
- **Flop / collapse** (sometimes added) — total submission, going limp, the dorsal-vagal shutdown extreme.

**Polyvagal hierarchy** (Porges — a nervous-system ladder; *note: physiologically contested*, use as evocative frame): **ventral vagal** (safe, social, connected — the green zone) → drop to **sympathetic** (mobilised: fight/flight — the red zone) → drop further to **dorsal vagal** (immobilised: freeze/collapse/dissociation/shutdown — the shutdown zone). Threat walks a person *down* the ladder; safety walks them back up. Useful for writing the *tempo* of a stress response and the felt difference between agitation (sympathetic) and numb collapse (dorsal).

**PTSD (DSM-5) — four symptom clusters** (single-incident or discrete trauma):
1. **Intrusion** — involuntary re-experiencing: flashbacks, nightmares, intrusive memories, physiological reactivity to cues.
2. **Avoidance** — of trauma-associated thoughts, feelings, people, places, reminders.
3. **Negative alterations in cognitions and mood** — persistent negative beliefs ("the world is dangerous, I am bad"), distorted blame, detachment, anhedonia, inability to feel positive emotion, amnesia for parts of the event.
4. **Alterations in arousal and reactivity** — hypervigilance, exaggerated startle, irritability/aggression, recklessness, concentration and sleep disturbance.

**Complex PTSD (ICD-11)** — the signature of *prolonged, repeated, inescapable* trauma (childhood abuse, captivity, trafficking, chronic domestic violence). The three core PTSD clusters (re-experiencing, avoidance, sense of current threat) **plus** three **Disturbances in Self-Organization (DSO)**:
- **Affect dysregulation** — heightened reactivity, violent outbursts, reckless/self-destructive behaviour, stress-triggered dissociation, or conversely emotional numbing and inability to feel pleasure.
- **Negative self-concept** — persistent beliefs about oneself as *diminished, defeated, or worthless*, accompanied by deep, pervasive shame, guilt, or failure related to the trauma. (The self-blame the abused child took on to preserve the fiction that the caregiver was safe.)
- **Disturbances in relationships** — difficulty feeling close to others, avoidance of relationships, persistent sense of disconnection. (C-PTSD's DSO overlaps heavily with BPD — many argue they are the same terrain seen through two diagnostic traditions.)

**Adjacent trauma constructs worth the palette:**
- **Betrayal trauma** (Jennifer Freyd) — trauma perpetrated by a trusted, depended-upon other (parent, partner, institution) produces *betrayal blindness*: the victim must not-know to preserve the necessary attachment. Explains the abused child's loyalty to the abuser and the adult's repeated tolerance of betrayal.
- **Moral injury** (Jonathan Shay, *Achilles in Vietnam*; Litz et al.) — the wound not of *fear* but of *conscience*: from perpetrating, failing to prevent, or witnessing acts that violate one's deepest moral beliefs (the soldier who did, or was ordered to do, the unforgivable). Signature: shame, guilt, self-condemnation, loss of meaning and trust — distinct from fear-based PTSD.
- **The "ghost" / the wound** — in craft terms (repo Layer-6 backstory → Layer-3 fear), the formative injury that generates the standing avoidance and the defense. Every wounded character has one; the fear *protects* what the wound taught them was unbearable to lose again.

**Vivid exemplars.** Fight-type: the veteran who answers every stress with aggression (many Vietnam-narrative characters). Flight/workaholic: the perfectionist who cannot stop moving because stillness lets the past in. Freeze/dissociation: characters who "go somewhere else" under threat, the numb survivor. Fawn: the eldest-daughter people-pleaser with no self, the abused partner managing the abuser's moods, Dobby's compulsive self-punishing service. PTSD: Rambo (*First Blood* — the intrusion and hyperarousal, not the sequels' action figure), Katniss Everdeen (nightmares, hypervigilance, numbing), Frodo (irreparably altered, cannot re-enter ordinary life — a portrait of PTSD's permanence). C-PTSD: Lisbeth Salander (childhood-abuse-forged affect dysregulation, negative self-concept, relational armour), Jessica Jones (dissociation, self-destruction, shame). Moral injury: Achilles' berserk grief-rage after Patroclus; the drone operator or executioner undone by what they did.

**What it gives you for character depth.** The **provenance engine for the wounded character** — a *causal, embodied, non-arbitrary* account of why a person is the way they are, which is exactly the repo's demand that every vector trace to genetics or history (`docs/generative-model.md`). The four F's give you *four distinct survival personalities* from the same raw injury (the abused child becomes the bully, the anxious achiever, the dissociator, or the pleaser — same wound, different program), and that fork is one of the most useful characterisation tools in this entire skill. C-PTSD's DSO gives you the *interior* of the broken protagonist — the shame, the self-worthlessness, the relational armour — with far more dignity and accuracy than "damaged goods." And the responses map straight onto the repo's `defense` patterns, giving the drives schema a trauma-grounded vocabulary.

**Combines-with.** The four F's **map directly onto `docs/drives-schema.md`'s `defense` field** (fight→attack/control, flight→withdraw, fawn→please, freeze→*a needed sixth: dissociate/numb*) — this is the tightest cross-link in the file; use it to give any authored defense a trauma origin. The **wound → fear → defense** chain *is* the repo's Layer-6→Layer-3 structure. Trauma responses are the Panksepp basis under threat (`docs/generative-model.md`): fight = RAGE, flight = FEAR, freeze = FEAR→dorsal shutdown, fawn = CARE/PANIC recruited for appeasement. C-PTSD's negative self-concept is a corrupted **self-image** stake (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`, Layer 9). Betrayal trauma is the **`view: self` split** weaponised for survival (must not-know). Secondary psychopathy and BPD both live at the trauma-forged end of this section. Attachment wounds (next) are the developmental substrate.

**Cautions.** (1) **Trauma is the most over-used word in contemporary characterisation** — not every hardship is trauma, not every sad backstory is PTSD; reserve the clinical signatures for genuine overwhelm, or the register goes flat. (2) **Polyvagal theory is popular but physiologically contested** — cite it as an evocative map of felt states (mobilised vs shutdown), not established neuroscience. (3) The **wound-as-single-flashback-that-explains-everything** is a cliché (the "one traumatic memory" reveal); real trauma is diffuse, embodied, and resistant to tidy narration — the *un*-narratable quality is truer than the neat origin scene. (4) **Trauma is not a superpower** — the "trauma made them brilliant/unstoppable" trope both falsifies and trivialises; write the cost, not the buff. (5) **Recovery is possible and common** — the permanently-broken victim is one truth (Frodo), but post-traumatic *growth* and integration are equally real; do not write survivors as only ever damaged. (6) Fawn especially is *invisible on the page* if you only write it as niceness — the tell is the *absence of self*, the boundary that is never there, the need that is never voiced.

**Sources.** Herman, *Trauma and Recovery* (1992); van der Kolk, *The Body Keeps the Score* (2014); Walker, *Complex PTSD: From Surviving to Thriving* (2013); Porges, *The Polyvagal Theory* (2011); WHO, *ICD-11* CPTSD (2022) & APA *DSM-5* PTSD criteria; Shay, *Achilles in Vietnam* (1994); Freyd, *Betrayal Trauma* (1996).

## Adjacent: attachment wounds — the relational template under the disorder

**Lineage & core idea.** **John Bowlby** (attachment theory, 1950s–70s) and **Mary Ainsworth** (the Strange Situation, 1970s) established that early caregiver relationships build an **internal working model** — a template for whether others are safe, whether the self is worthy of care, and how to get needs met. **Main & Solomon** (1986) added the *disorganized* pattern; **Hazan & Shaver** (1987) and **Bartholomew & Horowitz** (1991) carried it into adult romantic attachment. The template, formed pre-verbally, governs adult intimacy and is the developmental soil most personality pathology grows in.

**Structure in full.** The four adult patterns (Bartholomew's two-axis model: *model of self* × *model of other*):
- **Secure** (positive self, positive other) — comfortable with intimacy and autonomy; trusts, and can be trusted; the baseline healthy template. (Rare as a *protagonist wound*, common as the stabilising love interest.)
- **Anxious / Preoccupied** (negative self, positive other) — craves closeness, fears abandonment, clings, protests, needs constant reassurance; "I am not enough, they will leave." The engine under dependent and much of borderline. (≈ high separation-insecurity; the fawn/flight substrate.)
- **Avoidant / Dismissing** (positive self, negative other) — prizes self-reliance, suppresses need, keeps distance, devalues intimacy; "I don't need anyone." The engine under schizoid and narcissistic distance. (≈ Detachment; the flight/freeze substrate.)
- **Disorganized / Fearful-avoidant** (negative self, negative other) — the "come here / go away" collision: wants closeness *and* fears it, because the source of comfort was also the source of terror (the frightening caregiver). The classic **trauma / abuse** template, and the soil of borderline and C-PTSD relational instability. "I need you and you will destroy me."

**What it gives you / combines-with.** The **relational spine** under every wounded character — *why* they cling, flee, or oscillate in intimacy, one layer deeper than the trauma responses. Disorganized attachment is the single best account of borderline idealise↔devalue and C-PTSD relational chaos. It feeds `docs/relationships.md` directly (each character carries a working-model prior that biases how they read and build every bond), maps onto the anxious↔avoidant axis of the fear family (`docs/drives-schema.md`: "being abandoned" vs "losing autonomy/being controlled" as opposed core fears), and gives the trauma section its developmental origin. In HEXACO terms, anxious ≈ high Emotionality; avoidant ≈ low Emotionality + low Agreeableness-warmth.

**Cautions.** (1) Attachment "styles" are popular-psychology-flattened into fixed types; the research is *dimensional and context-varying* (a person can be secure with one partner, anxious with another). (2) Don't reduce a whole character to their attachment style — it is the *relational* template, not the whole person. (3) Adult self-report attachment and the developmental construct are related but not identical measures.

**Sources.** Bowlby, *Attachment and Loss* (1969–80); Ainsworth et al., *Patterns of Attachment* (1978); Bartholomew & Horowitz, "Attachment styles among young adults" (*JPSP*, 1991); Mikulincer & Shaver, *Attachment in Adulthood* (2016).

## Self-deception — how the villain is the hero of his own story

**Lineage.** **Leon Festinger**, *cognitive dissonance* (1957) — the mind's intolerance of holding self-image and contrary action together. **Anna Freud**, *The Ego and the Mechanisms of Defense* (1936), catalogued the *defense mechanisms*; **George Vaillant** ranked them by maturity. **Albert Bandura**, *moral disengagement* (1986–1999) — the specific mechanisms by which people do harm while feeling moral. **Ziva Kunda**, *motivated reasoning* (1990). **Robert Trivers**, *The Folly of Fools* (2011) — the evolutionary argument that we deceive ourselves *in order to deceive others more convincingly*. And the screenwriter's maxim, variously attributed: **"every villain is the hero of his own story."**

**Core idea.** Almost no one experiences themselves as evil. The mind protects its self-image (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`: self-image is a load-bearing stake) by *reconstruing* its own harmful acts as justified, necessary, deserved, or someone else's fault — *before* the guilt can land. This is the D-factor's defining second clause made mechanical: malevolence *comes pre-wrapped in justification.* The craft consequence is decisive: **a villain who knows he is evil is a cartoon; a villain who is certain he is right is a person.** The self-deception is not a garnish on the villainy — it *is* the interiority that makes the villain real, and it is the repo's `view: self` split doing its most important work.

**Structure in full.**

**Bandura's eight mechanisms of moral disengagement** (the villain's rationalisation toolkit — name the one your antagonist runs):
1. **Moral justification** — the harm serves a higher cause ("I burned the village to save the nation").
2. **Euphemistic labelling** — sanitised language ("collateral damage," "enhanced interrogation," "letting them go").
3. **Advantageous comparison** — "what I did is nothing next to what *they* do."
4. **Displacement of responsibility** — "I was following orders / I had no choice."
5. **Diffusion of responsibility** — "everyone was doing it; my part was tiny."
6. **Distorting / disregarding consequences** — refusing to see or minimising the harm done.
7. **Dehumanisation** — the victims are vermin, animals, not-really-people, so the moral rules don't apply.
8. **Attribution of blame** — "they brought it on themselves; they made me do it." (The abuser's signature, and the narcissist's.)

**Anna Freud's defense mechanisms** (self-protection more broadly; Vaillant's maturity ladder):
- *Psychotic* (reality-denying): denial, distortion, delusional projection.
- *Immature*: projection (my unbearable feeling is *yours*), acting out, passive aggression, fantasy, splitting (all-good/all-bad — the borderline/narcissistic core).
- *Neurotic*: repression, reaction formation (feeling X, performing the opposite — the closeted zealot), intellectualisation, displacement, rationalisation.
- *Mature*: sublimation (drive channelled into work/art), humour, altruism, suppression, anticipation. (Even the healthy self-deceives — maturity is *better* defenses, not none.)

**Cognitive dissonance & motivated reasoning** — the person acts against their self-image, feels the dissonance, and *resolves it by changing the belief, not the behaviour* ("I hurt them, and I am good, therefore they deserved it"). Effort/cruelty gets retroactively justified (the more you sacrifice for a bad cause, the more you must believe in it). Motivated reasoning: the conclusion is fixed first (I am right / I am good / they are to blame), and reasoning is recruited as its advocate.

**Trivers' self-deception-to-deceive-others** — the most unsettling piece: we bury the truth from *ourselves* because a sincere liar is more convincing than a knowing one. The villain who has fully convinced himself is more dangerous *and* more persuasive than the cynic who knows he lies — no tell, no flicker of guilt to read. (This is why the true believer frightens more than the mercenary.)

**The three-view structure (repo-native).** `docs/self-and-perception.md` and `docs/drives-schema.md`: every drive exists in **truth** (ground store) and optionally **self** (self-image) views, and *others-see* is derived. Self-deception is precisely a **truth-fear or truth-goal that is absent from, or inverted in, the self-view**: the man who calls his control "responsibility," the abuser who calls his cruelty "discipline," the one who stays "out of duty" but truly out of terror of being no one. The gap between truth-view and self-view *is* the character's richest interior material — the dramatic irony the reader sees and the character cannot.

**Vivid exemplars.** Moral justification: Thanos (genuine belief that genocide is mercy — the whole point of his menace is his sincerity), Javert (certainty that mercy is a moral failure). Attribution of blame: nearly every abuser ("look what you made me do"), Amy Dunne. Dehumanisation: the atrocity's functionaries, O'Brien in *1984*. Splitting: the borderline's lover who is a saint on Monday and a monster on Friday. Reaction formation: the crusading moralist policing the very desire he cannot admit. Trivers' true believer: the zealot, the cult leader who believes his own gospel, Commodus convinced of his own greatness. The *self*-view gap: Humbert Humbert narrating his predation as romance (the reader sees the truth the narrator's self-view forbids) — self-deception as unreliable narration.

**What it gives you for character depth.** The **conversion of a villain into a person** — the single highest-leverage move in this entire file. It supplies (a) the *interior* an antagonist needs to be more than a plot obstacle — the justification he actually believes, which you can write from *inside*; (b) the mechanism for **directing by circumstance without a seam** (`docs/generative-model.md`) — a self-deceiving character resists the truth the story pushes at him, which is *causally faithful* refusal, not authorial fiat; (c) a **redemption-arc target** — an arc is the slow collapse of a self-deception, the moment the truth-view breaks through the self-view (the recognition scene); and (d) the engine of **dramatic irony** — the reader holds truth-view + self-view simultaneously and watches the gap. Naming the *specific* disengagement mechanism (justification? blame? dehumanisation?) makes the rationalisation concrete and writable rather than a vague "he thinks he's right."

**Combines-with.** This is the operating layer under the whole file: the **D-factor's justification clause** (`trait-models.md`) made mechanical; the **narcissist's false self** (the self-view protecting against the wound); the **psychopath's** *lack* of it (the chilling exception — the pure psychopath may *not* need to self-deceive, because there is no conscience to appease, which is its own horror); the **abuser's** attribution-of-blame; **betrayal trauma's** must-not-know. Directly implements `docs/self-and-perception.md`'s three views and `docs/drives-schema.md`'s `view: self`. Splitting connects borderline/narcissistic idealise↔devalue. Feeds `docs/values-and-stakes.md` (self-image as the stake being defended) and the arc engine (`docs/arc-engine.md`) as the thing an arc dismantles.

**Cautions.** (1) **The pure psychopath is the exception** — a character with genuinely no conscience may need *no* justification (harm is frictionless), so don't reflexively give every villain a tortured rationale; sometimes the absence of one is the more terrifying choice. (2) Self-deception must be *shown through behaviour and the truth/self gap*, not narrated as "he told himself he was right" — let the reader infer the gap. (3) The "sympathetic villain with a point" can tip into *endorsing* the villainy — a self-justification the narrative fails to counter reads as the author agreeing; hold the truth-view visible. (4) Overdone, "everyone's the hero of their own story" flattens into moral relativism — some harms are simply harms; the mechanism explains the *perpetrator's experience*, it does not exonerate. (5) Not all self-deception is villainous — the mature defenses (sublimation, humour) and the trauma survivor's protective not-knowing are the *same machinery* serving survival; reserve the villain register for where it enables harm.

**Sources.** Festinger, *A Theory of Cognitive Dissonance* (1957); A. Freud, *The Ego and the Mechanisms of Defense* (1936); Bandura, "Moral disengagement in the perpetration of inhumanities" (*Personality and Social Psychology Review*, 1999); Kunda, "The case for motivated reasoning" (*Psychological Bulletin*, 1990); Trivers, *The Folly of Fools* (2011); Vaillant, *Adaptation to Life* (1977); Baumeister, *Evil: Inside Human Violence and Cruelty* (1997).

## The ethics of writing pathology — craft over diagnosis, and doing no cheap harm

Not an optional coda; a constraint on everything above. Four load-bearing commitments:

- **Build the person, not the label.** A diagnosis is an insurer's family-resemblance checklist, not a character. Start from the *caused particular* — this wound, this fear, this defense, this justification — and let a reader *maybe* recognise a pattern after the fact. Author backward (the person the story needs), validate forward (a history that plausibly yields them) — `docs/generative-model.md`. The label is a lens for *you*, never a costume for *them*, and never a word in the prose ("she was a classic narcissist" is telling, and lazy telling at that).

- **Mental illness ≠ villainy — and the conflation does real harm.** The "insane = violent," "disfigured/disordered = evil," "split personality = killer" tropes are both false and stigmatising. The evidence is blunt: **serious mental illness is a factor in only about 4% of violent crime**, and people with mental illness are **far more likely to be victims than perpetrators** (by some measures ~12×). Most violence has nothing to do with mental illness; most mentally ill people harm no one. Using "crazy" as a villain-explanation (a) libels a real, vulnerable population and (b) is *bad craft* — it substitutes a stigma for a motive. If a character's harm needs a cause, give it the real ones: the low-H disposition, the wound, the self-deception, the circumstance — not "they were mentally ill."

- **Separate the two registers.** *Personality-disorder / dark-trait* content (narcissism, psychopathy, the low-H region) describes *how a person chooses and relates* — legitimate villain material, because it is about agency and character. *Trauma, mood, psychosis, and most Cluster A/C conditions* describe *suffering* — and writing suffering as menace is where the harm lives. A psychopath choosing to exploit is a moral agent; a person having a psychotic episode is not a monster. Keep the villainy in the *choices and the justifications*, not in the *symptoms of pain*.

- **Dignity for the wounded protagonist.** The broken character (C-PTSD, borderline, the abuse survivor) deserves interiority, agency, and the possibility of growth — not to be "damaged goods," a manic-pixie wound, or a cautionary corpse. Write the cost honestly *and* the personhood underneath; trauma is not a personality, and recovery is real. The most humane and the most dramatically alive choice are, here, the same choice: the fully *caused, particular, self-experiencing* person — which is the whole repo's definition of faithful (`README.md`).

**Sources.** Corrigan et al., stigma-and-media research; NAMI and Lancet Public Health on mental illness & violence prevalence; Swanson et al. / MacArthur Violence Risk studies (population-attributable risk of violence ≈ 3–5%); Teplin et al., "Crime victimization in adults with severe mental illness" (*Archives of General Psychiatry*, 2005 — victimization 6–23× the general-population rate by crime type); disability-studies critiques of the "mental illness = monster" trope.

## How to reach into this file

Start from what the shadow in front of you *is*, not from a diagnosis:

- **Writing a villain who must feel real, not cartoonish** → the **self-deception** section first (the justification he believes — name the Bandura mechanism), then the trait region (`trait-models.md` low-H/Dark Triad) for the disposition, then a clinical overlay (**narcissism** or **psychopathy**) only if the mechanism fits. The interiority is the self/truth-view gap, not the crime.
- **A cold, instrumental, charming predator** → **psychopathy** (mask, Factor-1 core, low FEAR, primary vs secondary fork; boldness for the *functional* one). Reserve rage for a different engine — this one is the *absence* of brakes.
- **A grandiose or aggrieved antagonist with a fault line** → **narcissism** (grandiose vs vulnerable; the injury→rage; Kohut-reachable vs Kernberg-armoured to *choose the arc*). The most dramatically productive shadow.
- **A tyrant, despot, cult-leader, or dark lord — grandiose *and* cruel-on-principle** → **malignant narcissism** (NPD + antisocial + paranoid orientation + ego-syntonic sadism), the composite that stacks four overlays on one low-H spine. The most complete villain portrait here — but hold its cruelty in the *choices and justifications*, never in "madness."
- **A broken, wounded protagonist or sympathetic antihero** → **trauma responses** (which of the four F's became their personality?) + **C-PTSD** (the DSO interior: affect dysregulation, negative self-concept, relational armour) + **attachment** (the relational template underneath). Provenance, not pathology.
- **A rigid, one-note extreme (the paranoid, the OCPD moralist, the eerie recluse)** → the **DSM-5 categories** for the silhouette, used as pressure-points, not diagnoses.
- **An unusual hybrid the ten categories can't name** → the **AMPD/PID-5 domains** — dial a Criterion-A severity + a Criterion-B trait blend; it's the repo-native way (extreme means + collapsed variability = rigidity).
- **Any of the above** → run it through the **ethics** section and the repo spine: build the caused particular (`docs/generative-model.md`), tag the wound→fear→defense (`docs/drives-schema.md`), split truth-view from self-view (`docs/self-and-perception.md`), and never let a stigma stand in for a motive.

**The through-line:** the shadow is not a separate kind of person — it is the ordinary machinery (the HEXACO region, the Panksepp basis, the wound→fear→defense chain, the self-image stake) pushed to an extreme and, usually, *frozen there* by a history the character cannot metabolise. Disorder = extreme mean + collapsed variability. Villainy = harm plus a justification the person believes. Woundedness = an old survival program still running against a present that no longer needs it. Build the cause, name the mechanism, keep the dignity — and let the observer supply the frightening word after the fact.
