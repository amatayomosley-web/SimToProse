# Type and Temperament Typologies — the categorical well

The character frameworks in the other reference files are mostly *dimensional*: HEXACO's six continua, Schwartz's ten values on two axes, the seven Panksepp primaries, the drives schema's opposable operands. This file holds the other great tradition — the **categorical** one, which sorts people into a small number of named kinds: humors, functions, sixteen types, nine Enneagram points. Categorical systems trade the fine grain and psychometric defensibility of trait models for something a dimensional model cannot easily give: a *whole*, a gestalt, a felt "ah, that kind of person" that arrives as a package of motive, blind spot, gift, and characteristic collapse. For a project that generates characters and cuts their lives into novels, that gestalt is a fast, generative scaffold — a way to conjure a coherent person in one gesture and then dissolve the label back into the repo's dimensional machinery, where behavior is actually produced. Every type here is a **lens, not a law**. Read them as a designer's palette; be candid, as the sections are, about where the science thins to folk-psychology. The repo's own defaults (HEXACO, Schwartz + Moral Foundations, the Panksepp seven, the drives schema) are the *dimensional* backbone; the typologies below are the *categorical* companions you translate into that backbone when a type-word is the quickest way to name a soul.

---

## Where the repo sits — types as lenses over dimensional defaults

Before the well, the house position, so the cross-links below land. The project **stores and resolves behavior dimensionally**, never by type:

- **Disposition** is HEXACO — six factors (Honesty-Humility, Emotionality, eXtraversion, Agreeableness, Conscientiousness, Openness), each a `(mean, variability)` distribution, tracked to facet grain, resolved by sampling (`docs/trait-theory.md`).
- **Worth** is Schwartz's ten values + Moral Foundations + SDT needs — a shared menu the person weights (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`).
- **Affect** is the seven Panksepp primaries — SEEKING, FEAR, RAGE, CARE, PANIC/GRIEF, LUST, PLAY — the action-dispositional basis emotions and virtues are *generated* from, not stored (`docs/generative-model.md`).
- **Motivation** is the drives schema — per-person goals, fears/wounds, and orientation (locus, coping), all opposable (`docs/drives-schema.md`).

A **type** is a shorthand for a *region* in that space plus a *characteristic dynamic*. "Enneagram 6" is not a field on the character; it is a compact name for {high FEAR baseline, security-weighted worth, a wound around trust, a scan-for-threat defense} — a bias-pack over the dimensional basis, exactly like `character-model.md`'s model archetypes. So the workflow is always **type in, dimensions out**: reach for a typology to *conjure* a coherent person fast, then immediately translate the gestalt into HEXACO leanings, value weights, primary-emotion baselines, and drives — because that is what the decision engine runs on, and because a stored type-label would re-import every validity problem catalogued below. Types are generative scaffolding and a communication compression; they are never the operand the engine resolves.

## The Four Classical Temperaments (sanguine · choleric · melancholic · phlegmatic)

**Name and lineage.** The oldest surviving personality typology in the West. Roots in Empedocles' four elements (5th c. BCE) and Hippocrates' four humors (*On the Nature of Man*, c. 400 BCE); systematized by **Galen** (*De Temperamentis*, 2nd c. CE) into four temperament-kinds keyed to bodily fluids. Carried through medieval and Renaissance medicine, revived psychologically by **Kant** (*Anthropology*, 1798) and **Wundt** (who re-plotted the four on strength-of-emotion × changeability axes, 1900s), and — decisively — by **Hans Eysenck**, whose modern factor-analytic work re-derived the same four as the corners of an **Extraversion × Neuroticism** plane (choleric = unstable extravert, melancholic = unstable introvert, sanguine = stable extravert, phlegmatic = stable introvert). Echoed in every later four-quadrant model (DISC, Keirsey, Fisher's neurochemical four, LaHaye's Christian temperament books). The humoral *physiology* is dead science; the four behavioral *portraits* proved unexpectedly durable because they roughly track two real dimensions — the very two (Wundt's strong/weak emotion ≈ Neuroticism; changeable/unchangeable ≈ Extraversion) Eysenck later confirmed statistically.

**Core idea.** A person's habitual mood, energy, and social tempo were thought to follow the dominant bodily humor. Excess blood → sanguine, yellow bile → choleric, black bile → melancholic, phlegm → phlegmatic. Strip the fluids and four recognizable *behavioral styles* remain, later understood as combinations of two axes: **emotional reactivity** (strong/weak) × **response speed / changeability** (quick/slow).

**Structure in full.**
- **Sanguine** (blood · air · spring · warm-moist). Sociable, buoyant, enthusiastic, talkative, pleasure-seeking, impulsive. Lives in the present; charms easily; scatters, forgets, over-promises. Wundt: **weak-and-changeable** → *low emotional reactivity, quick tempo* (emotionally stable yet fast-moving — Eysenck's stable extravert). Modern read: high Extraversion, low-ish Conscientiousness, high positive affect.
- **Choleric** (yellow bile · fire · summer · warm-dry). Ambitious, driven, decisive, dominant, competitive, quick to anger. Leader and goad; impatient, controlling, prideful. Wundt: **strong-and-changeable** → *high reactivity, quick* (Eysenck's unstable extravert). Modern read: high Extraversion + low Agreeableness + high drive; RAGE-accessible.
- **Melancholic** (black bile · earth · autumn · cold-dry). Analytical, deep, conscientious, idealistic, perfectionistic, prone to sadness and rumination. Loyal and thorough; withdrawn, self-critical, pessimistic. Wundt: **strong-and-unchangeable** → strong emotion, slow (Eysenck's unstable introvert). Modern read: high Neuroticism/Emotionality + high Conscientiousness + introversion; PANIC/GRIEF-accessible.
- **Phlegmatic** (phlegm · water · winter · cold-moist). Calm, steady, patient, easygoing, agreeable, dependable. Peacemaker; conflict-averse, passive, slow to start, can seem indifferent. Wundt: **weak-and-unchangeable** → low reactivity, slow (Eysenck's stable introvert). Modern read: high Agreeableness, low arousal, emotional stability, low assertiveness.

Classically a person is a *blend* (a primary + secondary temperament — "choleric-melancholic"), not a pure kind — an early gesture at the dimensional truth underneath.

**Vivid exemplars.** Sanguine: Tigger; Falstaff; the Fool in a tarot deck; a golden-retriever host who never met a stranger. Choleric: Achilles; Miranda Priestly; a battlefield commander; the driven founder who burns out the room. Melancholic: Hamlet; Eeyore; the cloistered scholar-monk; Severus Snape's brooding interior. Phlegmatic: Samwise Gamgee's steadiness; a village elder who defuses every quarrel; Bilbo before the adventure; the unflappable butler.

**What it gives you for character depth.** The fastest possible temperament sketch — four words that fix a person's *tempo and default mood* before any content is decided. Its blend logic (primary + secondary) is a good teaching wedge toward "no one is one way." Because it is culturally ancient, it is legible to readers as archetype and lets you seed period characters (a medieval physician *thinks* in humors). Its two-axis reduction previews the modern circumplex, so it is a gentle on-ramp to dimensional thinking.

**Combines-with.** Maps cleanly onto **DISC** (choleric≈D, sanguine≈I, phlegmatic≈S, melancholic≈C), onto **Eysenck's Extraversion × Neuroticism** plane (its most defensible modern form), and onto the **Extraversion × Agreeableness/Neuroticism** corner of HEXACO. Sanguine/choleric = high-arousal, extraverted PLAY/SEEKING & RAGE; melancholic = low-arousal negative affect (FEAR/GRIEF); phlegmatic = low-arousal, high-Agreeableness calm. Feeds Panksepp baselines directly: choleric raises RAGE's gain, melancholic raises PANIC/GRIEF and FEAR, sanguine raises PLAY and SEEKING.

**Cautions.** The humoral *mechanism* is pseudoscience — do not have a modern narrator endorse it. As a typology it is coarse: four buckets lose everyone in the middle, and the portraits smuggle in value judgments (melancholic-as-gifted-sufferer is a Romantic flattery). Cliché trap: reducing a character to a single humor produces a mascot, not a person. Use it to *start* a temperament, then push variance and let drives override the lean.

**Sources.** Galen, *De Temperamentis*. Hippocrates, *On the Nature of Man*. Kant, *Anthropology from a Pragmatic Point of View* (1798). Wundt, *Grundzüge der physiologischen Psychologie* (1900s temperament plot). Eysenck, *The Biological Basis of Personality* (1967) — the N×E re-derivation. Stelmack & Stalikas, "Galen and the humour theory of temperament," *Personality and Individual Differences* (1991).

## Jungian Cognitive Functions (the type engine under MBTI, Keirsey, and Socionics)

**Name and lineage.** Carl Gustav Jung, *Psychological Types* (1921). The direct ancestor of MBTI, Keirsey, and Socionics — each of which is a different elaboration of Jung's raw material. Extended in the analytic-psychology tradition by Marie-Louise von Franz and, most influentially for typology hobbyists, by Jungian analyst **John Beebe** (the eight-function / eight-archetype model, 1980s–2000s).

**Core idea.** Jung proposed that conscious orientation splits along an **attitude** (introversion ↔ extraversion — where psychic energy flows, inward or outward) and four **functions** by which we take in and judge the world. Crucially, each function operates in either attitude, yielding **eight cognitive functions**; a person has a *hierarchy* of these, a dominant and an inferior, and the inferior is the seat of the shadow. Type is thus a *dynamic system of functions*, not a static box.

**Structure in full.**

*The two attitudes:* **Introversion** (energy/attention toward the inner, subjective world) and **Extraversion** (toward the outer, objective world). Not shyness/sociability — orientation of libido.

*The four functions,* in two pairs:
- **Rational / Judging** (how we decide): **Thinking** (logical, impersonal ordering — truth/consistency) and **Feeling** (value-based ordering — worth/harmony; a rational function in Jung's sense, not "emotion").
- **Irrational / Perceiving** (how we take in): **Sensation** (concrete, present, sensory fact) and **Intuition** (patterns, possibilities, the not-yet-present).

*The eight functions* (function × attitude), each a distinct cognitive style:
- **Te — Extraverted Thinking:** organizes the external world by logic, efficiency, metrics, procedure. (The systematizer, the operations mind.)
- **Ti — Introverted Thinking:** builds an internal framework of precise, self-consistent principles. (The theorist, the definitions-sharpener.)
- **Fe — Extraverted Feeling:** reads and tends the emotional field of the group; harmony, warmth, social value. (The host, the diplomat.)
- **Fi — Introverted Feeling:** a deep, private value-core; authenticity, "what is true for me." (The idealist, the conscience.)
- **Se — Extraverted Sensation:** vivid immersion in the concrete present; action, impact, the tangible now. (The performer, the operator.)
- **Si — Introverted Sensation:** detailed internal memory of past sensory experience; continuity, reliability, tradition. (The keeper, the archivist of how-it's-done.)
- **Ne — Extraverted Intuition:** proliferating external possibilities, connections, what-ifs. (The brainstormer, the ideator.)
- **Ni — Introverted Intuition:** convergent inner vision toward a single deep pattern or foresight. (The seer, the strategist.)

*The stack (function hierarchy).* Each MBTI-style type is an ordered four of these: **dominant** (the trusted lead), **auxiliary** (the balancing support — opposite in judging/perceiving and attitude), **tertiary** (relief, less mature), **inferior** (the "inferior function," aspirational and eruptive — the doorway to the unconscious, where one is childish, gripped, or transformed). E.g., a dom-Ni / aux-Te person leads with convergent vision, supports with external logic.

*Beebe's eight-function, eight-archetype model.* Beebe extends the stack to all eight functions and assigns each an **archetypal role** — a small cast inhabiting one psyche. Positions 1–4 (conscious): **Hero/Heroine** (dominant), **Good Parent** (auxiliary), **Puer/Puella — Eternal Child** (tertiary), **Anima/Animus** (inferior, the bridge to the unconscious and to relationship). Positions 5–8 (the shadow, same four functions in opposite attitude): **Opposing Personality** (5 — obstinate, contrarian), **Senex/Witch** (6 — the withering critic/authority), **Trickster** (7 — the double-binding, misleading function), **Demon/Daimon** (8 — the destructive-or-redemptive depth). The shadow functions surface under stress, in projection, and in the enemy one keeps meeting.

**Vivid exemplars.** Dominant Ni: Paul Atreides' convergent foresight; a chess prodigy who sees one line to the end. Dominant Ne: a restless inventor spraying ideas (Doc Brown). Dominant Fi: a quiet character with an unbreakable private moral line (Atticus Finch's inner compass). Dominant Te: a ruthless operations executive who ships. Inferior function gripped: the hyper-logical Ti-dom who, exhausted, erupts in a flood of raw, clumsy Fe (a needy plea for connection they can't articulate) — the "grip" is a character-defining collapse. Beebe's Trickster: the mentor whose one blind-spot function keeps sabotaging their student in ways neither can name.

**What it gives you for character depth.** This is the *most process-oriented* of the type systems — it models not just what a person is like but *how their cognition moves*, and where it breaks. The dominant/inferior polarity is a ready-made **arc engine**: growth = integrating the inferior; collapse = being gripped by it. Beebe's cast turns one interior into a *drama of parts* — the Hero, the wounded Anima, the sabotaging Trickster — which is gold for interiority and for rendering self-conflict without a second character in the room. It also explains *why* a person is reliably brilliant in one register and reliably blind in another.

**Combines-with.** It is the substrate of **MBTI**, **Keirsey**, and **Socionics** — read those as three grammars over this vocabulary. Maps loosely onto HEXACO Openness (N functions ≈ high O; S functions ≈ low O) and onto the judging axis of Agreeableness/Honesty (Fe/Fi ≈ affiliative valuation; Te/Ti ≈ impersonal). The **inferior-function grip** is a clean instance of the repo's "out-of-character tail sample" (`docs/trait-theory.md`) and of a Panksepp primary breaking through a suppressive baseline. Beebe's shadow cast pairs naturally with the **three views** (`docs/self-and-perception.md`) — the Trickster and Demon are precisely the disowned material self-image won't admit.

**Cautions.** Function theory is **not psychometrically validated** — there is no reliable instrument that measures the eight functions, and much of the elaboration (especially online "function stacks") is enthusiast lore, not science. It is easy to over-fit: any behavior can be post-hoc attributed to some function in some position. Treat it as a rich *generative* and *interpretive* language for a writer, never as a measurement. Beebe's archetypes are clinical-analytic conjecture, evocative but unfalsifiable.

**Sources.** Jung, *Psychological Types* (Collected Works vol. 6, 1921). John Beebe, *Energies and Patterns in Psychological Type* (2016) and "Evolving the Eight-Function Model" (APTi). Marie-Louise von Franz & James Hillman, *Lectures on Jung's Typology* (1971). Daryl Sharp, *Personality Types: Jung's Model of Typology* (1987).

## MBTI — the Sixteen Types

**Name and lineage.** The Myers-Briggs Type Indicator. Devised by **Katharine Cook Briggs** and her daughter **Isabel Briggs Myers** (neither a trained psychologist) from Jung's *Psychological Types*, developed from the 1940s, published as an instrument in 1962, now administered by the Myers-Briggs Company / CPP. The most widely used personality instrument in the corporate world and the most contested in academic psychology.

**Core idea.** Four dichotomies, each a forced binary, combine into **2⁴ = 16 types**, written as four letters. Myers overlaid a Jungian *function stack* on each type (the dominant/auxiliary/etc. of the prior section), so a "type" is meant to imply a whole cognitive hierarchy, though most popular use treats the four letters as independent switches.

**Structure in full.**

*The four dichotomies:*
- **E / I — Extraversion / Introversion:** outward, breadth, action-first vs. inward, depth, reflection-first.
- **S / N — Sensing / iNtuition:** concrete, factual, present, detail vs. abstract, pattern, future, meaning.
- **T / F — Thinking / Feeling:** impersonal logic and consistency vs. person-centered values and harmony.
- **J / P — Judging / Perceiving:** (Myers' addition to Jung) preference for closure, structure, scheduled vs. openness, flexibility, spontaneous. J/P also *points to* which function is extraverted, wiring the stack.

*The sixteen types* (with the popular four-word gloss and a vivid exemplar — typings are contested, so read each as "commonly read as"):

- **ISTJ — the Inspector / Logistician.** Dutiful, thorough, tradition-keeping. *Exemplar:* Eddard "Ned" Stark; the by-the-book quartermaster.
- **ISFJ — the Protector / Defender.** Warm, loyal, self-sacrificing caretaker. *Exemplar:* Samwise Gamgee; the devoted nurse.
- **INFJ — the Advocate / Counselor.** Visionary, private, moral, insight-driven. *Exemplar:* Atticus Finch; a quiet reformer-mystic.
- **INTJ — the Mastermind / Architect.** Strategic, independent, long-game. *Exemplar:* a chessmaster antagonist; Petyr Baelish's cold planning.
- **ISTP — the Crafter / Virtuoso.** Cool, hands-on, unflappable problem-solver. *Exemplar:* the laconic mechanic-gunslinger; Han Solo under the hood.
- **ISFP — the Composer / Adventurer.** Gentle, aesthetic, present-living, values-driven. *Exemplar:* the wandering artist; a soft-spoken ranger.
- **INFP — the Healer / Mediator.** Idealistic, imaginative, deeply principled dreamer. *Exemplar:* Frodo's inward idealism; the poet-outsider.
- **INTP — the Architect / Logician.** Theoretical, precise, detached idea-builder. *Exemplar:* the absent-minded theorist; a young detached logician.
- **ESTP — the Dynamo / Entrepreneur.** Bold, tactical, thrill-and-action. *Exemplar:* the daring con-artist; a fast-talking gambler.
- **ESFP — the Performer / Entertainer.** Spontaneous, warm, spotlight-loving. *Exemplar:* the irrepressible showman; a life-of-the-party bard.
- **ENFP — the Champion / Campaigner.** Enthusiastic, possibility-drunk, people-igniting. *Exemplar:* the charismatic rebel-recruiter.
- **ENTP — the Visionary / Debater.** Inventive, argumentative, idea-juggling. *Exemplar:* the trickster inventor; a devil's-advocate schemer.
- **ESTJ — the Supervisor / Executive.** Organizing, decisive, order-enforcing. *Exemplar:* the stern magistrate; a no-nonsense sergeant-major.
- **ESFJ — the Provider / Consul.** Sociable, dutiful, harmony-tending host. *Exemplar:* the community matriarch; Molly Weasley.
- **ENFJ — the Teacher / Protagonist.** Charismatic, mentoring, people-mobilizing. *Exemplar:* the inspiring coach; a magnetic movement-leader.
- **ENTJ — the Commander / Fieldmarshal.** Commanding, strategic, driving to the goal. *Exemplar:* the empire-builder; a ruthless CEO-general.

**What it gives you for character depth.** A shared, near-universal *vocabulary* — collaborators, and readers, know roughly what "INTJ villain" or "ENFP sidekick" evokes, which makes it a fast casting language. The four axes are a quick coverage checklist that keeps an ensemble from all thinking alike (deliberately spreading S/N and T/F across a cast prevents a monoculture). The J/P and E/I dials calibrate *tempo and social surface* efficiently. And each type carries a **stress-and-growth pattern** via its inferior function (the ESTJ who, cornered, spirals into catastrophic Fi/Ni gloom), which is arc material.

**Combines-with.** It is Jungian functions (prior section) packaged into sixteen boxes; **Keirsey** groups these sixteen into four temperaments; **Socionics** is a parallel sixteen with a different function model. Onto HEXACO: I/E ≈ eXtraversion; T/F ≈ Agreeableness (and Emotionality); S/N ≈ Openness; J/P ≈ Conscientiousness (the one robust empirical mapping — MBTI axes correlate with four of the Big Five, with **Honesty-Humility unrepresented**, which is exactly why the repo prefers HEXACO for moral drama). Translate a type to those leanings and discard the letters.

**Cautions.** The headline problem: **the dichotomies are not bimodal.** Real trait distributions are smooth and unimodal, so the forced cut at the middle is arbitrary — a person one point either side of the E/I line is labeled opposite kinds. **Test-retest reliability is poor for the type label:** across independent studies a large share of people — Pittenger's often-cited range is **39–76%** on retest after roughly five weeks — get a *different* four-letter type, precisely because so many sit near a dichotomy's midpoint where a point's drift flips the letter. (The publisher's own data report higher scale-level stability, r ≈ 0.8; the instability is in the *categorical label*, not the underlying continuous scores — which is the whole point.) It **does not reliably predict** job performance or major life outcomes; the 1991 National Academy of Sciences review found insufficient basis for career use. The descriptions also lean on the **Barnum effect** (flattering, universally-agreeable prose). Storytelling verdict: superb as a *casting shorthand and communication compression*, worthless as a *measurement* — never store the label as if it were data; translate to dimensions immediately.

**Sources.** Isabel Briggs Myers with Peter Myers, *Gifts Differing* (1980). Myers & McCaulley, *MBTI Manual* (3rd ed., 1998). Pittenger, "Cautionary Comments Regarding the MBTI," *Consulting Psychology Journal* (2005). Boyle, "Myers-Briggs Type Indicator: some psychometric limitations," *Australian Psychologist* (1995). Stein & Swan, "Evaluating the validity of Myers-Briggs Type Indicator theory," *Social and Personality Psychology Compass* (2019).

## Keirsey's Four Temperaments (and the sixteen Role Variants)

**Name and lineage.** David West Keirsey, *Please Understand Me* (1978) and *Please Understand Me II* (1998). A re-grouping of the sixteen MBTI types into **four temperaments**, explicitly re-anchored to a lineage *older* than MBTI: Keirsey took the four names from **Plato**'s *Republic* (four "kinds of men"), tied them to the Hippocratic-Galenic humors and to **Ernst Kretschmer**'s constitutional types, and cited Adickes, Spranger, and Fromm as forerunners. His move: temperament (the deep pattern) is primary; MBTI's letters are surface indicators.

**Core idea.** Keirsey argued the sixteen types cluster into four coherent temperaments defined not by Jungian functions but by **observable action** — how people *use tools/words* and pursue *goals*. He built the split on two abstractions: **communication style** (concrete/Sensing vs. abstract/iNtuitive) and **action style** (cooperative/rule-abiding vs. utilitarian/results-first), plus a role-directive/informative sub-split, yielding four temperaments and, within each, four **role variants**.

**Structure in full.**

*The two top splits.* First, **concrete (S) vs. abstract (N)** speech — do you talk mostly of facts/the present, or of ideas/possibilities? Second, within concretes: **cooperative (SJ)** vs. **utilitarian (SP)**; within abstracts: **cooperative (NF)** vs. **utilitarian (NT)**. (Keirsey's "cooperative" = does the socially sanctioned/right thing; "utilitarian/pragmatic" = does what works.)

*The four temperaments:*
- **Artisan (SP)** — the tactical, sensation-seeking maker/performer. Prizes freedom, action, impact, skill, the present. Optimistic, adaptable, impulsive. Plato's *iconic*; the choleric/sanguine energy. *Role variants:* Promoter (ESTP), Crafter (ISTP), Performer (ESFP), Composer (ISFP).
- **Guardian (SJ)** — the logistical, security-seeking steward. Prizes duty, order, tradition, responsibility, belonging. Dependable, cautious, hierarchical. Plato's *pistic*; the melancholic/phlegmatic steadiness. *Role variants:* Supervisor (ESTJ), Inspector (ISTJ), Provider (ESFJ), Protector (ISFJ).
- **Idealist (NF)** — the diplomatic, identity-seeking mentor/advocate. Prizes meaning, authenticity, growth, human potential, empathy. Warm, romantic, conflict-averse. Plato's *noetic*. *Role variants:* Teacher (ENFJ), Counselor (INFJ), Champion (ENFP), Healer (INFP).
- **Rational (NT)** — the strategic, knowledge-seeking architect/analyst. Prizes competence, logic, mastery, system, autonomy. Skeptical, independent, cool. Plato's *dianoetic*. *Role variants:* Fieldmarshal (ENTJ), Mastermind (INTJ), Inventor (ENTP), Architect (INTP).

Each temperament, Keirsey held, has characteristic **needs, values, talents, and social roles**, and even a signature form of intelligence (tactical / logistical / diplomatic / strategic).

**Vivid exemplars.** Artisan: Han Solo, Jack Sparrow, the daredevil pilot, the street performer. Guardian: Ned Stark, a stern headmistress, the loyal quartermaster, the parish constable. Idealist: Atticus Finch, a visionary teacher, the empath-healer, Jo March's romantic ardor. Rational: Sherlock's cold competence, a systems-building strategist, the aloof professor-general.

**What it gives you for character depth.** Four is a more workable ensemble palette than sixteen — casting a party as one of each (Artisan, Guardian, Idealist, Rational) instantly guarantees clashing *values and tempos*, which is drama by construction. Keirsey's action-orientation ("what tools they reach for") makes it more *behavioral* and less introspective than raw MBTI — good for scene-writing, where you show conduct. The temperament-level **core need** (Artisan→freedom/impact, Guardian→belonging/order, Idealist→meaning/identity, Rational→mastery/competence) is a fast handle on what a character will fight for.

**Combines-with.** Sits directly atop **MBTI** (it *is* a regrouping of the sixteen). Its four core needs translate almost one-to-one into **Schwartz values** (Artisan≈stimulation/hedonism, Guardian≈security/conformity/tradition, Idealist≈benevolence/universalism, Rational≈self-direction/achievement) — a clean bridge to `docs/values-and-stakes.md`. Maps onto the **four classical temperaments** and **DISC** by the same two-axis logic. Because it foregrounds *values and needs*, it is the typology that best connects to the repo's worth-menu.

**Cautions.** Inherits MBTI's validity problems (built on the same dichotomies) and adds Keirsey's own untested claims about "four intelligences." The temperament portraits are vivid but stereotype-prone (the "flighty Artisan," the "rigid Guardian"). Keirsey's dismissal of the Jungian function machinery is a genuine theoretical fork, not a settled truth — don't present it as *the* correct reading of MBTI. As always: a casting and values scaffold, not a measurement.

**Sources.** Keirsey & Bates, *Please Understand Me* (1978). Keirsey, *Please Understand Me II* (1998). Plato, *Republic* (Book on the four kinds). Kretschmer, *Physique and Character* (1925).

## Socionics — Model A, Quadras, and Intertype Relations

**Name and lineage.** Developed by Lithuanian economist and sociologist **Aušra Augustinavičiūtė ("Aushra Augusta")** in the 1970s–80s, in the then-USSR, fusing Jung's *Psychological Types* with **Antoni Kępiński**'s concept of *information metabolism* (the mind as a processor of information "food"). Widely studied in Russia, Ukraine, and the Baltics; largely parallel to, and independent of, the Western MBTI tradition, though it uses a similar sixteen-type frame. Elaborated by Grigory Reinin (the extra dichotomies) and many later socionists.

**Core idea.** People differ in how they *metabolize information* across eight **information elements** (Jung's functions, reconceived as kinds of information). Each of the sixteen **sociotypes** is defined by a fixed eight-slot structure — **Model A** — into which the eight elements are plugged in a type-specific order. Socionics' signature contribution is that it is fundamentally a theory of **relationships**: from Model A it derives precise, named **intertype relations** predicting compatibility and friction between any two types.

**Structure in full.**

*The eight information elements* (each function in an extraverted/"black" and an introverted/"white" form; conventionally drawn as filled vs. hollow geometric glyphs):
- **Ne (intuition of possibilities), Ni (intuition of time/development)** — perceiving, intuitive.
- **Se (sensing of force/volition), Si (sensing of comfort/experience)** — perceiving, sensory.
- **Te (logic of facts/efficiency), Ti (logic of structure/systems)** — judging, logical.
- **Fe (ethics of emotions/expression), Fi (ethics of relations/bonds)** — judging, ethical.
(Socionics says "logic/ethics" and "intuition/sensing" where MBTI says thinking/feeling and intuition/sensing; the mappings are *not* one-to-one — a socionics type is not simply its MBTI-letter twin.)

*Model A — the eight functional positions,* in four two-slot blocks:
- **Ego block (conscious, strong, valued):** 1 **Base/Leading** (the confident core), 2 **Creative** (the flexible tool).
- **Super-Ego block (conscious, weak, unvalued):** 3 **Role** (a clumsy public performance), 4 **Vulnerable / Point of Least Resistance (PoLR)** (the painful blind spot, avoided).
- **Super-Id block (unconscious, weak, valued):** 5 **Suggestive / Dual-seeking** (where one craves help), 6 **Mobilizing / Activating** (energized by encouragement).
- **Id block (unconscious, strong, unvalued):** 7 **Ignoring** (capable but disregarded), 8 **Demonstrative** (effortless, unshown-off).

The type *is* the assignment of the eight elements to these eight positions; e.g., the type **ILE** (≈ ENTp) has Ne base, Ti creative, and so on. Positions carry the attributes **strong/weak**, **valued/unvalued**, **bold/cautious**, **mental/vital** — the grammar that generates the intertype predictions.

*Quadras* — the four groups sharing the same four **valued** elements (the "home" information climate a person is comfortable in):
- **Alpha** (Ne, Si, Ti, Fe): ideas, comfort, playful theory, warm sociability.
- **Beta** (Se, Ni, Ti, Fe): power, vision, ideology, intense group emotion.
- **Gamma** (Se, Ni, Te, Fi): pragmatism, results, loyalty, hard realism.
- **Delta** (Ne, Si, Te, Fi): competence, personal warmth, quiet improvement.
Quadras give a *cultural-affinity* layer: members share values and communicate frictionlessly.

*Reinin dichotomies* — **fifteen** binary traits total: the four Jungian (extraversion, intuition, logic, irrationality) plus **eleven derived** by Reinin (e.g., *static/dynamic, process/result, positivist/negativist, questim/declatim, tactical/strategic, aristocratic/democratic*). They slice the sixteen types along non-obvious lines that cut across quadras.

*Intertype relations* — the payoff: sixteen named relations between types, derived from how their Model A structures interlock. Key ones: **Duality** (each type's ideal complement — your dual fills your Super-Id, the most comfortable/complete pairing), **Activity**, **Mirror**, **Identity**, **Semi-duality**, **Mirage**, **Kindred**, **Business**, **Super-Ego**, **Conflict** (the maximally friction-generating pair — your PoLR is their strength paraded), **Supervision** (an asymmetric one-up/one-down "benefactor/supervisee" dynamic), and **Benefit** (another asymmetric ring). These are Socionics' unique deliverable: a **compatibility map**, not just individual portraits.

**Vivid exemplars.** Because Socionics types don't cleanly equal MBTI ones, the strongest exemplars are *relational*: a **Conflict** pair — two people who each keep hammering exactly the other's rawest blind spot, a marriage that cannot stop bruising — is a ready-made antagonistic relationship. A **Supervision** ring — one partner perpetually, unintentionally auditing the other's weakest function, the "why do I always feel judged by them" dynamic — is a superb one-sided tension. A **Duality** pair — two people whose weaknesses and strengths mesh so cleanly they feel "at home" instantly — is the archetype of the effortless complementary bond (mentor-and-ward, the odd-couple who just *work*).

**What it gives you for character depth.** Socionics is the only mainstream typology that treats **relationships as first-class** and *derives* them from structure — a huge asset for a project whose drama is interpersonal (`docs/relationships.md`). Instead of "these two clash," it tells you *which cognitive slot the clash hits and why it's asymmetric*. The **valued/unvalued** and **strong/weak** distinction is subtler than MBTI's flat stack: a person can be *good at* something they don't *value* (Id block) — a rich source of characters who are competent yet contemptuous of their own gift. Quadras give a fast **faction/culture** climate. The PoLR (Vulnerable function) is a precise, non-arbitrary **wound-and-defense** location.

**Combines-with.** A parallel elaboration of **Jungian functions** and a sibling of **MBTI/Keirsey** (with a *deliberately different* function-attitude mapping — never assume ENTJ = the socionics type spelled ENTj). Its **intertype relations** are the natural companion to the repo's relationship model — translate Conflict/Supervision/Duality into trust/affinity/respect edge-profiles. Quadra values map onto **Schwartz** (Beta≈power/security/tradition-ideology; Gamma≈achievement/self-direction realism; Delta≈benevolence/competence; Alpha≈stimulation/hedonism/universalism-lite). The PoLR maps onto a **drives-schema** wound + `defense` pattern.

**Cautions.** Even **less empirically validated** than MBTI — Socionics is essentially unknown to mainstream psychometrics and has no accepted instrument; the intertype-relation predictions are theory, not tested findings. Typing methods disagree wildly between schools (there are competing Socionics traditions with different typings of the same person). The MBTI↔Socionics conversion is a notorious trap (the "j/p switch for introverts" debate). Use it as a **generator of relational structure** — its real gift — not as a claim about real compatibility. Never present its predictions as science.

**Sources.** Aušra Augustinavičiūtė, collected works (Socionics founding papers, 1980s). Antoni Kępiński, *Melancholia* / information-metabolism concept. Wikisocion (community reference for Model A, quadras, Reinin dichotomies, intertype relations). Reinin, *Socionics: Typology, Reinin Dichotomies* (2009 Eng. ed.).

## DISC — the Behavioral Four-Quadrant (an adjacent, applied categorical)

**Name and lineage.** Rooted in **William Moulton Marston**'s *Emotions of Normal People* (1928) — the same psychologist who helped invent the polygraph and created Wonder Woman. Marston theorized emotions along two axes; **Walter Clarke** (1940s) and later **John Geier** turned it into the assessment now sold as DISC. Ubiquitous in corporate training and sales; essentially absent from academic personality psychology.

**Core idea.** Behavior in a given environment sorts on two axes — **how one perceives the environment** (favorable/antagonistic) × **how one responds** (active/passive) — yielding four styles. Marston's original terms were Dominance, Inducement, Submission, Compliance; the modern rebrand is **D-I-S-C**.

**Structure in full.**
- **D — Dominance** (active + task-focused): direct, decisive, results-driven, competitive, blunt. (≈ choleric; high assertiveness, low Agreeableness.)
- **I — Influence** (active + people-focused): outgoing, enthusiastic, persuasive, optimistic, disorganized. (≈ sanguine; high Extraversion.)
- **S — Steadiness** (passive + people-focused): patient, dependable, cooperative, calm, change-averse. (≈ phlegmatic; high Agreeableness, low arousal.)
- **C — Conscientiousness / Compliance** (passive + task-focused): precise, analytical, careful, rule-following, reserved. (≈ melancholic; high Conscientiousness + caution.)
Most people are a *blend* (a primary + secondary, e.g., "DI" or "SC"), plotted as a point in the quadrant space — again the dimensional truth showing through the categorical surface.

**Vivid exemplars.** D: the hard-charging boss who wants the bottom line now. I: the magnetic salesperson who talks the room into it. S: the steady deputy everyone trusts to hold the line. C: the meticulous auditor who finds the one error. A classic buddy-pairing (blunt D + warm I, or driving D + cautious C) is a stock partnership dynamic.

**What it gives you for character depth.** The simplest possible *interaction-style* handle — how a character *comes across in a room and under conflict* — which is exactly the register scene-writing needs. Its explicit **active/passive × task/people** grid is a clean, memorable two-axis map you can place any character on in seconds, and its blend logic (primary + secondary) models the common "reads as X but really Y under pressure" beat.

**Combines-with.** The direct modern heir of the **four classical temperaments** (D≈choleric, I≈sanguine, S≈phlegmatic, C≈melancholic) and a near-isomorph of the **Interpersonal Circumplex** (agency × communion) — mention that circumplex when you want the *dimensional* version. Maps onto HEXACO Extraversion (active) and Agreeableness (people-focus). A fast surface layer to lay over any deeper type.

**Cautions.** Marston's two-axis theory was never validated; commercial DISC instruments vary in quality and are sold, not peer-reviewed. It measures *situational behavioral style*, not stable personality — the same person "DISCs" differently at work and home, which vendors sometimes obscure. Coarse (four buckets) and Barnum-prone. Fine as an interaction-surface sketch; not a character's core.

**Sources.** Marston, *Emotions of Normal People* (1928). Clarke Vocational Test / later DISC manuals. (Note the near-total absence of independent peer-reviewed validation as itself the honest citation.)

## The Enneagram — the Nine Types, in Full

**Name and lineage.** A nine-pointed typology of *core motivation*. The modern system is a 20th-century synthesis: the nine-pointed figure and cosmology come from **G. I. Gurdjieff** (via the Fourth Way, 1910s–30s, who did *not* attach personality types to it); the **nine personality types** were mapped onto the figure by **Oscar Ichazo** (Arica school, 1950s–70s, drawing on the "passions"/vices tradition); psychologized and popularized by psychiatrist **Claudio Naranjo** (1970s, adding modern clinical description); and elaborated in the dominant Anglophone lineage by **Don Richard Riso and Russ Hudson** (Enneagram Institute — the Levels of Development, refined type descriptions, RHETI test). Other lineages: Helen Palmer / David Daniels (Narrative Tradition), Beatrice Chestnut & Uranio Paes (instinctual subtypes), Katherine & David Fauvre (tritype). Its ancient-mystical *branding* is largely a 20th-century construction — take the pedigree with salt while valuing the system.

**Core idea.** Unlike the Jungian family (which types *cognition*), the Enneagram types **core motivation** — a **basic fear**, a **basic desire**, and a habitual **passion** (chief emotional vice) that each type organizes a whole personality around. Nine types sit on a figure whose **connecting lines** encode how each type *shifts* under stress and growth, so the Enneagram is intrinsically **dynamic** — a person is a *point in motion*, not a fixed box. This motivational, dynamic, health-graded character is exactly why it is the richest of these systems for *drama*.

**Structure in full.**

### The nine types
Each: *Basic Fear → Basic Desire · Passion (vice) → Virtue · characteristic defense.*

- **Type 1 — the Reformer / Perfectionist.** Fear: being corrupt, defective, bad. Desire: to be good, have integrity, be right. Passion: **anger/resentment** (repressed into a critical, correcting stance). Virtue: **serenity**. The principled, self-controlled idealist with an inner critic; can become rigid, judgmental, self-righteous.
- **Type 2 — the Helper / Giver.** Fear: being unwanted, unworthy of love. Desire: to be loved. Passion: **pride** (in one's indispensability; denial of own needs). Virtue: **humility**. The warm, giving, people-pleasing caretaker; can become intrusive, possessive, martyred.
- **Type 3 — the Achiever / Performer.** Fear: being worthless without accomplishment. Desire: to be valuable, admired. Passion: **deceit/vanity** (becoming the image of success). Virtue: **authenticity/truthfulness**. The driven, adaptive, image-conscious striver; can become empty, calculating, self-promoting.
- **Type 4 — the Individualist / Romantic.** Fear: having no identity or significance. Desire: to be uniquely themselves. Passion: **envy** (of what others have that would complete them). Virtue: **equanimity/emotional balance**. The sensitive, expressive, self-aware romantic; can become moody, self-absorbed, tormented by longing.
- **Type 5 — the Investigator / Observer.** Fear: being helpless, incapable, overwhelmed, depleted. Desire: to be competent, capable, self-sufficient. Passion: **avarice** (hoarding energy, resources, privacy). Virtue: **non-attachment**. The perceptive, private, cerebral analyst; can become withholding, isolated, detached.
- **Type 6 — the Loyalist / Skeptic.** Fear: being without support or guidance; unable to survive alone. Desire: security, support, certainty. Passion: **fear/anxiety** (itself). Virtue: **courage**. The committed, questioning, vigilant troubleshooter — appears as *phobic* (fleeing danger) or **counterphobic** (charging it). Can become anxious, suspicious, reactive.
- **Type 7 — the Enthusiast / Epicure.** Fear: being deprived, trapped in pain. Desire: to be satisfied, content, free. Passion: **gluttony** (for experiences, options, stimulation). Virtue: **sobriety**. The spontaneous, versatile, upbeat adventurer; can become scattered, escapist, unable to sit with pain.
- **Type 8 — the Challenger / Protector.** Fear: being controlled, harmed, violated. Desire: to protect oneself, be in control of one's own life. Passion: **lust/excess** (intensity, force). Virtue: **innocence**. The powerful, decisive, protective force; can become domineering, confrontational, ruthless.
- **Type 9 — the Peacemaker / Mediator.** Fear: loss, separation, fragmentation, conflict. Desire: inner and outer peace, wholeness. Passion: **sloth** (self-forgetting; inertia of the soul, not the body). Virtue: **right action/engagement**. The receptive, reassuring, harmonizing mediator; can become complacent, self-erasing, stubbornly checked-out.

### The three Centers / Triads
Each type belongs to a **center of intelligence** with a dominant emotion it is organized around:
- **Body / Gut / Instinctive center — 8, 9, 1.** Core emotion **anger**; core concern **autonomy, control, resistance, boundaries**. (8 acts anger out, 1 represses it, 9 falls asleep to it.)
- **Heart / Feeling / Image center — 2, 3, 4.** Core emotion **shame**; core concern **identity, image, worth in others' eyes**. (2 focuses on others' image of them, 3 on a successful image, 4 on a unique/deficient self-image.)
- **Head / Thinking / Fear center — 5, 6, 7.** Core emotion **fear/anxiety**; core concern **security, support, certainty**. (5 withdraws to conserve, 6 seeks/tests external support, 7 flees fear forward into stimulation.)

### The Hornevian and Harmonic groups (social + coping styles)
Two orthogonal triads add texture (Hornevian from Karen Horney's "moving toward/against/away"):
- **Hornevian (social stance):** **Assertive/aggressive** (moving against) — 3, 7, 8; **Compliant/dutiful** (moving toward a code/others) — 1, 2, 6; **Withdrawn** (moving away) — 4, 5, 9.
- **Harmonic (how they cope with loss/conflict):** **Positive-outlook** (reframe, avoid negativity) — 2, 7, 9; **Competency** (stay objective, set feeling aside) — 1, 3, 5; **Reactive/emotional** (need an emotional response first) — 4, 6, 8.

### Wings
Each type is flavored by one (or both) of its **adjacent** types — its **wing**, written `4w3` or `4w5`. The wing shades the core: a **9w1** is more principled and orderly; a **9w8** more forceful and grounded. Some people are balanced ("**9w1** and **9w8**"); one wing usually dominates. Wings are the first move from nine buckets toward individuation.

### Integration and Disintegration (the arrows — the dynamic core)
Every type is connected by lines to two others, giving a **direction of growth (integration)** and a **direction of stress (disintegration)**. Under security/health a type takes on the *healthy* qualities of its integration point; under stress it takes on the *unhealthy* qualities of its disintegration point.
- The **hexad** (types 1-4-2-8-5-7): **disintegration** follows **1→4→2→8→5→7→1**; **integration** runs the reverse **1→7→5→8→2→4→1**.
- The **inner triangle** (3-6-9): **disintegration** **3→9→6→3**; **integration** **3→6→9→3**.
Worked reads: a stressed **One** (methodical, controlled) slides to unhealthy **Four** — moody, irrational, self-pitying; a growing One moves to healthy **Seven** — spontaneous, joyful, self-accepting. A stressed **Two** becomes aggressive and resentful like an unhealthy **Eight**; a growing Two becomes creative and self-nurturing like a healthy **Four**. This is a *built-in arc mechanism*: the arrows tell you *exactly* how a given character decompensates and how they grow.

### The Instinctual Subtypes (27 variants)
Cross the nine types with the three **instinctual drives** (which biological instinct dominates), and each type splits three ways — **27 subtypes**:
- **Self-Preservation (sp):** focus on safety, health, resources, physical comfort, home.
- **Social (so):** focus on group, belonging, status, contribution, reading the collective.
- **Sexual / One-to-One (sx):** focus on intensity, chemistry, the charged one-on-one bond, attraction.
The dominant instinct + the type's passion produce a specific flavor — e.g., **sp/6** (warm, security-building), **so/6** (dutiful, ideology-following), **sx/6** (counterphobic, strength-testing). One subtype per type is a **countertype** — it seems to *contradict* the type's passion (e.g., the sx/1 who expresses anger openly against type-1's usual repression), a superb source of "doesn't look like their type" characters. The subtype is often *more* behaviorally decisive than the core type.

### Tritype (Fauvre)
An extension (contested, popular): a person has a dominant type in **each** of the three centers (Gut, Heart, Head), producing an ordered **tritype** like **5-1-2** or **8-2-7** — 27 tritype architectures. It captures that most people show more than one type's logic; the lead type dominates, the other two color it. Useful as an individuation dial; treat as enthusiast extension, not core doctrine.

### The Levels of Development (Riso-Hudson — the health continuum)
The Enneagram's other great gift: **nine Levels** within *every* type, a vertical continuum from liberation to pathology, so the *same type* looks radically different at different health. Three bands of three:
- **Healthy (Levels 1–3):** 1 = liberation (the type's virtue realized, ego relaxed); 2 = the psychological capacity/gift; 3 = the social/constructive expression.
- **Average (Levels 4–6):** 4 = the type's characteristic imbalance begins (the ego-agenda ramps); 5 = interpersonal control/over-compensation; 6 = overreach, the type's classic dysfunction in full (the "bad reputation" behaviors).
- **Unhealthy (Levels 7–9):** 7 = violation/desperation; 8 = delusion and compulsion; 9 = pathological destruction (of self or others).
A healthy Eight (Level 2) is a magnanimous protector who empowers others; an unhealthy Eight (Level 8) is a ruthless tyrant destroying anyone who threatens control — *same type, opposite person*. The Levels are what let the Enneagram model a **character's moral trajectory over a novel** — the arc *is* movement along the Levels (and along the arrows), not a change of type.

**Vivid exemplars.** 1: Atticus Finch; Inspector Javert (unhealthy, rigid justice). 2: Molly Weasley; a smothering, self-sacrificing mother. 3: Jay Gatsby (the manufactured image); Don Draper. 4: Hamlet; the tormented, envious artist; Severus Snape's romantic longing. 5: Sherlock Holmes; a reclusive scholar; Gandalf's withdrawn knowledge. 6: Samwise (loyal, phobic); a paranoid, testing lieutenant (counterphobic). 7: a manic adventurer fleeing grief; Peter Pan; a charming escapist. 8: Tony Soprano; a warlord-protector; a fierce matriarch who shields her clan. 9: a conflict-avoidant mediator who erases himself to keep the peace; the sleepy everyman roused to right action.

**What it gives you for character depth.** More than any system here, the Enneagram is *built for fiction*: it types **motivation** (the basic fear/desire — precisely the drives-schema layer), it is **dynamic** (the arrows give a character a native stress-collapse and growth path), and it is **graded** (the Levels give a moral trajectory). A single Enneagram spec — *type + wing + instinct + level + direction-of-current-stress* — conjures a character with a fear, a desire, a vice, a defense, a way they decompensate, and a way they could grow. That is a nearly complete character seed. The countertype and tritype dials pre-load "not what you'd expect." It is the categorical system that translates most directly into the repo's **drives** and **worth** layers.

**Combines-with.** The Enneagram's basic-fear/basic-desire is almost literally the repo **drives schema** (`docs/drives-schema.md`): the *basic fear* → a `fear` with its `protects`/`wound`/`defense`; the *basic desire* → a terminal `goal`'s `serves`; the *passion* → a Panksepp-primary bias (1's anger = elevated RAGE routed to control; 6's fear = elevated FEAR baseline; 7's gluttony = SEEKING/PLAY overdrive). The **arrows** are a ready model of the "out-of-character tail" and the arc engine — disintegration = which primary/behavior erupts under load; integration = the growth beat. The **Levels** map onto the health/health-band a character occupies and onto HEXACO Honesty-Humility + Emotional stability (low Levels ≈ the low-H, dysregulated corner). Centers map to the Panksepp cores (Gut≈RAGE, Heart≈PANIC/GRIEF-shame, Head≈FEAR). Type passions map onto Schwartz value conflicts. It is the tightest bridge between the categorical well and the repo's dimensional engine — which is why it is the one to reach for first when you want a *motivated, arc-ready* character fast.

**Cautions.** Psychometrically the Enneagram is **weakly validated** — the RHETI and similar tests show mixed reliability, the nine-type structure has not been robustly confirmed by factor analysis, and the mystical/ancient origin story is largely a 20th-century fabrication (Gurdjieff→Ichazo→Naranjo is the real, recent chain). It is prone to **confirmation bias and Barnum reading**, and to *mistyping by behavior* (two types can act alike from opposite motivations — the whole point is motivation, which is invisible, so typing is inherently uncertain). Cliché traps: the "angry Eight," the "sad Four," the "helpful Two" as mascots. **Use its structure — motivation, arrows, Levels — as a generative scaffold and arc engine; do not present it as validated science or store the type as data.** Its storytelling utility is genuinely high *precisely because* it types the invisible thing (motive) that drama runs on — but that same invisibility is why it fails as measurement. Hold both truths.

**Sources.** Riso & Hudson, *Personality Types* (rev. 1996) and *The Wisdom of the Enneagram* (1999) — Levels, arrows, type descriptions. Claudio Naranjo, *Character and Neurosis* (1994). Helen Palmer, *The Enneagram* (1988). Beatrice Chestnut, *The Complete Enneagram* (2013) — instinctual subtypes. The Enneagram Institute (enneagraminstitute.com) — Levels of Development, movement directions. For critique: Newgent et al., "The Riso-Hudson Enneagram Type Indicator: estimates of reliability and validity," *Measurement and Evaluation in Counseling and Development* (2004).

## Empirically-Derived Personality Types — where "types" survive the data

**Name and lineage.** Every system above sorts by theory, tradition, or mysticism. The honest question the validity sections keep raising: does *any* categorical structure fall out of personality data itself, rather than being imposed on it? Two lines of research say a qualified yes — and, tellingly, **neither reproduces the popular types.** The first is **Jack Block**'s ego-resiliency / ego-control tradition (Q-sort, mid-century), cluster-replicated by **Caspi, Robins, Asendorpf, van Aken** and others (1990s–2000s). The second is the big-data reanalysis by **Gerlach, Farb, Revelle & Amaral** (*Nature Human Behaviour*, 2018).

**Core idea.** Instead of positing kinds and building a test to sort people into them, let the *distribution of trait scores* speak: run cluster analysis (or density-peak detection) on large Big Five / HEXACO datasets and see whether people actually pile up into recurring regions. Where they do, you have a **data-driven type** — a named region of the dimensional space, not a category prior to it.

**Structure in full.**

*The Resilient / Overcontrolled / Undercontrolled typology (RUO, sometimes "ARC").* Three types recur across samples, ages, sexes, and cultures:
- **Resilient:** low Neuroticism, above-average on the other four. Well-adjusted, adaptable, ego-resilient — copes flexibly.
- **Overcontrolled:** high Neuroticism, low Extraversion. Inhibited, anxious, brittle; over-regulates impulse. Breaks **inward** (internalizing — withdrawal, depression, self-blame).
- **Undercontrolled:** low Agreeableness, low Conscientiousness. Impulsive, rule-breaking, poor delay of gratification. Breaks **outward** (externalizing — aggression, acting-out).
Longitudinal work ties the childhood forms (well-adjusted / inhibited / undercontrolled — cf. Kagan's inhibited-vs-uninhibited temperament) to adult outcomes. This is the type set with the *best* empirical pedigree — yet note it is just the Big Five re-described as three clusters, and replication is imperfect (some studies recover two, some four; the number is not settled).

*The Gerlach four clusters (2018).* A density-based reanalysis of **>1.5 million** Big Five responses found four peaks in the trait cloud:
- **Average:** near-mean on all five — the modal person; slightly high N and A. The most common type.
- **Reserved:** emotionally stable, low Openness, moderately agreeable/conscientious, introverted. Steady, unadventurous.
- **Role Model:** low Neuroticism, high on the other four — mature, dependable, prosocial. Increases with age; more common in women and older adults.
- **Self-Centered:** high Extraversion with low Openness, Agreeableness, and Conscientiousness — the disagreeable extravert. Over-represented among young men; people tend to *age out* of it toward Role Model.
The age/sex drift is itself a characterization gift (a cast of twenty-year-old men skews Self-Centered; the same men at fifty drift toward Role Model or Reserved). A **2019 commentary** in the same journal disputed the robustness — the "peaks" may be artifacts of the method rather than real discontinuities — so hold the four loosely.

**Vivid exemplars.** Resilient: the steady protagonist who bends without breaking under mounting pressure. Overcontrolled: the tightly-wound perfectionist who implodes rather than explodes (Overcontrolled ≈ the internalizing melancholic under load). Undercontrolled: the charming delinquent, the impulsive who acts out and burns bridges. Self-Centered (Gerlach): the brash young operator all appetite and no reflection; Role Model: the trusted elder the ensemble leans on.

**What it gives you for character depth.** These are the *counter-example* to the whole file, and their gift is honesty plus one sharp handle. Honesty: they prove *some* categorical structure is real — but that it is coarse (three or four kinds, not sixteen or nine), lives inside the dimensional space the repo already stores, and is contested even so. The sharp handle is RUO's **internalizing/externalizing** axis — a research-backed answer to *how a given character breaks under load*: inward collapse (Overcontrolled) vs outward acting-out (Undercontrolled), which is exactly the fork the decision engine dramatizes when a primary erupts. For generativity they are *weaker* than the Enneagram (no motive, no arrow, no arc) — but where you want a defensible, non-folk temperament label, these are the ones that survive peer review.

**Combines-with.** They *are* HEXACO/Big Five regions (`docs/trait-theory.md`), which is precisely what this file asks you to translate every folk type *into* — so they are the empirical vindication of the house move (**store dimensions; treat types as named regions**). Overcontrolled = high-Emotionality/low-eXtraversion corner; Undercontrolled = low-Conscientiousness/low-Agreeableness (and low-H) corner; Resilient/Role-Model = the stable, high-functioning corner. The internalizing/externalizing split maps onto *which* Panksepp primary breaks through under stress (FEAR/PANIC-GRIEF turned inward vs RAGE/SEEKING turned outward) — a direct feed to the `docs/generative-model.md` collision. Kagan's inhibited/uninhibited infant temperament is the developmental root (`developmental-and-psychodynamic.md`).

**Cautions.** "Data-driven" is **not** "proven natural kinds." Clustering a smooth multivariate cloud *always* returns clusters; whether they mark real discontinuities (as opposed to convenient cuts through a continuum) is exactly what the 2019 rebuttal contested. Treat even these as regions of convenience, not essences. The load-bearing honest headline: **the popular systems do not appear when you let the data speak** — no sixteen MBTI kinds, no nine Enneagram points, emerge from factor or cluster analysis. What emerges is a handful of blurry lumps in the Big Five space. That absence is the strongest single statement this file can make about categorical validity.

**Sources.** Robins, John, Caspi, Moffitt & Stouthamer-Loeber, "Resilient, overcontrolled, and undercontrolled boys: three replicable personality types," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* (1996). Asendorpf, Borkenau, Ostendorf & van Aken, cross-sample replication, *European Journal of Personality* (2001). Block, *Personality as an Affect-Processing System* (2002). Gerlach, Farb, Revelle & Amaral, "A robust data-driven approach identifies four personality types across four large data sets," *Nature Human Behaviour* (2018); and the critical reply (same journal, 2019) questioning robustness.

## How to reach into this file

- **Conjure, then translate — always.** Reach for a typology when a *type-word* is the fastest way to summon a coherent soul ("a stressed Enneagram 6 counterphobic," "an inferior-Fe-gripped INTJ," "a Conflict-relation pair"). Then immediately dissolve the label into the repo's dimensional store: HEXACO leanings, Schwartz/MFT weights, Panksepp baselines, drives. The type is scaffolding and shorthand; the **dimensions are what the decision engine resolves** (`docs/generative-model.md`, `docs/decision-engine.md`). Never persist a bare type-label as if it were data — that re-imports every validity problem above.
- **Match the system to the job.** Need a *tempo/mood* in one word → four temperaments or DISC. Need a *cognitive style and its collapse* → Jungian functions / Beebe. Need a *casting vocabulary* a collaborator shares → MBTI/Keirsey. Need *relational structure* between characters → Socionics intertype relations. Need a *motivated, arc-ready* character with a native stress-path and moral trajectory → the **Enneagram** (type + wing + instinct + level + arrows). For an ensemble, spread types deliberately so the cast doesn't think alike.
- **Mine the dynamics, not just the boxes.** The load-bearing gifts here are the *motions*: the Jungian inferior-function grip, the Enneagram disintegration arrow and Levels, Socionics' Conflict/Supervision dynamics, the temperament blend. These are arc engines and out-of-character-tail generators — feed them to `docs/trait-theory.md`'s variability and `docs/drives-schema.md`'s opposability.
- **Stay honest about the science.** Almost every system in this file is, to varying degrees, *psychometrically weak* — MBTI's non-bimodal dichotomies and poor retest reliability; the Enneagram's unconfirmed factor structure and invented pedigree; Socionics' and DISC's near-absence from peer review; function theory's unmeasurability. The **one exception** is the empirically-derived types (RUO, Gerlach's four) — and even those are just blurry regions of the Big Five space, contested as natural kinds. Tellingly, *no* popular categorical system (sixteen types, nine points) emerges when you let personality data cluster itself; what emerges is a handful of lumps in the dimensional cloud. So the folk systems' value is **generative and communicative**, not evidentiary. When a character or narrator *believes* in a typology, that belief is characterization, not the repo endorsing it. For measurement, the repo defaults (HEXACO et al.) are the backbone; the typologies are the palette laid over it — and the defensible types are literally named regions of that backbone.
