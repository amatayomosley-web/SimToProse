# Developmental and Psychodynamic Depth

Where a character *comes from*, and the hidden machinery running under the traits. The dimensional models (`trait-models.md`), the value menus (`motivation-and-values.md`), and the affective basis (`docs/generative-model.md`) tell you *what* a person is like now and *what* they weight; they are near-silent on *why this person became this shape* and on the sub-surface architecture — the buried wound, the split-off self, the defense that fires before thought, the developmental crisis that got fumbled at age four and is still being re-fought at forty. This file is the well of frameworks that supply that: Freud's structural machine and the full defense catalog, Vaillant's maturity ladder, Erikson's eight life-crises, Jung's depth (shadow, persona, anima/animus, complex, individuation), Adler's inferiority-and-style-of-life, Horney's three interpersonal moves and the tyranny of the shoulds, attachment theory's internal working models, object relations' internal cast of introjected others, plus Kohut's self, Berne's life-scripts, and Loevinger's ego-development ladder. These are the twentieth century's great *depth* traditions — clinical, interpretive, mostly non-quantified, sometimes unfalsifiable, and precisely for that reason the richest source of *interiority, provenance, and self-deception* a writer has. The repo already carries a compact psychodynamic default without naming it as such: the **wound → fear → defense** chain in `docs/drives-schema.md` (a formative injury generates a standing avoidance which drives a protective behavior — `withdraw | control | attack | please | perform`), the **three views** self-deception engine in `docs/self-and-perception.md` (ground-truth vs self-image vs others-see; a real fear absent from the self-image *is* self-deception), and the **coping / locus orientation** (approach↔avoid, express↔suppress, internal↔external). Those five defense verbs, that wound-provenance, and that gap between truth and self-image are this whole tradition in miniature. This file is where they come from and how much further they go. A caution that governs everything below: these are *interpretive* frameworks, not measured ones — reach for them for depth, provenance, and the shape of an inner conflict, never as validated fact about real psychology.

## Freud — the structural model (id, ego, superego)

**Lineage.** Sigmund Freud, Vienna, the second (1920s) topography — *The Ego and the Id* (1923) — superseding his earlier conscious/preconscious/unconscious "topographic" model. The founding text of all depth psychology; every framework in this file is either a development of Freud or a revolt against him.

**Core idea.** The mind is not a unity but a *contest of agencies* with different aims, mostly out of awareness. A person's behavior is the vector-sum of an appetitive engine that wants discharge *now*, a moral tribunal that forbids, and a harried executive negotiating between them and reality. Conflict is the normal condition of mind, not a malfunction — and the conflict is largely unconscious, which is why people act against their own stated interests and cannot say why.

**Structure in full.**
- **Id** — the reservoir of drives (libido/Eros, and later the death drive/Thanatos), wholly unconscious, ruled by the **pleasure principle**: it wants what it wants, immediately, and knows nothing of contradiction, time, negation, or reality. "A cauldron of seething excitations." The infant is nearly pure id.
- **Ego** — the executive, developed out of the id by contact with the world, ruled by the **reality principle**: it delays, plans, tests reality, and mediates among id, superego, and the external world. Largely conscious but with unconscious portions (the defenses run here, unseen). The ego is "a rider trying to hold in check the superior strength of the horse."
- **Superego** — the internalized authority, formed by introjecting the parents (the resolution of the Oedipus complex), split into two: the **conscience** (the prohibitions — what earns punishment/guilt) and the **ego-ideal** (the aspirations — what earns love/pride, and whose failure yields shame). It is often *harsher* than the real parents ever were.
- **Anxiety** — the ego's alarm. **Reality anxiety** (real external threat), **neurotic anxiety** (fear the id will break loose), **moral anxiety** (fear of the superego — guilt/shame). Anxiety is the signal that mobilizes the defenses.

**Vivid exemplars.** The id unbound: Mr. Hyde, Gollum reaching for the Ring, a toddler mid-tantrum, Shakespeare's Falstaff as pure appetite. The superego tyrannical: Javert (the Law internalized past all mercy), Angelo in *Measure for Measure*, the punishing internal voice in a scrupulous depressive. The ego overwhelmed and mediating: Hamlet paralyzed between the id's murderous drive and the superego's dread; Raskolnikov after the murder, ego crushed between the deed and the conscience. The ego-ideal as tyrant: Black Swan's Nina, destroyed by the gap between her real self and the perfect self she must be.

**What it gives you for character depth.** The originating insight that a person is *divided against themselves* and mostly doesn't know it — the source of every "why did I do that?" and every action that betrays a stated intention. It gives you the three-way internal negotiation to stage as inner conflict, and it names the two flavors of the moral lash: **guilt** (I did a bad thing — conscience) versus **shame** (I *am* a bad thing — failed ego-ideal), which drive different behavior (repair vs hide).

**Combines-with.** Maps cleanly onto the repo spine: the **id** is the raw Panksepp primaries (`docs/generative-model.md` — LUST, RAGE, SEEKING pushing for discharge); the **superego** is the internalized values/Moral Foundations weighting plus self-image (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`, Layer 6); the **ego** is the narrative resolver, the LLM weighing the collision — the id-vs-superego contest *is* the decision engine's drive-vs-value collision, in older vocabulary. Guilt/shame split maps to conscience/ego-ideal. The defenses (next section) run in the ego. Kohlberg's conventional morality (`moral-character.md`) is superego content.

**Cautions.** (1) The energetics (libido as a hydraulic quantity that must discharge) are dead science — treat the *structure* (the three-way conflict) as a useful map, the *hydraulics* as period metaphor. (2) The whole edifice is essentially unfalsifiable; do not present it as fact about brains. (3) Deeply culture-bound (Viennese bourgeois patriarchy). (4) Still the most productive single metaphor for staging inner division — keep the tripartite conflict, discard the certainty.

**Sources.** Freud, *The Ego and the Id* (1923); *New Introductory Lectures* (1933, Lecture XXXI); *Inhibitions, Symptoms and Anxiety* (1926). Overview: Mitchell & Black, *Freud and Beyond* (1995).

## Freud — the psychosexual stages

**Lineage.** Freud, *Three Essays on the Theory of Sexuality* (1905). The first developmental-stage theory in psychology; Erikson's eight stages are its direct, socially-reframed descendant.

**Core idea.** Libidinal energy settles on a different bodily zone at each phase of childhood, and each phase poses a conflict; too much or too little gratification **fixates** part of the libido there, leaving an adult "character type" stamped with that phase's concerns. The past is *carried in the character structure*, not merely remembered.

**Structure in full.**
- **Oral** (0–~18 mo) — zone: mouth (sucking, biting). Conflict: weaning; dependence and trust. Fixation → the **oral character**: dependency, neediness, "oral" habits (smoking, overeating, biting sarcasm), gullibility (swallowing anything) or its opposite, cynical "biting" hostility.
- **Anal** (~1.5–3 yr) — zone: bowel/bladder control. Conflict: toilet training; control, autonomy, holding vs letting go. Fixation → **anal-retentive** (orderly, stingy, obstinate, punctual — the "anal triad" of orderliness, parsimony, obstinacy) or **anal-expulsive** (messy, defiant, cruel).
- **Phallic** (~3–6 yr) — zone: genitals. Conflict: the **Oedipus complex** (desire for the opposite-sex parent, rivalry with and fear of the same-sex parent — castration anxiety in boys; a penis-envy variant in girls, much disputed). Resolution: identification with the same-sex parent → the superego is born. Fixation → vanity, recklessness, or the reverse, plus disordered authority and rivalry patterns. (The popular term "**Electra complex**" for the girl's version is *Jung's* coinage, 1913 — Freud explicitly *rejected* it, insisting the two sexes' developments are not symmetrical and preferring "the feminine Oedipus attitude." Attributing "Electra complex" to Freud is a common error.)
- **Latency** (~6–puberty) — libido dormant; energy goes to school, skills, same-sex friendship. (Erikson's Industry maps here.)
- **Genital** (puberty onward) — mature sexuality directed outward toward others; the achievement of the well-resolved prior stages: capacity to love and to work.

**Vivid exemplars.** Oral: any needy, clinging, or bottle-attached character — Tennessee Williams' fragile dependents; the sardonic wit whose humor "bites." Anal-retentive: Felix Unger, Hercule Poirot's fastidiousness, Dickens' Scrooge (parsimony + obstinacy), any control-obsessed miser. Phallic/Oedipal: Hamlet (the canonical psychoanalytic reading — the paralysis as Oedipal), Norman Bates (the unresolved mother-bond made monstrous), Sons and Lovers' Paul Morel.

**What it gives you for character depth.** A *developmental etiology* for a present trait — a way to answer "where did this fastidiousness / this neediness / this authority-problem come from?" with a childhood origin. Even stripped of the sexual theory, the **character-type descriptions** (oral-dependent, anal-controlling) are vivid, recognizable adult silhouettes, and the principle — *the unresolved childhood conflict is re-enacted in the adult structure* — is load-bearing craft.

**Combines-with.** The Oedipal resolution *creates* the superego (structural model above). The anal character ≈ high Conscientiousness/Orderliness with low warmth (`trait-models.md`). Erikson's stages are these re-cast as *psychosocial* crises (see below) — Trust≈Oral, Autonomy≈Anal, Initiative≈Phallic, Industry≈Latency. The "fixation as re-enacted conflict" is the drives-schema **wound → defense** pattern given a developmental clock.

**Cautions.** (1) The most scientifically discredited part of Freud — no evidence libido localizes to zones, penis-envy is rightly abandoned, the timings are arbitrary. (2) Use the **character types** as evocative shorthand and the **re-enactment principle** as craft; do not present the mechanism as real. (3) The Oedipal reading of Hamlet is a *reading*, not a fact about the play — flag it as one lens.

**Sources.** Freud, *Three Essays on the Theory of Sexuality* (1905); *Character and Anal Erotism* (1908). Critical overview: Fisher & Greenberg, *The Scientific Credibility of Freud's Theories* (1996).

## The defense mechanisms — the full catalog, ranked by maturity (Vaillant)

**Lineage.** Sigmund Freud named several; **Anna Freud** systematized them in *The Ego and the Mechanisms of Defence* (1936); **George Vaillant** (Harvard, *Adaptation to Life* 1977; the Grant Study) ordered them into a four-level **maturity hierarchy** validated against life outcomes, and they entered the DSM as the "Defensive Functioning Scale." This is the single most useful psychodynamic tool for a character writer: it is the mechanism *behind* the repo's five defense verbs (`withdraw | control | attack | please | perform`).

**Core idea.** A defense is an *unconscious* mental operation the ego runs to keep unbearable feeling, impulse, or knowledge out of awareness. Everyone uses them; what differs is *which* ones and *how rigidly*. Vaillant's finding: the defenses form a **hierarchy of maturity** — the same conflict can be handled by a defense that distorts reality grossly or by one that channels it adaptively, and which a person habitually reaches for predicts their whole life course. Maturity is not the *absence* of defense but the *grade* of defense. This is a **character-depth gold mine**: a person's signature defense is a compact, showable fingerprint of their inner life, and their movement *up* the ladder under a good relationship (or *down* under stress) is a ready-made arc.

**Structure in full — the catalog, by level.**

**Level I — Psychotic** (reality grossly rewritten; normal only in dreams, early childhood, and psychosis):
- **Delusional projection** — frank persecutory delusion: "the neighbors are transmitting into my teeth."
- **Denial (psychotic)** — flat refusal of external reality: insisting a dead loved one is merely asleep, for weeks.
- **Distortion** — reshaping reality to inner need: grandiose delusion, hallucinated wish-fulfillment.

**Level II — Immature** (common in adolescents, personality disorders, and everyone under enough stress; they externalize and burden others):
- **Projection** — attributing one's own unacceptable feeling to another: "*You're* angry at *me*," said by the one who is furious. The engine of paranoia, jealousy, prejudice.
- **Fantasy (autistic/schizoid)** — retreat into daydream to gratify what reality denies: the isolate who lives in an elaborate inner world instead of seeking real contact.
- **Acting out** — discharging an impulse directly in behavior to avoid feeling it: the grieving teenager who picks a fight rather than cry; self-harm, tantrum, reckless sex after a loss.
- **Passive aggression** — turning aggression against the self or expressing it obliquely: the "forgotten" favor, the compliant sabotage, chronic lateness, the sulk.
- **Hypochondriasis / somatization** — converting reproach or conflict into bodily complaint: the person whose every unmet need becomes an ache.
- **Dissociation** — abrupt disconnection of feeling, identity, or memory to escape distress: going numb and "watching from the ceiling"; the fugue, the blank.
- **Splitting** — (Kernberg's borderline-level; see object relations) collapsing a person into all-good or all-bad, flipping between idealization and devaluation with no middle. "You were perfect; now you're a monster" — same person, same week.
- **Projective identification** — projecting a feeling into another *and* unconsciously provoking them to actually feel/enact it: making the calm therapist feel the rage the patient can't own.
- **Idealization / devaluation** — inflating or deflating another to manage anxiety about them.
- **Identification with the aggressor** — (Anna Freud's signature contribution) mastering terror of a threatening figure by *becoming* them — adopting the abuser's aggression, values, or gestures. The bullied child who turns bully; the hazed pledge who hazes the next class; the captive who takes the captor's side (the mechanism under "Stockholm-syndrome" dynamics and the intergenerational transmission of abuse). Ordinary **identification** (modeling oneself on an admired other) is a normal developmental engine and, in mature form, healthy; the *aggressor* variant is the defensive, trauma-driven case. (Its inward-directed cousin, **introjection** — swallowing another whole into the self — is treated under object relations.)

**Level III — Neurotic** (common in everyone; distorts inner life more than outer reality, burdens the self not others):
- **Repression** — the master defense: pushing an unbearable idea/wish out of consciousness (the forgotten appointment with the person you dread; the "can't remember" of the trauma).
- **Reaction formation** — turning a forbidden impulse into its conscious opposite, held with suspicious zeal: the crusader against a vice he secretly harbors; smothering solicitude masking hostility.
- **Displacement** — redirecting affect from its true (dangerous) target to a safe one: kicking the dog after the boss humiliates you; the scapegoat.
- **Intellectualization** — handling emotional threat as an abstract problem, feeling stripped out: the doctor discussing a grim prognosis in cool jargon; the philosopher who theorizes his heartbreak.
- **Isolation of affect** — recounting a devastating event flatly, as if reading a bus schedule; the fact retained, the feeling severed.
- **Rationalization** — inventing a plausible, creditable reason for what was really driven by an unacceptable one: "I didn't want the job anyway" (the fox and the sour grapes).
- **Undoing** — a symbolic act to cancel an unacceptable thought/deed: the compulsive ritual, checking, the apology-spree.
- **Regression** — retreating to an earlier developmental mode under stress: the adult who sulks, curls up, becomes helpless and childlike when overwhelmed.
- **Compartmentalization** — walling off incompatible attitudes, roles, or whole selves so they never meet and the contradiction never has to be felt: the tender family man who is also a ruthless executioner at work and never lets the two rooms connect; the churchgoer whose weekday cruelties stay sealed off from Sunday.
- **Repression's cousin, denial (neurotic)** — minimizing/looking away from a painful but not-psychotic truth: "the drinking isn't really a problem."

**Level IV — Mature** (adaptive; integrate reality, feeling, and others' needs — the marks of Vaillant's flourishing subjects):
- **Sublimation** — channeling a forbidden or painful impulse into a socially valued achievement: aggression → surgery or sport; grief → art; the wound → the vocation.
- **Altruism** — meeting one's own need vicariously through constructive service to others: the survivor who founds the charity.
- **Suppression** — the *conscious*, deliberate decision to postpone attending to an impulse or feeling ("I'll deal with this after the funeral") — the one defense that is willed; distinct from repression, which is unconscious.
- **Anticipation** — realistic planning for future discomfort, pre-feeling it in manageable doses: rehearsing the hard conversation, grieving before the death.
- **Humor** — expressing feeling without individual discomfort or unpleasant effect on others; the gallows joke that holds terror at a survivable distance. Vaillant's favorite: "one of the truly elegant defenses."

**Vivid exemplars.** Signature-defense characters: Don Draper (repression + compartmentalization, whole selves walled off); Dexter (intellectualization + sublimation of a murderous drive into a code); Tony Soprano (displacement — the ducks, the rage redirected; acting out); Miss Havisham (denial and fantasy, the stopped clocks); Holden Caulfield (projection — everyone else is a "phony"); Basil Fawlty (displacement, passive aggression); the war hero who tells the atrocity flat (isolation of affect); Beth's "I'm fine" through catastrophe (suppression, or repression if unconscious); Chandler Bing (humor as the shield). An *arc up the ladder*: Good Will Hunting's Will moving from acting-out and projection toward mature contact.

**What it gives you for character depth.** A person's *habitual* defense is the most economical single handle on their inner life — showable in one gesture (the joke at the funeral, the sudden numbness, the redirected rage) and diagnostic of everything beneath. Vaillant's hierarchy gives you a **maturity axis orthogonal to the traits** (a high-Conscientiousness person can be anywhere on it) and a **built-in arc engine**: growth = climbing the ladder (acting-out → sublimation) under a healing relationship; breakdown = sliding down it under load (a normally-mature adult regressing to projection and denial in a crisis). The level also tells you *who pays* — immature defenses burden others (the reader feels it), neurotic ones burden the self, mature ones cost the character but spare everyone.

**Combines-with.** This *is* the mechanism under `docs/drives-schema.md`'s five defense verbs: **withdraw** ≈ repression/dissociation/schizoid fantasy; **control** ≈ intellectualization/isolation/reaction formation/undoing; **attack** ≈ projection/displacement/acting out/passive aggression; **please** ≈ reaction formation/altruism/idealization; **perform** ≈ the false-self presentation (Winnicott, Kohut) atop denial. The whole catalog is the operational face of the **three views** (`docs/self-and-perception.md`): every defense is a *device for keeping a truth out of the self-image* — the exact `truth ≠ self` gap the repo calls self-deception. Coping orientation (`suppress↔express`, `avoid↔approach`) is the two-dimensional summary of which defenses fire. Horney's three moves (below) are defenses raised to whole-personality strategies. Vaillant's maturity ≈ ego-development height (Loevinger) and secure attachment.

**Cautions.** (1) A defense is by definition *unconscious* — if the character knows they're doing it, it's suppression or a strategy, not repression; keep the machinery below the character's own awareness or you lose the whole point (it belongs in the ground-truth view, not the self-image view). (2) Cliché trap: not every quiet person is "repressed," not every joker is "deflecting" — over-diagnosis flattens. (3) The maturity ranking carries a value judgment (Vaillant's WASP-flourishing ideal) — a "mature" defense in one culture reads differently in another. (4) Don't stack every defense on one character; a real person has a *signature* one or two, plus regressions under stress.

**Sources.** A. Freud, *The Ego and the Mechanisms of Defence* (1936); Vaillant, *Adaptation to Life* (1977) and *The Wisdom of the Ego* (1993); *DSM-IV* Defensive Functioning Scale (Appendix B); Cramer, *Protecting the Self* (2006).

## Erikson — the eight psychosocial stages

**Lineage.** Erik Erikson (Freud-trained, but ego-and-society oriented), *Childhood and Society* (1950), *Identity: Youth and Crisis* (1968). Reframes Freud's psychosexual stages as *psychosocial* crises spanning the whole lifespan, not just childhood — the first true life-span developmental theory, and the origin of the word "identity crisis."

**Core idea.** The ego develops through eight sequential **crises**, each a tension between a syntonic (healthy) and dystonic (unhealthy) pole, precipitated by the widening social world's new demands. Each crisis, well enough resolved, yields a **basic virtue** (a lasting ego strength); badly resolved, a **maldevelopment**. Crucially the resolutions are *not* permanent — an earlier crisis can be reopened by later life, and later strengths need the earlier ones as foundation. A person is legible as *where they are in the sequence* and *which earlier crisis is still unhealed and bleeding into the present*.

**Structure in full** (stage — crisis — age — virtue — core question):
1. **Trust vs. Mistrust** — infancy (0–1½) — virtue **Hope** — *Is the world safe? Can I rely on others?* The bedrock; failure → suspicion, withdrawal, a baseline that others will not come.
2. **Autonomy vs. Shame & Doubt** — toddler (1½–3) — virtue **Will** — *Can I do things myself? Am I my own person?* Failure → chronic self-doubt, shame, over-control or its collapse.
3. **Initiative vs. Guilt** — preschool (3–5) — virtue **Purpose** — *Is it okay for me to act, to want, to lead?* Failure → guilt over one's own drives, fear of asserting.
4. **Industry vs. Inferiority** — school age (5–12) — virtue **Competence** — *Can I make it in the world of skills and tasks? Am I good enough?* Failure → the sense of inadequacy, "I am not capable."
5. **Identity vs. Role Confusion** — adolescence (12–18) — virtue **Fidelity** — *Who am I? What do I believe and belong to?* Failure → the identity crisis, drifting, foreclosed or diffuse selfhood.
6. **Intimacy vs. Isolation** — young adulthood (18–40) — virtue **Love** — *Can I give myself to another and stay myself?* Requires a settled identity first; failure → isolation, guardedness, serial shallow bonds.
7. **Generativity vs. Stagnation** — middle adulthood (40–65) — virtue **Care** — *Have I made something that outlasts me — children, work, mentees?* Failure → stagnation, self-absorption, the midlife hollowness.
8. **Ego Integrity vs. Despair** — late adulthood (65+) — virtue **Wisdom** — *Was my life whole, was it mine, can I accept it?* Failure → despair, bitterness, terror of death and of a life un-lived.

(James Marcia later subdivided stage 5 into four **identity statuses** — Achievement, Moratorium, Foreclosure, Diffusion — by crossing *exploration* with *commitment*; useful for staging a young character's search.)

**Vivid exemplars.** Trust broken at the root: Miss Havisham, a Dickensian orphan, the traumatized child who flinches. Identity vs. role confusion: nearly every coming-of-age protagonist — Stephen Dedalus, Holden Caulfield, Lady Bird, Simba's "who am I" exile. Intimacy vs. isolation: the commitment-phobe who cannot let anyone close (many a rom-com and noir lead). Generativity vs. stagnation: the midlife crisis whole genre — Lester Burnham in *American Beauty*, Walter White's monstrous bid for a legacy that outlasts him. Integrity vs. despair: King Lear on the heath reckoning with a squandered reign; the deathbed lookings-back of *Ikiru*, *The Bucket List*, Ebenezer Scrooge shown his end.

**What it gives you for character depth.** A *life-span timeline* — the single best tool for placing a character by age and for locating the **unhealed earlier crisis** driving present trouble (the forty-year-old still fighting stage-2 autonomy against every authority; the leader whose stage-4 inferiority secretly powers all his striving). It gives midlife and old-age characters real developmental content (most trait models are silent past 30). And each stage's *virtue* is a nameable arc destination — a story can be "the earning of Fidelity" or "the failure of Generativity."

**Combines-with.** Maps to Freud's psychosexual stages (1–4 ≈ oral/anal/phallic/latency) but extends them across life. The unhealed-earlier-crisis is `docs/drives-schema.md`'s **wound** with a developmental address (a stage-1 rupture = an attachment wound → the "being abandoned" fear). Identity (stage 5) is Layer 6 self-image/self-narrative (`docs/self-and-perception.md`); Generativity feeds Schwartz benevolence/universalism and the drive to legacy (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`, the "son as legacy" loss-frame). The virtues (Hope, Will, Purpose, Competence, Fidelity, Love, Care, Wisdom) overlap the VIA character strengths (`moral-character.md`). Attachment security (below) is essentially a good stage-1 resolution.

**Cautions.** (1) The ages and the strict sequence are idealized and culture-bound (Western, mid-century, individualist; the intimacy-requires-prior-identity ordering has been challenged, notably for women — Gilligan). (2) It's a rich *narrative* scaffold, not a measured mechanism — no instrument cleanly validates the eight stages. (3) Cliché trap: "he's just having a midlife crisis" flattens stage 7 into a punchline; use the *specific* crisis content, not the label. (4) The virtues can go saccharine — give each one a cost and a shadow.

**Sources.** Erikson, *Childhood and Society* (1950), *Identity: Youth and Crisis* (1968), *The Life Cycle Completed* (1982); Marcia, "Development and validation of ego-identity status" (*JPSP*, 1966).

## Jung — analytical psychology (shadow, persona, anima/animus, complex, individuation)

**Lineage.** Carl Gustav Jung, Zürich; broke from Freud c. 1913 over the primacy of sexuality. *Psychological Types* (1921), *Two Essays on Analytical Psychology*, the *Collected Works*. The deepest single well of *archetypal* character material and the source most metabolized by story theory (Campbell's monomyth, the Jungian "archetype" screenwriting books all descend from here). Jung's *types* (introversion/extraversion + the functions) live in `type-typologies.md`; this section is his *depth* psychology.

**Core idea.** The psyche is self-regulating and reaches, over a lifetime, toward **wholeness** — the integration of its conscious and unconscious parts into a realized Self. Beneath the personal unconscious lies a **collective unconscious**: inherited, universal patterns (**archetypes**) that structure how humans everywhere image mother, hero, trickster, death, the divine. Psychological growth (**individuation**) is the arduous work of making the unconscious conscious — meeting the disowned parts (shadow), seeing through the mask (persona), integrating the contrasexual soul-image (anima/animus), and dissolving the autonomous **complexes** that hijack the ego. This is, structurally, a whole *character arc* map.

**Structure in full.**
- **Ego** — the center of consciousness, the "I."
- **Persona** — the social mask, the face turned to the world, "a compromise between the individual and society about what one should appear to be." Necessary; pathological only when the person *becomes* the mask and loses the self behind it (the man who is nothing but his office).
- **Shadow** — everything the ego has disowned and repressed because it's incompatible with the persona/ideal: not only the "dark" (greed, rage, cruelty) but often *unlived positive* potential (the suppressed ambition, tenderness, or spontaneity). Encountered first in **projection** — we hate in others what we can't own in ourselves. Integrating the shadow (owning it *without* enacting it) is the first and hardest step of individuation; the un-integrated shadow runs the person from behind.
- **Anima / Animus** — the contrasexual inner image: the **anima** the unconscious feminine in a man, the **animus** the unconscious masculine in a woman (Jung's own gendered framing; read today as the disowned "other" mode of being in anyone). Projected onto love-objects (the beloved carries your own unlived opposite), it drives fascination and disappointment; integrated, it becomes the inner bridge to the unconscious and to relatedness.
- **The Self** — the archetype of wholeness and the true center of the *total* psyche (conscious + unconscious), toward which individuation moves; imaged as mandala, wise old man/woman, divine child, the union of opposites.
- **Complex** — an emotionally charged cluster of images/memories around an archetypal core (the mother complex, the power complex, the inferiority complex — Jung coined the term), *autonomous*: it can seize the ego, and "everybody knows nowadays that people 'have complexes'; what is not so well known… is that complexes can have us." The complex is the operational unit — a buried, feeling-toned tangle that fires and takes over.
- **Archetypes** (the collective forms): the Great Mother, the Wise Old Man, the Hero, the Trickster, the Child, the Maiden, the Wounded Healer, Death/Rebirth — universal casting types the unconscious keeps re-imaging.
- **Individuation** — the lifelong integrative journey (Jung placed its main work in the *second half* of life): withdraw the projections, meet the shadow, integrate the anima/animus, relativize the ego to the Self. Not perfection — *wholeness*.

**Vivid exemplars.** Persona/shadow split: Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde (the disowned shadow made flesh); Dorian Gray (the portrait *is* the shadow); Walter White's "Heisenberg" (the shadow named and donned). Shadow projected: Ahab hunting the whale that carries his own rage; every Jungian villain who is the hero's disowned half (Luke/Vader — "I am your father"). Anima projection: Dante's Beatrice, the *femme fatale* of noir (the man's anima made deadly), Scottie's remaking of Madeleine in *Vertigo*. The complex having the person: Miss Havisham owned by a betrayal-complex; Norman Bates by the mother-complex. Individuation as arc: Luke Skywalker, Simba, the whole Campbell monomyth; Prospero relinquishing power for wholeness.

**What it gives you for character depth.** The richest **arc and antagonist** engine in this file: the shadow gives you the antagonist-as-disowned-self (the villain who is what the hero won't admit they could be), the persona/self gap gives you the hollow-mask character and the arc of dropping it, the anima/animus gives you the depth-charge under obsessive love, and individuation gives you a whole life-shaped transformation. The **complex** is a precise unit — a charged tangle that autonomously fires and hijacks behavior, exactly the sub-agent that acts before the ego can. And the archetypes are a casting palette of universal roles.

**Combines-with.** The **shadow** is the sum of what the defenses (above) keep out of the self-image — Jung's shadow ≈ the repo's `truth` view minus the `self` view (`docs/self-and-perception.md`); shadow-projection *is* the defense "projection" raised to a life-pattern. The **complex** ≈ `docs/drives-schema.md`'s wound+fear+defense cluster, autonomous and trigger-fired — a Jungian complex is a bundle of charged memory that the relevance gate surfaces and that seizes the decision. Individuation is a Layer-6 arc (`character-anatomy.md`). Jung's *types* (extra/introversion, the four functions) feed MBTI/Keirsey (`type-typologies.md`). Archetypes feed Campbell/monomyth (`worldbuilding-frameworks`, `dramatic-structure`).

**Cautions.** (1) Almost entirely unfalsifiable and mystical at the edges (synchronicity, the collective unconscious as literal inheritance) — use as *symbolic/narrative* apparatus, never as science. (2) The anima/animus is built on essentialist gender assumptions; modernize it as "the disowned mode" rather than "the inner woman/man." (3) Archetypes slide into cliché the instant they're used lazily — the *individuated* Wise Old Man is a person; the un-individuated one is a fortune-cookie. (4) Jungian language seduces; keep it earning its place with concrete behavior, not incense.

**Sources.** Jung, *Two Essays on Analytical Psychology* (CW 7), *Aion* (CW 9ii — the Self/shadow), *The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious* (CW 9i); *Man and His Symbols* (1964, accessible); Stevens, *Jung: A Very Short Introduction* (1994).

## Adler — individual psychology (inferiority, striving, style of life, social interest)

**Lineage.** Alfred Adler, Vienna; the first major defector from Freud's circle (1911). "Individual Psychology" — *individual* meaning *indivisible/whole*. Ancestor of humanistic psychology, cognitive therapy (the "fictional final goal" is a cognitive schema), family-systems birth-order work, and much pop-psychology of self-esteem.

**Core idea.** The master motive is not sex but the **striving from a felt minus to a felt plus** — from inferiority toward mastery, completeness, "superiority" (self-perfection, not domination). Each person, early in childhood, forges a unitary **style of life** — a self-created, largely unconscious strategy for that striving, organized around a **fictional final goal** — and everything they do coheres with it, *teleologically* (pulled by the imagined future, not pushed by the past). Health is measured by **social interest** (Gemeinschaftsgefühl): whether the striving is turned toward *contribution and community* or collapsed into self-serving superiority.

**Structure in full.**
- **Inferiority feelings** — universal and *motivating*; the felt gap that drives growth. Normal.
- **Inferiority complex** — the pathological version: the feeling becomes overwhelming, paralyzing, an excuse ("I can't, because…").
- **Superiority complex** — the *overcompensation*: masking deep inferiority with arrogance, contempt, conquest, boasting — grandiosity as a defense against smallness. (The braggart and the coward are the same person from two angles.)
- **Compensation / overcompensation** — turning a weakness into a strength (Demosthenes the stammerer become the great orator) or into an inflated counterfeit of one.
- **Style of life** — the unified, self-authored life-plan/personality laid down by ~age 5, the lens through which the person reads every situation; all behavior is consistent with it.
- **Fictional final goal (fictional finalism)** — the imagined, often unconscious ideal end-state ("to be safe," "to be admired," "to be the one who saves everyone") that *pulls* behavior teleologically; a "fiction" because it's a subjective construction, not an objective truth.
- **Social interest (Gemeinschaftsgefühl)** — the innate-but-must-be-cultivated feeling of connectedness to and concern for the human community; Adler's *criterion of mental health*: the useful (contributing) side of life vs the useless (self-absorbed) side.
- **The three life tasks** — work, friendship/community, and love/intimacy; a life-style is tested by how it meets all three.
- **Birth order** (as *psychological position*, not literal rank): the **firstborn** (dethroned by the sibling, often conservative, authority-oriented); the **second/middle** (competitive, "runner-up," striving to overtake); the **youngest** (pampered or spurred, ambitious or dependent); the **only child** (adult-oriented, may struggle to share).
- **Safeguarding tendencies** — Adler's version of defenses: excuses, aggression (depreciation, accusation), and distance-seeking, all protecting the self-esteem and the life-style.

**Vivid exemplars.** Overcompensating superiority masking inferiority: Napoleon-as-archetype, the puffed-up small man; Miles Gloriosus; Michael Scott's desperate need to be loved and admired; Gatsby (the whole self reinvented to reach a fictional final goal — Daisy-as-completeness). Inferiority-driven striving turned useful: Rocky, the underdog who converts the minus to a plus; Hermione's over-achievement. The fictional final goal running a life: Ahab ("to master the thing that maimed me"), Jay Gatsby (the green light). Failed social interest: Scrooge before the ghosts (striving collapsed into self); Ebenezer's arc *is* the recovery of Gemeinschaftsgefühl. Birth-order textures: the dutiful eldest, the rebellious middle, the charming baby of the family in any large-family saga.

**What it gives you for character depth.** The cleanest **single-engine** account of a whole personality: find the childhood **inferiority**, the **fictional final goal** it spawned, and the **style of life** that pursues it, and the character's every choice coheres. It's *teleological* — behavior pulled by an imagined future — which gives you a character's *aim* rather than just their history, and a built-in flaw (the superiority-defense) and arc (the growth of social interest). Adler is the most *writer-friendly* depth theory: goal-driven, unified, hopeful, and it explains the braggart, the underdog, and the tyrant with one mechanism.

**Combines-with.** The fictional final goal ≈ `docs/drives-schema.md`'s terminal **goal** + self-image ("I am the one who…"); the inferiority ≈ the **wound**; the superiority complex ≈ the **defense** (`perform`/`attack`/`control`) that guards it — Adler is drives-schema with a developmental origin story. Social interest maps to Schwartz self-transcendence (benevolence/universalism) and Moral Foundations care (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`) — the "useful side of life" is the self-transcendent pole. Striving-for-superiority ≈ McClelland's need for achievement/power and Schwartz achievement/power (`motivation-and-values.md`). The style of life ≈ the integrated Model (Layer 10). Complements Horney (below) — her three "moves" are three failed styles of life.

**Cautions.** (1) Birth-order effects are the weakest, most-debunked part — large studies find little consistent personality effect of literal birth order; keep Adler's *psychological position* (how the child construed their place) as a character lens, not birth rank as a predictor. (2) "Superiority" is easily misread as mere dominance — it means self-completion; keep the nuance. (3) The single-master-motive is a simplification (as Freud's was); use it as a *unifying* device, not a total theory. (4) The teleology (behavior pulled by a fiction) is exactly the useful part for a writer — but it's philosophy, not measured mechanism.

**Sources.** Adler, *The Neurotic Constitution* (1912), *Understanding Human Nature* (1927), *What Life Should Mean to You* (1931); Ansbacher & Ansbacher (eds.), *The Individual Psychology of Alfred Adler* (1956).

## Horney — the three interpersonal moves and the tyranny of the shoulds

**Lineage.** Karen Horney (pronounced "horn-eye"), German-American psychoanalyst; broke with Freudian orthodoxy over penis-envy and the death drive, replacing biology with *culture and relationship*. *The Neurotic Personality of Our Time* (1937), *Our Inner Conflicts* (1945), *Neurosis and Human Growth* (1950). A founder of the "culturalist" and feminist revisions of psychoanalysis.

**Core idea.** Neurosis grows not from thwarted libido but from **basic anxiety** — the child's feeling of being "isolated and helpless in a potentially hostile world," bred by real deficits of warmth and safety. To cope, the child adopts one of three interpersonal **strategies** toward people, over-relies on it, and builds a whole personality around it; the strategies *conflict* (you can't move toward, against, and away at once), producing the neurotic's inner war. Meanwhile the person, unable to accept the despised real self, invents an **idealized self** and then *tyrannizes* the real self with its impossible demands — the "shoulds."

**Structure in full.**
- **Basic anxiety** — the foundational sense of helplessness/isolation in a hostile world; the wound all three strategies defend against.
- **The three moves** (each a way to make safety out of people; the healthy person uses all three flexibly, the neurotic is *driven* into one):
  - **Moving toward people — Compliance / the self-effacing solution.** Safety through love, approval, dependence, submission. "If I give in / am good / am needed, I won't be hurt." Becomes the doormat, the clinger, the one who *must* be liked, who cannot be angry, whose virtue is a shield. (→ repo `please`.)
  - **Moving against people — Aggression / the expansive solution.** Safety through mastery, power, exploitation, winning. "If I have power / am the strongest, no one can hurt me." Becomes the dominator, the competitor, the one who must control and prevail, for whom the world is a jungle and life a fight. (→ repo `attack`/`control`.)
  - **Moving away from people — Detachment / the resigned solution.** Safety through self-sufficiency, distance, withdrawal. "If I need no one, no one can hurt me." Becomes the aloof, the onlooker, the one who prizes privacy and freedom above all and feels crowded by intimacy. (→ repo `withdraw`.)
- **The idealized self / idealized image** — the glorified, impossible self the person constructs to escape the felt inadequacy of the real self.
- **The tyranny of the shoulds** — the idealized self's relentless inner demands ("I *should* be able to do everything, please everyone, never fail, never be afraid"), producing self-hatred when (inevitably) unmet. The gap between the shoulds and reality *is* the ongoing neurotic suffering.
- **The despised real self** — what the person actually is, held in contempt for failing the ideal; the self-hatred pole.
- **The real self** — the "central inner force common to all human beings" that *could* grow if freed from the pride system; the goal of therapy is its recovery ("self-realization").
- **Vicious circles** — each strategy generates the very conditions that intensify the anxiety it was meant to solve (the aggressive person's hostility makes the world more hostile).

**Vivid exemplars.** Moving toward (self-effacing): Fanny Price, Charlotte Brontë's meeker heroines, the co-dependent enabler, any "I just want everyone to be happy" martyr. Moving against (expansive): Miranda Priestly, Gordon Gekko, Tywin Lannister, Scarlett O'Hara's steely conquest, the boardroom shark. Moving away (resigned): Sherlock Holmes' detachment, Bartleby, the fortress-of-solitude loner, Meursault's affectless remove. The **tyranny of the shoulds** made visible: Black Swan's Nina destroyed by "I should be perfect"; the burned-out overachiever; the parent who "should" never feel resentment and hates themselves for feeling it; almost every impostor-syndrome protagonist.

**What it gives you for character depth.** The single best **interpersonal-strategy** typology for a character under stress: which of the three moves do they *reach for* when frightened, and what does over-relying on it cost? It directly names the repo's defense verbs and gives them a *relational* origin (basic anxiety) and an *inner-conflict* structure (the other two moves, suppressed, still pulling). The **shoulds** are the most usable model of *self-hatred and perfectionism* in this whole file — the gap between idealized and despised self is a ready inner monologue and a ready arc (learning to occupy the real self). And because the three moves *conflict*, Horney hands you a character at war with themselves.

**Combines-with.** The three moves *are* the repo's `please` / `attack`+`control` / `withdraw` defense verbs (`docs/drives-schema.md`) — Horney is their theoretical parent, so use her to give any repo defense a relational wound behind it. Basic anxiety ≈ the wound / insecure attachment (below). The idealized self vs despised self is a sharp form of the **three views** (`docs/self-and-perception.md`): the idealized self is a defensive *self*-view, the shoulds are its demands, the despised self is a truth the person won't hold — pure `self ≠ truth`. The shoulds ≈ a tyrannical superego/ego-ideal (Freud). The three solutions overlap the Enneagram's stances (3-6-9 Horneyic groupings are explicitly Horney-derived — `type-typologies.md`). Moving-against ≈ low Agreeableness + Dark-Triad; moving-toward ≈ high Agreeableness pushed to self-erasure (`trait-models.md`).

**Cautions.** (1) Like all analytic theory, clinically derived and unfalsifiable — use for structure, not as measured fact. (2) The three moves are a *dominant tendency*, not a box; a rounded character uses all three and is merely *pulled* toward one under threat — don't reduce a person to one arrow. (3) The "neurotic" framing is dated; read the moves as normal coping styles that turn costly at the extreme, not as pathology labels. (4) The shoulds are so recognizable they can turn into a therapy-speak cliché ("she has an inner critic") — render them as specific voiced demands, not a diagnosis.

**Sources.** Horney, *Our Inner Conflicts* (1945), *Neurosis and Human Growth* (1950), *The Neurotic Personality of Our Time* (1937); Paris, *Karen Horney: A Psychoanalyst's Search for Self-Understanding* (1994).

## Attachment theory — internal working models and adult attachment

**Lineage.** John Bowlby (British psychoanalyst + ethology, *Attachment and Loss* trilogy, 1969–80); Mary Ainsworth (the "Strange Situation," 1970s — the empirical engine); Mary Main (the Adult Attachment Interview + the *disorganized* category, 1980s–90s); Hazan & Shaver (romantic attachment, 1987); Bartholomew & Horowitz (the four-category adult model, 1991). Unusually for this file, attachment theory is *heavily empirically validated* — the most strongly measured framework here (Loevinger's ego-development test is the other with real psychometrics, but attachment's longitudinal and cross-cultural evidence base is deeper).

**Core idea.** Humans are born with an **attachment behavioral system** that seeks proximity to caregivers under threat. From the pattern of the caregiver's responsiveness the infant builds an **internal working model (IWM)** — a durable, largely unconscious set of expectations about *whether others will come when I need them* and *whether I am worthy of care* — and that model, carried forward, shapes how the person loves, trusts, fights, and grieves for the rest of their life. Attachment is the developmental root of the repo's "being abandoned" wound and of relational trust priors.

**Structure in full.**

*Infant patterns (Ainsworth's Strange Situation):*
- **Secure (B)** — caregiver reliably responsive; child explores freely, protests separation, is comforted on reunion. IWM: *others will come; I am worth coming for.* ~55–65%.
- **Anxious / ambivalent / resistant (C)** — caregiver inconsistent; child clingy, hard to soothe, angry-and-clingy on reunion, too preoccupied to explore. IWM: *love is unreliable; I must amplify distress to be seen.*
- **Avoidant (A)** — caregiver rejecting/unresponsive to need; child suppresses attachment behavior, appears indifferent, self-reliant beyond years, minimal reunion response (though physiologically stressed). IWM: *needs get rebuffed; rely on no one.*
- **Disorganized (D)** — (Main) caregiver is *both* the source of comfort and the source of fear (frightening or frightened caregiver, often trauma/abuse); child shows contradictory, freezing, or bizarre behavior — the approach-and-flee paradox with no coherent strategy. IWM: *the one I need is the one I fear.* Strongly linked to later dissociation and personality disturbance.

*Adult correlates (AAI states of mind / self-report styles):*
- **Secure / autonomous** — values attachment, recalls the past coherently and with balance (even hard childhoods, if *resolved*); comfortable with intimacy *and* autonomy. Positive model of self and other (Bartholomew).
- **Preoccupied / anxious** (≈ infant anxious) — enmeshed, still angrily entangled with attachment figures; high attachment-anxiety, craves closeness, fears abandonment, hyper-activates the system (protest, jealousy, clinging). Negative self / positive other.
- **Dismissing / avoidant** (≈ infant avoidant) — devalues attachment, idealizes or can't recall the past, compulsively self-reliant; deactivates the system (distance, contempt for "neediness"). Positive self / negative other.
- **Fearful-avoidant** (Bartholomew's fourth cell; ≈ disorganized) — *wants* intimacy but *dreads* it; high on both anxiety and avoidance; the come-here/go-away whipsaw. Negative self / negative other.
- **Unresolved / disorganized** (AAI) — lapses in reasoning around loss/trauma; the adult face of the D pattern.
- The two underlying **dimensions** (Brennan/Fraley): **attachment anxiety** (fear of abandonment, need for reassurance) × **attachment avoidance** (discomfort with closeness/dependence). The four styles are the four quadrants; secure = low-low.
- **Internal working model** — the carried-forward, self-fulfilling schema; can be updated (an "earned secure" adult reworks an insecure childhood through a corrective relationship or reflection — a ready arc).

**Vivid exemplars.** Anxious/preoccupied: Rose fixating on Jack; the protest-and-cling partner who texts sixteen times; Catherine's stormy fusion with Heathcliff. Dismissing/avoidant: Mr. Darcy's early armor, Sherlock, Elsa ("conceal, don't feel"), the self-sufficient loner who calls love a weakness, Don Draper's flight from every intimacy. Fearful-avoidant/disorganized: the trauma survivor who pulls a lover close then torches the relationship at the first real closeness; Catherine *and* Heathcliff's mutual devastation; many a noir *femme*/*homme fatale*. Secure/earned: Samwise (steady, comes when needed); the partner in a romance whose settledness lets the anxious lead heal. The IWM in action: any character whose present love repeats the parent's failure until they see it.

**What it gives you for character depth.** The most *validated* and most *relational* origin story in this file — a principled account of **how a character loves and fears losing**, rooted in caregiving history, expressed as showable behavior under threat (clings / withdraws / whipsaws). It supplies the repo's core relational wound ("being abandoned"), the trust priors on relationship edges (`docs/relationships.md`), and a clean **arc** (earned security via a corrective bond — the backbone of the healing-romance and found-family story). The anxiety×avoidance plane gives you two dials to place any character's love-style precisely, and the mismatch of two styles (anxious-avoidant "trap") is a ready-made relationship engine.

**Combines-with.** The IWM *is* the repo's foundational **wound → fear** (`docs/drives-schema.md`): insecure attachment → the "being abandoned"/"being controlled" fear → the `please`/`withdraw` defense. Attachment security ≈ Erikson stage 1 (Trust/Hope) well-resolved. The four styles map onto the interpersonal-circumplex and onto Horney's moves (anxious≈moving-toward, avoidant≈moving-away). Relationship trust/affinity edges (`docs/relationships.md`) are IWM-in-action; negativity bias in edge-updating is sharper for the anxious. Feeds directly into `emotion-and-decision` (attachment shapes appraisal of threat-to-bond) and the "son dies" loss-response divergence (`docs/values-and-stakes.md`) — attachment style governs grief shape (protest vs deactivation). Secure ≈ mature defenses (Vaillant), high Agreeableness stability.

**Cautions.** (1) Style is *relationship-specific and somewhat fluid*, not a fixed trait stamped for life — a person can be secure with one partner, anxious with another, and can *earn* security; don't write it as destiny. (2) Pop-attachment has become a horoscope ("he's an avoidant," "she's an anxious") — the categories are prototypes over two continuous dimensions, not boxes; prefer the dimensional read. (3) Cross-cultural variation is real (rates of avoidant/anxious differ by caregiving norms — don't universalize the Western distribution). (4) Adult self-report attachment and AAI attachment are *different measures* that don't perfectly agree — the science is more textured than the four-quadrant meme.

**Sources.** Bowlby, *Attachment and Loss* (1969–80); Ainsworth et al., *Patterns of Attachment* (1978); Main & Solomon on disorganization (1986/1990); Hazan & Shaver, "Romantic love conceptualized as an attachment process" (*JPSP*, 1987); Bartholomew & Horowitz (*JPSP*, 1991); Mikulincer & Shaver, *Attachment in Adulthood* (2016).

## Object relations — the internal cast of introjected others

**Lineage.** The British-and-American relational tradition: Melanie Klein (the founder, 1920s–50s), Ronald **Fairbairn** (Scotland — the radical reformulation), Donald **Winnicott** (the "good-enough mother," true/false self), Margaret **Mahler** (separation-individuation, US), Otto **Kernberg** (borderline/narcissistic organization, US). The great mid-century revolt against Freud's drive theory: the mind is built not to discharge drives but to *relate*.

**Core idea.** The fundamental human motive is not pleasure but the **object** (the other person) — we are object-seeking from birth. In relating to early caregivers the infant **introjects** them, building an *inner world populated by internal objects*: images of self and other (a rejecting mother, an exciting-then-withholding lover, a persecuting authority) that then govern adult relationships, which are largely *re-castings* of these internal dramas. A character's relational life is a stage on which their internalized figures keep being reassigned to new actors.

**Structure in full.**
- **Internal object** — a mental representation of another (and of the self-in-relation-to-them), formed by introjection; carries the affect of the original relationship. Adults "relate" as much to these inner objects as to real people.
- **Klein's two positions** (modes of experience, not stages — one can revisit them lifelong):
  - **Paranoid-schizoid position** — the earliest mode: unable to hold love and hate toward the *same* object, the infant **splits** the world into all-good and all-bad ("good breast"/"bad breast"), uses **projection**, **introjection**, and **projective identification**, and lives under persecutory anxiety (the bad object will retaliate). The mode we regress to under threat, and the organizing mode of borderline states.
  - **Depressive position** — the developmental *achievement*: recognizing that the good and bad objects are *one whole person*, holding love and hate toward the same other at once — which brings **guilt** (I hated the one I love), **grief**, and the wish for **reparation**. Ambivalence tolerated; the birth of concern.
- **Splitting** — the paranoid-schizoid defense: collapsing self/other into all-good or all-bad, flipping between idealization and devaluation. (Kernberg's signature of borderline organization.)
- **Projective identification** — projecting an unbearable part of the self *into* another and then unconsciously inducing them to actually *feel/enact* it (making the other carry your rage) — the most interpersonally powerful defense, and a whole model of how one person "poisons" or "recruits" another.
- **Fairbairn's structure** — libido is object-seeking, not pleasure-seeking; the child, needing the parent, *internalizes* the unsatisfying parent to control them inwardly and splits the internal object into the **exciting object** (the tantalizing, promising-but-withholding one) and the **rejecting object** (the depriving, hostile one), with corresponding split-off parts of the ego (the **libidinal ego** craving the exciting object, the **anti-libidinal ego / internal saboteur** attacking the need). Explains why people *cling to* bad objects — the tie to the depriving other is the only tie they have.
- **Winnicott** — the **good-enough mother** (perfection isn't needed, reliability is); the **holding environment**; the **transitional object** (the blanket/teddy that bridges me and not-me); the **true self** (spontaneous, alive) vs the **false self** (the compliant mask built to meet a mother who needed the child to caretake *her* — the character who performs a self and feels dead behind it).
- **Mahler's separation-individuation** — the toddler's psychological birth: from *symbiosis* through *differentiation, practicing, rapprochement* (the crisis of wanting autonomy *and* the mother), to *object constancy* (holding a stable inner image of the loved other even when absent or frustrating). Failure → the abandonment terror and unstable relationships of borderline organization.
- **Kernberg's levels of personality organization** — **neurotic** (integrated identity, mature defenses, intact reality-testing) / **borderline** (identity diffusion, splitting-based defenses, intact-but-fragile reality-testing — the level of borderline and narcissistic personalities) / **psychotic** (loss of reality-testing). A severity axis under any character.

**Vivid exemplars.** Splitting / paranoid-schizoid: the borderline lover who idealizes then annihilates ("you were my soulmate; you're dead to me") — many a *Fatal Attraction*, many a volatile parent; Gollum/Sméagol as literalized splitting. Clinging to the bad object (Fairbairn): the abused partner who cannot leave; Estella's pull to the men who wound; anyone who keeps re-choosing the parent who failed them. False self (Winnicott): Don Draper (a performed identity over a hollow), Norman Bates (mother introjected and enacted), the perfect child who caretook a fragile parent and lost themselves. Transitional object: Linus's blanket, Hobbes to Calvin, the keepsake a soldier can't part with. Rapprochement/object-constancy failure: the character who reads any partner's brief absence as abandonment. Reparation (depressive position): the arc of Scrooge, of anyone who moves from splitting others into good/bad toward seeing whole, forgivable people.

**What it gives you for character depth.** The richest model of the *inner relational world*: a character carries an internal cast (the introjected mother, the withholding father, the persecutor) and keeps *re-casting real people into those roles* — the deepest account of *transference* and of why people re-choose the partner who wounds them. **Splitting** gives you the all-good/all-bad perceiver and the volatile relationship engine; the **true/false self** gives you the hollow-performer and their arc toward aliveness; **clinging to the bad object** explains the self-defeating loyalty that trait/value models can't; the **depressive position** gives you the maturational arc from splitting to seeing-whole (= forgiveness, ambivalence, concern) — one of the deepest character arcs available.

**Combines-with.** Internal objects *are* the introjected figures behind `docs/relationships.md`'s belief-edges and behind the wound (`docs/drives-schema.md`) — the "rejecting object" is the wound's original cast member, re-projected onto new people (the relevance gate re-surfaces the old object when a new one matches). Splitting = the immature defense in Vaillant's catalog and the paranoid-schizoid mode. Projective identification is the deepest of the defense mechanisms. The **false self** = Winnicott's name for the repo `perform` defense and the defensive `self`-view (`docs/self-and-perception.md`). Object constancy failure ≈ disorganized/anxious attachment (above). Kernberg's organization-levels sit *under* the trait models as a severity/integration axis and align with Vaillant's maturity and Loevinger's ego development. Klein's depressive-position arc ≈ the moral maturation in `moral-character.md` and the integration goal of Jungian individuation.

**Cautions.** (1) The most speculative reconstructions of infancy in all of psychology (Klein's phantasy-life of the newborn is not observable and is widely doubted) — use the *adult* structures (splitting, internal objects, true/false self, clinging to bad objects) which are clinically vivid, and hold the infantile etiology lightly. (2) "Splitting" and "borderline" are heavily stigmatized clinical labels — deploy the *behavior* (idealize-then-devalue, unstable identity) without slapping a diagnosis on a character. (3) Jargon-dense; translate to concrete relational behavior or it turns to fog. (4) Kernberg's severity levels are a useful spine but not a licence to pathologize every difficult character.

**Sources.** Klein, *Envy and Gratitude and Other Works* (1946–63); Fairbairn, *Psychoanalytic Studies of the Personality* (1952); Winnicott, *The Maturational Processes and the Facilitating Environment* (1965); Mahler, Pine & Bergman, *The Psychological Birth of the Human Infant* (1975); Kernberg, *Borderline Conditions and Pathological Narcissism* (1975); overview: Greenberg & Mitchell, *Object Relations in Psychoanalytic Theory* (1983).

## Adjacent essentials

Three further frameworks earn a place: one completes the map of narcissism and self-esteem regulation, one is the most directly *writable* of all depth models, and one supplies the single best-*measured* maturity axis in the whole tradition.

### Kohut — self psychology (the narcissistic self and its selfobjects)

**Lineage.** Heinz Kohut, Chicago; *The Analysis of the Self* (1971), *The Restoration of the Self* (1977). The major American theory of narcissism and self-cohesion, and the origin of "empathic attunement."

**Core idea.** The organizing need is not drive-discharge but a **cohesive self**, built and maintained through relationships with **selfobjects** — others experienced not as separate people but as *functions that sustain one's own self* (a source of mirroring, of idealized strength, of kinship). When early selfobject needs go unmet, the self stays fragile and the adult keeps hungrily seeking those functions — the root of narcissistic character.

**Structure in full.** Three selfobject needs: **mirroring** (to be seen, admired, "the gleam in the mother's eye" — "look at me, be delighted by me"); **idealizing** (to merge with a calm, strong, admired other — "you are wonderful and I am part of you"); **twinship/alter-ego** (to feel essential likeness with others — "there are people like me"). A healthy self forms a **bipolar structure**: ambitions (from mirroring) at one pole, ideals (from idealizing) at the other, talents/skills arching between. Failures produce **fragmentation** (the self falling apart under slight), **narcissistic rage** (annihilating fury at a wound to self-esteem — the disproportionate reaction to being slighted), and lifelong hunger for mirroring (the grandiose, applause-dependent character) or for an idealized figure to merge with.

**Vivid exemplars.** The mirror-hungry narcissist: Gilderoy Lockhart, Bojack Horseman, most fading-star and influencer characters; Norma Desmond ("I *am* big"). Narcissistic rage: the tyrant who razes a kingdom over an insult; Amy Dunne's annihilating retaliation; Commodus. The fragile self behind the grandiosity: Walter White's wounded pride powering an empire; the bully revealed as hollow. Idealizing/twinship hunger: the disciple who merges with a guru; the fan who needs the idol to be perfect.

**What it gives you.** The best model of *self-esteem regulation and narcissism from the inside* — the narcissist not as villain but as a person with a fragile self hungry for mirroring, whose rage is a wound-response. It explains the disproportion of narcissistic injury (why a small slight detonates) and gives the grandiose character an ache underneath.

**Combines-with.** The mirror-hunger and fragile self underlie the Dark-Triad *narcissism* facet (`trait-models.md`) — Kohut supplies its interior. Narcissistic rage = a low-severity trigger producing high RAGE (`docs/generative-model.md`) via a self-image threat (`docs/values-and-stakes.md` — the self-image *is* the stake). Selfobject need ≈ an attachment/relatedness drive turned on self-cohesion. The false-self (Winnicott) and the mirror-hungry self are close kin.

**Cautions.** Clinically derived, unfalsifiable; "narcissist" is an over-flung pop label — use the *interior* (fragile self + mirror-hunger + injury-rage), not the epithet. Don't reduce every proud character to a wounded narcissist.

**Sources.** Kohut, *The Analysis of the Self* (1971), *How Does Analysis Cure?* (1984); Mitchell & Black, *Freud and Beyond* (1995, ch. 6).

### Berne — Transactional Analysis (ego-states and the life script)

**Lineage.** Eric Berne, *Games People Play* (1964), *What Do You Say After You Say Hello?* (1972); Claude Steiner (scripts). A popularized, unusually *operational* offshoot of psychoanalysis — the most writer-friendly, because it renders inner structure as *observable dialogue and repeated scenes*.

**Core idea.** At any moment a person operates from one of three **ego-states**, and much of life runs on a **script** — an unconscious life-plan "decided" in childhood in response to parental messages, which the person then spends adulthood *proving right*.

**Structure in full.** Three ego-states: **Parent** (introjected authority — Critical Parent / Nurturing Parent, the "taped" voices of caregivers), **Adult** (the here-and-now rational processor), **Child** (the felt/impulsive self — Free/Natural Child and Adapted Child). Transactions between people can be complementary, crossed, or **ulterior** (a hidden message under the surface one). **Games** = repetitive ulterior-transaction sequences with a predictable painful payoff ("Why Don't You / Yes But," "Now I've Got You," "Kick Me"). **Life script** = the childhood-authored life-story (with a payoff — the "script ending"), driven by parental **injunctions** ("Don't exist," "Don't be you," "Don't be close," "Don't succeed") and **counter-injunctions/drivers** ("Be perfect," "Please others," "Be strong," "Try hard," "Hurry up"). **Strokes** = units of recognition the person is organized to seek.

**Vivid exemplars.** Script in action: the character who "always ends up alone/betrayed/broke" and unconsciously engineers it (a Tennessee Williams fate); "Kick Me" players who provoke the rejection they dread. Ego-states: the parent whose Critical Parent voice is audibly their own mother's; the regressed adult dropping into Child under stress. Drivers: the "Be Perfect" overachiever, the "Please Others" people-pleaser, the "Be Strong" stoic who can't ask for help.

**What it gives you.** The most *directly stageable* depth model — ego-states are literally different *voices* you can write (the internal Critical Parent, the frightened Child), games are *repeatable scenes* with a built-in payoff, and the life script is a character's self-authored destiny they keep fulfilling (the "ghost"/wound rendered as a life-plan with an ending). The **drivers** are a crisp catalog of compulsive styles ready to voice.

**Combines-with.** Ego-states map onto Freud's structure (Parent≈superego, Adult≈ego, Child≈id) but are *hearable as dialogue* — ideal for `narrative-craft` interiority and free-indirect voice. The life-script *is* `docs/drives-schema.md`'s wound → self-narrative → repeated defensive behavior, with a decided *ending* (excellent for `arc-engine`/`dramatic-structure`). Injunctions/drivers ≈ Horney's shoulds and the tyrannical ego-ideal. Games = the interpersonal loop the relationship model (`docs/relationships.md`) keeps re-running.

**Cautions.** The most *pop* of these frameworks — light on validation, heavy on catchy labels; use it as a *craft* device (voiceable ego-states, repeatable games, the scripted ending), not as science. Its jargon dates badly; keep the mechanics, drop the 1960s packaging.

**Sources.** Berne, *Games People Play* (1964), *What Do You Say After You Say Hello?* (1972); Steiner, *Scripts People Live* (1974); Stewart & Joines, *TA Today* (1987).

### Loevinger — ego development (the ladder of meaning-making maturity)

**Lineage.** Jane Loevinger, Washington University in St. Louis; *Ego Development* (1976), built on the **Washington University Sentence Completion Test (WUSCT)** — a projective measure with, unusually for this file, decades of psychometric validation behind it. Descends from Sullivan/Adler's "the ego is the frame of reference the person imposes on experience"; a sibling to Kohlberg (moral) and Kegan (constructive-developmental) stage theories.

**Core idea.** The "ego" here is not Freud's executive but the person's **master frame of meaning-making** — the whole style by which they construe self, others, and rules — and it matures through an invariant sequence of **stages**, each a more differentiated, less egocentric, more complexity-tolerant way of holding the world. Where Vaillant's ladder ranks *defenses* and Kernberg's ranks *structural integration*, Loevinger's ranks *the sophistication of the meaning-frame itself*. Most adults plateau in the middle; the top stages are rare. A character's stage is a compact statement of *how much of reality's complexity they can hold at once* — and moving up one rung is a profound (and slow) arc.

**Structure in full** (the stages, low → high; classic names):
- **Impulsive** — the world is what I want/fear right now; others are sources of supply or threat; rules are external constraints ("you'll get in trouble"). The demanding, present-bound self (normal in early childhood; in an adult, a stunted frame).
- **Self-Protective** — the first guardedness: opportunistic, wary, blaming, rule-exploiting ("don't get caught"); control and advantage. The con, the grifter, the manipulative operator.
- **Conformist** — self = group; the good is what the in-group approves; difference is distrusted, feelings are clichés, rules are obeyed because they're *the* rules. The organization man, the tribe-loyalist.
- **Self-Aware** (conscientious-conformist) — the modal adult stage: the first inner life, an awareness of alternatives and exceptions, self-criticism ("usually, but it depends").
- **Conscientious** — internalized standards replace group approval; self-evaluated goals, responsibility, guilt over falling short of *one's own* code; sees others' motives and inner lives. The principled, self-directed adult.
- **Individualistic** — tolerance for one's own and others' paradox and dependence; awareness that the earlier drive for achievement can itself be a trap; heightened sense of individuality against role.
- **Autonomous** — holds conflict and ambiguity as inherent, not soluble; respects others' autonomy and their right to their own path; integrates warring needs. The wise, self-actualizing frame.
- **Integrated** — Loevinger's rare theoretical ceiling (≈ Maslow's self-actualization): identity fully consolidated, the reconciler of opposites.

**Vivid exemplars.** Self-Protective: Flashman, Becky Sharp, any charming opportunist who reads every rule for a loophole. Conformist: the company man who cannot conceive of the firm being wrong; Angela in early *The Office*. Conscientious: Atticus Finch, a principled whistle-blower acting against her own group's approval. Autonomous: the elder who holds two irreconcilable truths without flinching — a well-written mentor, Marilynne Robinson's Reverend Ames. An *arc up a rung*: a conformist protagonist forced by events into the lonely, self-authored stance of the conscientious stage (the whole "person who learns to think for themselves against the tribe" story).

**What it gives you.** A **meaning-making maturity axis orthogonal to the traits, the defenses, and the morals** — two equally kind, equally conscientious characters can inhabit radically different *frames* (one sees rules as sacred givens, the other as human constructs to be weighed), and that gap is a deep, quiet engine of conflict. It answers "how much complexity can this person hold?" and gives a rare, hard-won, slow arc (stage change, not mood change). Because it's measured, it's the most defensible "depth/maturity" ranking in this file.

**Combines-with.** Loevinger's height ≈ Vaillant's defense-maturity ≈ Kernberg's integration ≈ secure attachment ≈ Kegan's orders of consciousness — the same underlying "differentiation/integration" spine seen through different lenses; stack them for a converging maturity read. The stage is a Layer-10/Layer-6 property (the frame the Model reasons *within*), not a Layer-1 trait. Maps against Kohlberg's moral stages (`moral-character.md`) — Loevinger is the broader self-frame, Kohlberg the specifically-moral slice. The Conformist→Conscientious shift is the classic self-authorship arc (`arc-engine`).

**Cautions.** (1) The WUSCT is validated, but stage *assignment* is coder-dependent and the top stages are rarely observed — don't over-claim precision at the ceiling. (2) It carries an obvious progressive value-slope (higher = better); a lower-stage character is a *different frame*, not a lesser person, and can be the moral center of a story (the Conformist's loyalty, the Impulsive's aliveness). (3) Stage change is *slow and rare* — resist arcing a character up two rungs in one act; a single hard-won half-stage is more honest. (4) Distinct from Freud's "ego" — don't conflate the meaning-frame with the executive-among-agencies.

**Sources.** Loevinger, *Ego Development* (1976); Loevinger & Wessler, *Measuring Ego Development* (1970); Hy & Loevinger, *Measuring Ego Development*, 2nd ed. (1996); Cook-Greuter's later extension of the top stages (2000). Sibling: Kegan, *The Evolving Self* (1982).

## How to reach into this file

Start from *what the moment needs*, not from a favorite theory — and remember these are **interpretive depth tools**: use them for provenance, interiority, and the shape of an inner conflict, never as measured fact.

- **Giving a present trait a childhood origin ("where did this come from?")** → **Erikson** (which life-crisis went unhealed and is bleeding into now) or **Freud's psychosexual character-types** (the oral-dependent, the anal-controller) or **Adler** (the childhood inferiority + fictional final goal). All three answer the repo's **wound** provenance (`docs/drives-schema.md`) with a developmental address.
- **Writing how a character *loves* and fears losing** → **attachment theory** (secure / anxious / avoidant / disorganized; the anxiety×avoidance plane) — the most validated tool here, and the source of the "being abandoned" wound and the relationship trust-priors (`docs/relationships.md`). For the *inner cast* they re-project onto lovers, → **object relations** (internal objects, clinging to the bad object, the true/false self).
- **Choosing a character's signature defense (the showable fingerprint of their inner life)** → the **defense catalog by Vaillant's levels** — pick one or two, place them on the maturity ladder, and let stress push them *down* it. These *are* the repo's five defense verbs (`withdraw/control/attack/please/perform`) with a mechanism underneath.
- **Building an interpersonal *strategy* under threat** → **Horney's three moves** (toward/against/away) — the relational parent of the repo defense verbs — plus the **tyranny of the shoulds** for perfectionism and self-hatred.
- **Writing an antagonist who is the hero's disowned half, or a hollow-mask character** → **Jung** (shadow, persona/self gap, the complex that fires autonomously) — the deepest arc-and-antagonist engine; individuation as the whole-life arc.
- **Writing narcissism, grandiosity, and disproportionate injury-rage from the inside** → **Kohut** (the fragile self, mirror-hunger, narcissistic rage) — the interior of the Dark-Triad narcissism facet (`trait-models.md`).
- **Setting how much complexity a character can hold — their whole meaning-frame — or arcing them slowly toward wisdom** → **Loevinger's ego-development ladder** (Impulsive → Self-Protective → Conformist → Self-Aware → Conscientious → Individualistic → Autonomous → Integrated) — a maturity axis orthogonal to traits, defenses, and morals, and the best-measured one here; stack it with Vaillant's defense-maturity and Kernberg's integration for a converging read.
- **Staging the inner division as *voiced* material, or a self-fulfilling life-destiny** → **Berne's TA** (Parent/Adult/Child ego-states you can literally write as dialogue; the life script with its decided ending; the drivers) or **Freud's id/ego/superego** three-way contest — the most stageable options, feeding `narrative-craft` interiority and `arc-engine`.
- **Naming the gap between who they are and who they think they are (self-deception)** → any of these read as content for the **three views** (`docs/self-and-perception.md`): the **shadow** (Jung), the **idealized vs despised self** (Horney), the **false self** (Winnicott), and every **defense** (the machinery keeping a truth out of the self-image) are all `truth ≠ self`, the repo's richest interior material.

**The through-line:** the dimensional and value models say *what a person is and weights*; this file says *how they got that way and what runs underneath* — the wound and its developmental address, the introjected cast, the defense that fires before thought, the mask over the disowned self, the crisis still being re-fought. Take the **structure** (the divided self, the wound → defense chain, the internal objects, the maturity ladder, the life-crises) and leave the **certainty** — none of this is validated like the traits, and its power for a character writer is precisely its interpretive richness. Wire the wound into `docs/drives-schema.md`, the self-deception into `docs/self-and-perception.md`, the maturity/arc into `arc-engine`, and let the generator (`docs/generative-model.md`) turn the buried structure into surface behavior an observer then names.
