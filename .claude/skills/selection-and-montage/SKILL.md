# Selection & Montage — the cutter's craft well

This is the toolbox the **`cutter`** agent draws on. The simulation produces exhaustive biographies — every thought and act of every life; the cutter makes the *book* — the selected, ordered, paced sequence of scenes. This skill holds the craft of that transform: **the documentary editor's cut — selection, order, pacing — never invention.** (The book cannot contain what did not happen in the sim; `README`, `acceptance-criteria.md`.)

**It holds craft, never events.** *How* to choose, order, and pace lives here. *What happened* — the recorded moments the cut draws from — reaches the cutter through the biographies and the ledger, never through this toolbox.

## The method — the documentary editor's cut
1. **Cut the inert.** A life is mostly inert; a story is not. Keep only moments that carry a turn, a promise, a payoff, or a revelation.
2. **Order for tension, not time.** Chronology is one option among many; arrange scenes so the pressure rises.
3. **Pace by speed.** Full scene for the pivotal; summary for the connective tissue; montage to compress a stretch. Vary the speed or the book flattens.
4. **Hold the POV wall.** Every scene is bounded by its POV's knowledge (`narration.md`); you cannot cut to what no POV witnessed.
5. **Track every promise to its payoff.** Nothing planted goes unpaid; nothing paid was unplanted.
6. **Never invent.** If the shape wants a beat the footage lacks, report the gap — do not fabricate it.

## Framework-neutral & portable
The frameworks here are peers, not a ladder; any convention (a three-act cut, non-linear, a frame) is a tool, not a rule. Plain markdown, liftable to any harness — the cut method works against any recorded-then-shaped narrative, not only this repo's.
