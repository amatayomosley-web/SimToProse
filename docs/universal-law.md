# Universal Law — the guide (what must be *settled*, not what's *true*)

> **Step 1 of the world workflow** (`world-model.md`: premise → **law** → planet → history → present). This doc does **not** pick a world's laws — that's directorial work, per book. It is the **rubric of law-questions every world-setup must answer** before anything downstream, plus the discipline that keeps each question load-bearing. The director answers; the guide guarantees nothing load-bearing is skipped, and that every answer has teeth and a truth-status.

## What "universal law" is
The **deepest ground truth** of a world — *what is actually true here* — from which planet, history, and present all follow and which **none of them may violate** (`world-model.md`). Every character vault is a partial, possibly-false *projection* of this layer (`knowledge-model.md`). Settle it first because everything inherits it.

## How to use this guide (the meta-rules)
1. **Serve the premise (step 0).** Every law choice must serve the one-line conceit, or it's arbitrary. The premise is the finish you work backward from.
2. **Default to mundane / earthlike; the premise must *justify* each deviation.** This is sparse authoring at the cosmic scale — don't switch on the supernatural unless the story needs it. The bias is "no, unless."
3. **Every "yes, X exists" carries two mandatory companions** (the cross-cutting checks below): its **Limit** and its **Epistemic status**. A "yes" without both is incomplete.
4. **Only settle to the depth that's levered** (the depth rule) — answer each question as deep as the probe/story needs, mark the rest "undetermined — fill when levered." **Exception: the switches** (does magic / the divine / the supernatural exist *at all*) must be answered even if shallow, because everything downstream forks on them.
5. **Nothing downstream may contradict what's settled here.** If planet/history/present needs to break a law, the law was wrong — revise the law, not the violation.

## The two cross-cutting checks — apply to *every* "yes" above
These are the discipline that turns a wish-list into a usable law-set.

- **The Limit (the teeth).** For every power, force, or being that exists, state explicitly **what it cannot do.** This is non-optional, and it's the most important field in the guide: the probe's hardest pass-condition is the honest *"no lever works → revise the beat,"* which is only reachable if the world can plausibly **refuse** a lever (`world-model.md`, `probe-plan.md`). **A power with no stated limit is the director's get-out-of-jail card and makes the probe hollow.** Author the ceiling, not just the ability.
- **The Epistemic status (known vs believed).** For every supernatural element, mark it **known-true / known-false / contested-unknowable** *within the world*. Characters act on vault **beliefs**, not facts (`knowledge-model.md`), so the gap between what is *actually* true and what is *believed* is the deepest dramatic engine — and the sim must hold the truth even when every character is wrong. A praying character is either talking to **someone** or to **nothing**; step 1 decides which, and the believer is simulated identically either way — only the ground truth differs.

## The law domains (the checklist)

### A. Physical law — the substrate
- **Question:** Is physics earthlike, or altered (gravity, time's arrow, scale, conservation)?
- **Why load-bearing:** the floor planet and history are built on; bounds what is physically reachable and scarce.
- **Default:** earthlike.
- **If altered:** state the *rules* of the deviation (a lawful alteration, not a vibe) — and its Limit.
- **Time:** linear by default; if cyclic, branching, or *manipulable* (travel, loops, dilation) that is a law — state its rules and Limit. (Whether the *future* is fixed is domain D.)

### B. The supernatural switch — does the more-than-physical exist?
The single deepest toggle. Three **independent** forks (any combination is legal):
- **Magic** — does it exist? If yes: **source** (where the power comes from), **who can wield it**, **cost** (what it takes), and **Limit** (what it can't do — the teeth). *Unbounded magic breaks the probe.*
- **The divine / cosmic order** — are there gods, fate, a moral order? If yes: the **Epistemic fork is sharpest here** — are they **real (known-true)** or is religion **sincere false belief**? This changes how every believer is simulated and what their prayers actually reach.
- **Supernatural beings** — can undead / demons / spirits / magical beasts exist? (Mundane animals need no permission — they're step-2 ecology; *supernatural* ones need the law-level "yes" — the monster fork, `world-model.md` placement rule.)

### C. Persons — mind, soul, death (where the stakes are set)
- **Mind / soul:** Is consciousness special? Are there souls — is the self more than the body?
- **Death:** Is it **final**? Or afterlife / reincarnation / resurrection / undeath? **This is the stakes question.** If death isn't final, the dramatic floor (mortal stakes) transforms — every life-or-death decision the engine weighs is reweighted (`generative-model.md`: death sets "the worst that can happen," which the FEAR/PANIC systems key on).
- **Why load-bearing:** it sets the ceiling of what's at stake — the top rung of every character's stakes-ladder.

### D. Causation & fate — is the future open?
- **Fate / prophecy:** Is the future fixed? Is destiny real and *operative* (not just believed)?
- **Cosmic agenda:** Does the universe have a moral arc — does divine justice actually *operate*, or is it only believed in (back to the Epistemic check)?
- **Moral ontology:** Are good and evil **real cosmic forces** — detectable, substantive (literal corruption, taint, sanctity; "detect evil" *works*) — or **human constructs** (morality is judgment, not physics)? Gates whether a curse/blessing is real, and whether redemption is metaphysical or psychological. (Epistemic check still applies — people may *believe* in a cosmic evil that isn't there.)
- **Why load-bearing:** governs whether the world itself is an **agent with intentions**, and whether the character-autonomy premise (free choice) is even metaphysically real in-world. A fixed-future world simulates choice differently than an open one.

### E. Cosmological structure — the shape and extent of reality
- **Question:** Is reality a **single physical plane**, or are there **others** — an underworld, a spirit world, the Fae, heavens/hells as actual *places*, nested or parallel realms?
- **Why load-bearing:** it **houses** the rest — where the dead go (C), where gods dwell (B), where magic draws its power from (B). Gates whether planar travel, incursion, or "the beyond" exist as circumstance at all. (*Other planes* are a law question; the *physical* world's geography is step 2 — planet.)
- **Default:** one plane — the physical world is all there is.
- **Physical extent & reachability:** is the inhabited cosmos **one world or many** (a system, a galaxy, intergalactic)? Is travel/contact between worlds possible (FTL, gates, generation ships), and its **Limit**? Default: one world. *This is the upstream of any interstellar/intergalactic community — whether worlds can connect at all is settled here; the communities themselves (species, polities, contact-history) are a **present-systems / step-4** concern, at whatever scale this sets.*
- **Origin (cosmogony):** was reality **made** (a creator / a creation) or eternal/uncaused? This is law; its truth-status is an Epistemic call (a true creation vs a creation *myth*). (The planet's deep-time *natural* history is step 2.)
- **Limit (teeth):** the **boundary rules** — can anything cross between realms, who, and at what cost? An open boundary with no rules is the same hollow-probe failure as unlimited magic.
- **Epistemic:** is the structure **known-true or believed**? (Does anyone *know* there's an afterlife, or is it faith?)

## Worked example — one premise, cascading (illustrative, not a chosen world)
**Premise (step 0):** *"a world where the dead can be bargained with."*
- **C (death):** death is **not final** — the dead persist in some form. **B (beings):** that form exists (shades / the bound dead). **B (magic):** a necromantic law — its **source** and **cost** (who can bargain, what it takes).
- **Limit (teeth):** the dead **cannot** be compelled, only bargained with; the price **compounds**; they **cannot lie about the past but cannot be trusted about the future.** Now the world can *deny* a lever — a character can't just raise an army of the obedient dead. Probe stays honest.
- **Epistemic:** are the bargains **real** (known-true: the dead actually answer) or **sincere false belief** (the "answers" are projection, the bargainers charlatans or self-deceived)? That single fork makes the *same* premise either a **horror of the real** or a **tragedy of delusion** — and the believer is simulated identically; only the ground truth differs.

One premise → answers in C, B, B, each carrying its Limit and Epistemic status. That cascade *is* using the guide.

## On completeness — why the list is not the guarantee
The domain set (A–E) covers the load-bearing law-questions for the common case. It is deliberately **not** claimed exhaustive: a premise can always demand an exotic axis no list anticipated (a world whose *premise* is that true names have power, or that the dead outvote the living). The real coverage guarantee is **not** the list — it's the **meta-rules**: default mundane, the premise justifies every deviation, every "yes" carries a Limit + Epistemic status, settle only what's levered. Those absorb an unforeseen axis exactly the way they handle the listed ones — so the guide stays robust even where it isn't complete. (One more axis worth a stated default: **is the law itself stable, or in flux** over the story's timescale — a fading magic, an ending age? Default stable; flip it only if the premise is *about* the change.)

## Output of step 1
A settled-to-probe-depth set of: **physical law**; the **supernatural switches** (magic / divine / beings), each with **Limit + Epistemic status**; the **death/soul stakes**; the **fate/causation stance**. Everything downstream inherits it and may not violate it. Mark everything not yet levered "undetermined."
