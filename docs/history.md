# History — the justification layer (the rules that govern its process)

> Step 3. History is **the *why* of the present**, not a chronology — a **causal chain authored *backward* from the premise's demanded present and *validated forward***, to the depth levered. It opens with the origin of peoples and ends *loaded with live tension*. This doc is the rules that govern how it's authored. Rubric, not content.

## Role
History is to the world what backstory is to a character: the **justification, not the deliverable**. It produces the present's *conditions* — "why is this region poor and rebellious? — *that war*," not "poverty = high" — and writes man-made features (cities, borders, ruins) back onto the planet map. (`world-model.md` step 3.)

## Inputs (what it inherits)
- **Premise** — the **curator**: which threads to author, what the history is *about*, and the demanded present it aims at. (Not just a target — the editorial lens; same selection-not-transcription as the novel, one layer up.)
- **Universal law** — the **causal palette**: what forces can drive events (magic, gods, monsters, fate, moral-force) *plus* the load-bearing **beliefs** (a *false* faith still drives crusades — events follow belief, not only truth).
- **Broader community** (if one exists) — the **exogenous drivers**: contact, colonization, war, trade, abandonment.
- **Planet** — the **board + stakes**: geography/barriers (where events can happen), **resource spread** (the conflict engine), biomes (where civilization concentrates), creature habitats.

## Opening move
History begins with the **origin of peoples** — *created* (law/divine), *evolved* (planet/ecology), or *arrived* (community/colonization), resolved from the three upstream layers — then time runs forward. The planet seated only non-sapient life; peoples enter here.

## The rules that govern the process
A **three-tier hierarchy**, not a flat list: the prime rule **curates** the craft; the floor **bounds** even the prime rule.

### ▸ Prime rule — governs everything below
**Curate to the premise.** History is a *cut*; the premise is the editor. Every authored event earns its place by bearing on the premise's demanded present (or a character/beat it needs); author those threads to depth, stub the rest. **This rule selects how every craft rule below applies** — which causes to trace, which events to earn, which hinges to author, which tensions to leave live. It is the lens, not a peer.

### ▸ Craft rules — serve the prime rule (it curates which/how they apply)
- **Author causes, not dates.** A causal chain ("this, *because* that"), never a timeline. An event with no downstream consequence in the present isn't history — it's trivia; don't author it.
- **Earn events by conditions, never by fiat** *(the load-bearing craft rule)*. The historical twin of direct-by-circumstance: if the premise needs a war or a collapse, author the **conditions** that make it plausible (the scarcity, the power vacuum, the heir's death) — don't decree the event. A forced, unearned event is the same failure as forcing a character's beat.
- **Authored backward, validated forward** — the method that reconciles the prime rule's *backward* demand with the floor's *forward* check. From the demanded present backward to the events that yield it; then run the chain forward and confirm it *actually produces* the target. If it can't, revise the present or the upstream, never just declare it. (`world-model.md`: order ≠ authoring direction.)
- **Hinges only, recency-weighted.** Author the *pivotal* events to depth; mark the rest "undetermined — fill when levered." Weight depth toward the **recent** past — it shapes the present and the living characters' formative worlds; the deep past stays a one-line myth/sketch until something levers it.
- **End on live tension, not equilibrium** *(the forgotten one)*. History's output is the present's **unresolved** tensions (the old grievance, the contested border, the suppressed faction) — the **fuel** for the director's circumstances. A history that tidily resolves everything leaves the present inert. And it must lay the **formative conditions** the principals are generated from — history is the source the character-generator reads.

### ▸ The floor — bounds even the prime rule (not curatable)
Two consistency invariants the premise **cannot** override *within history's process*. They aren't "under" the prime rule — they're the floor it stands on; overriding them is the **forcing failure**, not curation.
- **Inherit upstream, never violate it.** Every event possible under the (already-set) universal law, on the planet's real geography, consistent with the community gate. *(The premise can DRIVE the upstream during whole-world backward authoring — even set the law — but once set, history obeys it.)*
- **Accumulate without contradiction.** Each event consistent with all prior ground truth + the world-state ledger. History only adds; it never silently rewrites what's already true.

## Output
The present's **state** (the society/world the sim consumes) + its **live tensions** (the director's levers) + the **formative conditions** (for character generation) + **features written onto the map** (cities, borders, ruins). Authored to the depth the premise levers; the rest stubbed.

## Falsification
The rules assume a premise-demanded present can be *reliably forward-derived* from authored conditions (rules 3–4). If, in the probe, backward-authored histories routinely *can't* forward-validate (the conditions won't plausibly yield the demanded present), then either premise-demanded presents are over-constraining and need a relief valve, or rule 3 needs softening toward limited fiat. Test in the probe.
