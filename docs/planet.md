# Planet — the physical stage (sized to what's required)

> Step 2. **Not** "design a whole planet" — design the **physical stage the story actually needs**: its size, then geology, resource spread, ecology, and creature habitats — each **to the depth levered**. All of it is the **stage**, and it comes **before the people** (history + present, steps 3–4). Inherits law (1) + the community gate (1.5); constrains everything downstream. Rubric, not content.

## "Planet" is the wrong default — size to the footprint first
The label overscopes. A story set in one valley does not need a globe. **The first decision is scope:** what physical extent do the characters traverse and the plot touch? Size the stage to *that* — a region by default, a world only if the story is planetary. Everything below is authored *at that scope*, to the depth the probe/story levers (`world-model.md` depth rule). This is the load-bearing discipline here: **the stage is sized to need, never to completeness.**

## The stack — designed in order, each constrains the next
*All at the chosen scope, hinges-first, before any people.*

1. **Scope / size** — the footprint (valley → region → continent → world) and the few defining scales (how far across, how long to cross, how isolated). Sized to the story, not to realism.
2. **Geology / terrain** — the bones: landforms, what's underground, water (rivers, seas, coasts), and the **barriers** (mountains, deserts, straits) that *gate movement*. Barriers are levers — they decide what's reachable, where an army can't go, what stays isolated.
3. **Resource spread (rare-earth / valuable materials)** — *where the scarce, valuable, or strategic materials sit.* The **economic + conflict substrate**: what makes a place worth taking, trading for, settling, or dying over. **Uneven distribution is the point** — scarcity-here / abundance-there is what makes geography *dramatically* matter; most plot pressure traces to who controls what's scarce. (First-class — do not bury it under "resources.")
4. **Climate / biomes** — derived from geology + latitude + water: what's survivable and growable where. The habitability map.
5. **Ecology** — flora + fauna at this scope: what lives here, the food web's broad strokes, the dangerous *mundane* beasts.
6. **Creature habitats — where animals & monsters live** — place the *significant* creatures: apex predators, herd resources, and — **only if law (step 1) permitted them** — supernatural monsters. Existence was settled at law; **presence is settled here** (`world-model.md` placement rule: monster = existence @ law, presence @ planet, role @ history). A habitat is *where*, *why there* (it ties to geology/biome), and *what it gates* (the pass no one crosses because of what lives in it).

*Non-sapient life only.* **Sapients / peoples are not placed at this step** — their **origin** is history's *opening move* (step 3), resolved from law (created) + planet (evolved) + community (arrived). The planet sets the stage that will *author* the peoples; it does not seat them.

## Cross-cutting discipline
- **Depth rule — dominant here.** Author the **hinges**: the resource node the plot needs, the pass that gates the army, the monster-haunted region. Mark the rest "undetermined — fill when levered." *Do not author a whole planet's geology.* This is the literal answer to "planet is too large for what's required."
- **Inherits upstream.** Obey law (magical materials or supernatural monsters exist here *only if* step 1 permits) and the community gate (if colonized: extracted/depleted resources, introduced species, off-world demand on what's scarce).
- **Constrains downstream — why it precedes the people.** Geology + resources + habitats author the *people's* options before the people exist: where they'd settle, what they'd fight over, what isolates them, where history happens. This is **formative coupling at the world scale** (`world-model.md`) — the stage authors the civilization the way a formative world authors a character's baseline.

## Output
A physical stage **sized to the story**, with: scope · geology/terrain (+ movement barriers) · **resource spread** · climate/biomes · ecology · **creature habitats** — each to the depth levered. The stage on which history (step 3) plays out and people are placed.
