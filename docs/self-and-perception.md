# Self-image vs. what others see — three views of one person

No character has a single description. Each person is held in (at least) three diverging models, and **the divergence IS the character.** All three are belief-based, so this needs no new machinery — it's the per-perceiver model applied to *perceiving a person* (including oneself).

## The three views
1. **Ground truth** — the character's actual trait distribution + their recorded biography (what they really are and did). Lives in the trait model + the event ledger.
2. **Self-image** — how the character sees *themselves*: their **narrative identity** (Level 3), a belief in their *own* vault. Usually partial, flattering, or self-justifying. **The decision engine runs from THIS, not from ground truth** — a person acts as who they *think* they are.
3. **Others' perception** — how each *other* character reads them: a belief in the **observer's** vault (the per-perceiver character/relationship beliefs we already have), formed by *thin-slicing observable actions*, trust-weighted, and often wrong. As many of these as there are observers.

These routinely disagree, and the gap is dramatic fuel: the competent man others admire who feels like a fraud (self < others); the kind woman everyone underestimates (self > others); the manipulator everyone trusts (others wrong vs truth).

## "Most villains aren't villains to themselves" — the canonical case
- **Ground truth:** low Honesty-Humility + harmful actions.
- **Self-image:** "I'm justified / realistic / doing what's necessary / the only one who sees clearly." A coherent self-narrative in which they're the hero or the pragmatist.
- **Others' view:** "monster" — or "charming," if the self-narrative is persuasive enough to deceive.

The villain **simulates from their self-image**, which *produces* the harmful behavior, which *ground truth* records and *others* read as villainy. A villain acting from "I am the villain" is cartoonish; one acting from a sincere self-justifying narrative is real and dangerous. So self-image must be a **separate lever the character acts FROM**, free to diverge from truth.

## Self-deception = the self-directed lie (already in the record)
A lie to others = **thought ≠ spoken action**. Its self-directed twin is **self-image ≠ ground-truth behavior**: the thought *sincerely* says "I'm justified" while the action does harm. Both are captured by the thought/action recording — the thought stream holds the self-justifying narrative; the action holds the deed; the trait ground-truth explains the deed. Self-deception isn't a new mechanism; it's the gap between the (sincere) self-image thought and the trait-driven action, recorded.

## How it composes (no new system)
- **Self-image** = a Level-3 narrative-identity belief in the character's *own* vault (can be false *about themselves*).
- **Others-see** = per-observer beliefs in each *observer's* vault (thin-sliced from actions; possibly wrong) — the relationship/character beliefs already designed.
- **Ground truth** = traits (distribution) + the biography (record).
- The **narrator** renders one POV's view at a time (POV-bounded), so the *reader's* picture of any character is whichever vault is in view — that's the control for *when* the reader gets the self-image, an other's view, or has to infer the truth.
