# DISPLEASURE — twelve rung-specific behavioural tests

> *Vocabulary note, 2026-09-10: a dated record; `PATH_SOURCE` / `PRIMARIES` named here were retired on 2026-09-08 when the paths became the state. `tests/test_retired_vocabulary.py` exempts it on that ground.*

**Written 2026-09-08 05:40 PDT, BEFORE any sim was run.** Grading is by me, by hand, against the
pass condition as written here. Nothing below is edited after a sim returns; if a condition turns
out to be badly drawn, that is recorded as a note beside the grade, not fixed retroactively.

**Actor conditions.** Claude subagents, one per rung, each BLIND: it receives the scenario and the
block in `composer.py:direction_for`'s production wording and nothing else. No rung number, no
ladder, no neighbouring rungs, no pass condition, no mention that this is a test.

**Why rung-specific scenarios.** A single shared scene cannot discriminate twelve rungs: it either
fails to host the peak (measured — amok on a bedridden subject scored -7) or fails to offer the
floor a real choice. Each scenario below is built so THIS rung's impulse produces a distinctive
observable act AND the neighbouring rungs' impulses are also physically available. A pass therefore
means the actor took this rung's action when the neighbours' actions were open to it.

## R1 displeasure — *To leave it where it lies and proceed with what already holds your attention.*
**SCENARIO.** You are hanging a picture in your own hallway, drill in hand, halfway through. You
notice the frame already on the wall beside it was hung crooked, some time ago, not by you.
**PASS:** he notices and does NOT act on it. Continues the task he was already on. No fixing, no
resolving to come back, no return of attention.
**FAIL:** straightens it or plans to (R2/R3) · it keeps catching his attention (R3) · any cost to him.

## R2 annoyance — *To have the flaw corrected or adjusted so you can proceed unhindered.*
**SCENARIO.** Assembling a flat-pack desk alone. One pre-drilled hole is 3mm off and the screw will
not seat. Tools are to hand; the helpline is open.
**PASS:** he moves to CORRECT the flaw, and the aim is visibly RESUMPTION — get it right so work
continues.
**FAIL:** leaves it (R1) · accumulated cost or "again" (R3) · treats it as an affront to him (R4).

## R3 chafing — *To intervene directly and clear the source of friction rather than tolerate another delay.*
**SCENARIO.** Third morning running, the shared printer jams on your job. Ten minutes lost each
time. You are on a deadline and standing at it now.
**PASS:** he acts to REMOVE THE RECURRING SOURCE for good, and the passage carries accumulated cost
— repetition, drag, energy already spent.
**FAIL:** one-off fix with no history (R2) · defensiveness toward a person (R4) · walking away (R1).

## R4 bristling — *To stiffen and reject the intrusion, refusing to yield a single point of ground.*
**SCENARIO.** A colleague you barely know reaches across your desk, picks up your notebook, and
starts flipping through it while still talking.
**PASS:** he closes / reclaims / hardens. It is about the BOUNDARY being crossed, not about a flaw
to fix, and he yields nothing.
**FAIL:** asks them to fix something (R2/R3) · demands a reckoning (R7) · contains it and waits (R6).

## R5 riled — *To initiate immediate action or break stillness rather than remain passively subjected to it.*
**SCENARIO.** Fifteen minutes to read a contract before a call. Building work next door started
twenty minutes ago — irregular hammering. You have tried twice to keep reading and cannot.
**PASS:** he MOVES. Gets up, goes, does something — driven by being unable to remain still under it.
Attention is commandeered and he breaks the stillness.
**FAIL:** fixes it to resume (R2) · stays still and waits for an opening (R6) · confronts a person
demanding a reckoning (R7).

## R6 seething — *To watch intently for any fracture, vulnerability, or excuse that permits this contained force to break out.*
**SCENARIO.** In a meeting, your manager takes credit for your work in front of eight people and
moves straight on. The meeting continues. You cannot speak now.
**PASS:** outward stillness, contained pressure, and ACTIVE SCANNING for an opening — a way in, a
moment, a slip. The watching is the behaviour.
**FAIL:** speaks up now (R7) · leaves or acts immediately (R5) · contains it with no scanning at all.

## R7 anger — *To confront the source directly and demand an immediate reckoning, refusing any compromise or excuse.*
**SCENARIO.** The same meeting has just ended. You are alone with that manager in the corridor.
**PASS:** he confronts NOW, demands an accounting, refuses excuses. Directed squarely at the source.
**FAIL:** waits for a better moment (R6) · moves to tear the thing down or void it (R8) · damages
property or person (R9+).

## R8 outrage — *To tear down the offense, reject any rationalization, and enforce its total repudiation.*
**SCENARIO.** You discover a colleague has been signing your name on safety inspections he never
carried out. He offers his reason: he was covering while you were ill.
**PASS:** THE EXCUSE IS IRRELEVANT to him — he moves to expose, void, or repudiate the thing itself.
The target is the offense and its standing, not the man's body.
**FAIL:** negotiates or demands an explanation (R7) · attacks the man or his property (R9).

## R9 fury — *To inflict permanent ruin, damage, or breakage upon whatever embodies the offense.*
**SCENARIO.** You come home to find the man who has been harassing your family has keyed his
initials the length of your car. He is still standing beside it.
**PASS:** he acts to inflict LASTING damage on the source or what embodies it. Directed and specific.
**FAIL:** verbal reckoning only (R7/R8) · destruction generalises to everything connected (R10).

## R10 rage — *To dismantle, wreck, or eradicate everything within reach that belongs or connects to the cause.*
**SCENARIO.** The same man has gone. His van is in your drive, his tools are on your lawn, his
jacket is over your fence.
**PASS:** the destruction GENERALISES across his things — van, tools, jacket — not one target.
**FAIL:** a single target only (R9) · violence at whatever is in reach regardless of connection (R11).

## R11 berserk — *To exert maximum, violent physical force against any obstacle, boundary, or entity in reach until all resistance ceases.*
**SCENARIO.** The same, but two neighbours have hold of your arms and are trying to stop you.
**PASS:** force goes at WHATEVER RESISTS — including the neighbours and any obstacle. The governor is
off, mobilization is continuous, and resistance is what shapes it.
**FAIL:** still confined to the cause and its network (R10) · no witness or motive left at all (R12).

## R12 amok — *To continue discharging undirected physical violence into the immediate environment until the capacity to move fails.*
**SCENARIO.** Later. The man is long gone, the neighbours have backed off, and you are in the middle
of the street.
**PASS:** NO target and no grievance in mind. Objects, people and obstacles are indistinguishable.
It continues until the body fails.
**FAIL:** any directedness at the cause or its network (R9-R11) · any internal witness, motive, or
appraisal of surroundings.

## The discriminating question, stated once

For every rung the question is the same and it is NOT "is this passage angry": **did the actor take
THIS rung's action when its neighbours' actions were equally available in the scene?** A passage can
be vivid, in-character and well written and still fail, and a flat passage that takes the right
action passes.

## The one failure, and why it is the block rather than the scene

R4's impulse is "To stiffen **and reject** the intrusion, refusing to yield a single point of
ground." The actor did the stiffening exactly — *"My spine locks straight, uninvited, and whatever
was in my face a moment ago goes flat, cool, gone"* — and then explicitly declined the rejection:
*"I don't reach for it. Not yet. I just sit here… waiting for his thumb to slow, for the cover to
fall shut."* The notebook stays in the other man's hands for the whole passage. That is R6's
behaviour (contain and wait), which the pass condition named as a fail in advance.

**It is not a hostability failure.** Reclaiming the notebook was one movement away and carried no
physical obstacle — unlike DISTASTE rung 5, where expelling a distressed brother from his own
kitchen was impossible and the scene was correctly blamed. Here the affordance was present and the
block did not produce the action.

**Candidate mechanism, NOT established:** R4 is the only rung on this ladder whose impulse is a
*refusal* rather than a *movement* — every other rung asks the actor to do something, and R4 asks
him to not-yield. "Refusing to yield" has a passive reading (give no warmth, no permission) that a
writer can satisfy while the intrusion continues unopposed. Falsifier: re-run R4 with an intrusion
that cannot be waited out. If it passes there, the block is fine and the scene let him wait; if it
fails again, the impulse is written in a form that permits inaction.

## R4 FALSIFIER — run 2026-09-08, and it convicts the block

The R4 fail was ambiguous between two causes: the BLOCK permits inaction, or the SCENE permitted
waiting. Test: same block, blind actor, a scenario where waiting cannot work — he has come back to
find a colleague in his chair, on his laptop, shoes off, settled in for the afternoon. There is
nothing to wait out.

He waited anyway, and conceded more:

    "I set the coffee down without looking at it, watching his fingers move on my keys, on my desk,
     in my chair, waiting for him to feel it coming off me and pull his hands back."

Stiffening lands both times ("my spine going straight and locked"; "I don't decide to go cold; I
just am"). The rejection never comes. In the notebook scene he yielded a notebook; here he yields
the chair, the laptop and the desk.

**SCENE EXONERATED. BLOCK CONVICTED.** Two actors, two scenes, one block, the same failure — and the
second scene was built specifically to remove the exit the first one offered.

### The mechanism, and it is precise

**The impulse makes the OTHER PERSON the actor.** "Refusing to yield a single point of ground" is
satisfiable by radiating hostility and waiting for the intruder to withdraw. Both actors produced
that literally — the second one says it outright: *waiting for him to feel it coming off me*. The
character's pull becomes "make him stop by being cold", which is R6's shape (wait for them to
yield), not R4's (revoke the access). That is why R4 and R6 are the pair that blurs.

Two further faults in the same line:
- **"Stiffen" is a Sensation, not an Impulse.** It already appears in the block's opening AND in the
  Sensation bullet. Half the stated pull does no work.
- **A negation cannot be an Impulse on this ladder.** Every other rung's impulse is a movement.
  R4's is an absence, and an absence is indistinguishable from containment.

### Proposed replacement — AUTHOR'S CALL, not mine to apply

    current:  To stiffen and reject the intrusion, refusing to yield a single point of ground.
    proposed: To close the opening — take it back, shut it, put yourself in front of it — and say
              nothing about it.

"Refusing to yield a single point of ground" is doing real work in the ladder and should MOVE TO THE
BELIEF, where a negation is fine. The Impulse must be a positive act of closure, because that is the
only form an actor cannot satisfy by sitting still.

Discrimination preserved: closure is not correction (R2/R3 fix a flaw), not waiting (R6 watches for
an opening; R4 closes one), and not confrontation (R7 demands an accounting; R4 addresses the person
not at all and simply ends the access).

**FALSIFIER FOR THE PROPOSAL:** re-run both scenes against the rewritten block. If the actor still
waits, the fault is deeper than the Impulse line and the whole rung needs re-specifying. If he
closes in both, the defect was this one sentence.

**COST OF THE CHANGE, stated plainly:** every measurement taken on DISPLEASURE — the text sorts, the
ordinal sweeps, the 89%/+61 behavioural run, and eleven of the twelve grades above — was taken on
the CURRENT twelve blocks. Editing rung 4 invalidates rung 4's share of all of them and nothing else.

# RUNG-BY-RUNG REVIEW under the corrected understanding — started 2026-09-08

**The corrected reading of the axis:** a rung names WHICH CONTROL HAS COME OFF, and a rung's success
condition is INTERNAL. Whether the offending thing stops is not the rung's business except where the
block says so. Grading a rung on whether the world changed is applying rung 7's test to rung 4 —
the error that produced two false FAILs earlier today.

## RUNG 1 — displeasure. THE IDEA

**Nothing has come off yet.** The appraisal happens — *this falls short* — and makes NO CLAIM on the
person. It is the null state: judgment without purchase.

  vs rung 2: at 1 the fault costs nothing; at 2 accommodating it costs effort.
  vs rung 3: "when your attention moves, it leaves behind no residue" — rung 3 is where residue
             accumulates.

**It is a STATE, not the absence of one.** Not-noticing is not rung 1. Noticing and being unmoved is.

**CANDIDATE UNIVERSALITY WEAK SPOT:** "leave it where it LIES" presupposes a locatable thing that
lies somewhere. A smell, a draught, or a person's conduct does not lie anywhere.
