# World model — the other half of the loop (SEED — to co-design)
**Status: seed.** The character system is designed end-to-end; the world is its comparable other half and is now the **prerequisite for the probe** — a character can't be probed without the world that influences them. This doc frames the world-design problem and lists the open questions. It does **not** yet invent world content (laws, religion, economy) — that's the directorial work we'll do together, the same way we built the character dimensions.

## Why the world comes before the probe
The probe tests whether a director can move a faithful character **using circumstance alone** (`probe-plan.md`). Circumstance **is** world-state. And the world is not set-dressing here — it's the **constraint that makes the lever non-arbitrary**:
- With no real world, the director has unlimited power to invent any circumstance ("a dragon forces her out"). The probe then tests "can you invent *a* lever" (trivially yes) instead of "can you find a *plausible, world-consistent* lever" (the real skill).
- The probe's hardest pass-condition — the honest **"no lever works → revise the beat"** (`probe-plan.md` rigor) — is only discoverable if the world can plausibly **deny** a lever. A world without teeth can never say no, so the probe can't fail honestly → hollow.

**Bound (so this doesn't balloon):** we don't need the whole world before the probe — we need a **slice with teeth**: enough rules that the world can plausibly refuse a lever. Author the hinges; lazy-resolve the rest; never pre-simulate the whole economy.

## The world's job in the character-decision loop (its three outputs)
1. **Circumstance** — the situations placed before characters (the director's only lever; the core stance in `README`).
2. **Consequence** — how the world pushes back on a character's action. This is what makes it a *loop*, not a stage: actions change world-state, which becomes the next circumstance. A static world starves the biographies — they go inert with nothing real to push against.
3. **Ground-truth facts** — the world is the canonical source the vaults draw from. Each character's vault is a *partial, possibly-false projection* of the world's ground truth (`knowledge-model.md`). The world is what they *can* come to know; the vault is what they *do*.

## Two timescales of world→character influence
The three outputs above are all **runtime** (the world acting on a character *during* the sim). There is a second, earlier channel: **creation-time / formative.**

**Formative coupling — the world authored the character's baseline.** The world a character *grew up in* seeds their starting disposition and relationship-priors. A streets / dog-eat-dog upbringing → a low **trust prior**. Mechanism (precise — *not* a flat "trust debuff"):
- **Lowered prior, scoped to out-group — not a global penalty.** The *default* trust a *new* relationship edge initializes to is low, toward **strangers and authority**. It does NOT suppress in-group bonds; the same upbringing typically *raises* loyalty to the chosen few. The true signature is **low out-group trust + high in-group loyalty + high vigilance**, not "distrustful of everyone."
- **Altered update dynamics.** It also tunes the trust *update rule* (`relationships.md`) — slower to grant (higher evidence threshold), faster to withdraw (heightened negativity bias). Formative conditions set both the *starting value* and the *update coefficients*.
- **Disposition lean.** Surfaces as a HEXACO Agreeableness-facet lean (low trust-of-others), so it shows in behavioral *style*, not just relationship math.

**Grounding discipline:** baseline stats are *derived from / justified by* the formative world, never set arbitrarily. "Why is their trust low? — the streets," not "the slider was at 0.2." The world is the **character-generator's input.**

**This is where models come from.** A recurring formative condition distills into a reusable **model** (`character-model.md` §models): "Street-Hardened" = trust-prior-down (out-group) + loyalty-up (in-group) + vigilance-up + (Layer-10) survival-wins-over-openness. As a *model* the low trust is exactly the "debuff" (bias-not-set transform) applied to any character of that background; as *content* it's just this one character's baseline — the content/model split. (Not all models are formative — temperament exists — but formative world-conditions are a major source of both baselines and models.)

## Same architecture as a character (not a new paradigm)
The world is *state + lazy-resolution + levers + ledger* applied to a place instead of a person:
- **Bible (ground truth)** — the canon. Analog of the character's disposition + worth + identity.
- **World-state ledger** — the evolving present. Analog of character STATE.
- **Dynamics** — how it changes on its own and reacts to action. Analog of the decision engine — resolved lazily, on-demand, where levered.
- **Three views** — ground-truth world ↔ what each character *believes* about it (their vault) ↔ what a faction/culture *holds*. The same truth/self/others split, one level up.

## World-design workflow (LOCKED order)
Design top-down by **dependency** — each layer constrains the next, never the reverse — but author each layer only to the **depth the probe needs** (top-down for *order*, backward-from-probe for *scope*; a slice with teeth, not the whole cosmos).

**Order ≠ authoring direction (the resolution of "society defines history").** The numbering is the **dependency / validation** order (forward: history *causes* society). But when the **premise demands a specific present** — a certain society — you **author backward** from that target: society → the history that would yield it → the planet/peoples/law that would yield that history. Then you **validate forward**: run the dependency order and confirm it *actually produces* the target. The forward chain is what you **check against**; the premise's demanded present is what you **author toward**. This is the same **authored-backward / validated-forward** discipline used for principals (`design.md`), now at world scale — and it *is* "the premise curates history" (step 3) from the other side: the demanded society is the backward-target the curation aims at. **Non-negotiable:** a society *decreed* without a forward-plausible history is the "forcing" failure — arbitrary, unearned; if forward-derivation can't yield it, revise the society or the upstream, never just declare it. Spine proposed by William (law → planet → history); refined with step 0, the present-state tail, and the depth rule.

0. **Premise / conceit** — the one-line "what world is this, and why simulate it." The finish we work backward from; universal-law choices must *serve* it or they're arbitrary.
1. **Universal law** — what is *actually true* here: physics (earthlike? magic, and its rules?) **AND metaphysics — is the religion TRUE? real gods, fate, afterlife, souls?** This is the deepest **ground truth** every vault is a partial/false projection of (`knowledge-model.md`); nothing downstream may violate it. Settle whether faith is *knowledge* or *sincere false belief* — it changes how every believer is simulated. **It also fixes what can EXIST** — magic, and whether *supernatural* creatures (undead, demons, magical beasts) are possible: the same metaphysics switch as gods/magic (mundane animals need no such permission; supernatural monsters do). → **The full rubric of law-questions to settle at this step — with the mandatory Limit (teeth) + Epistemic (known-vs-believed) checks — is `universal-law.md`.**
1.5. **Broader community (scope gate — before the planet).** Does the story's world stand alone, or sit inside a **wider community of worlds/peoples** (interplanetary → intergalactic)? **No → standalone, go to the planet. Yes → define it first** (membership · connection +Limit · structure · *this world's place* · contact status · who-knows), because the community *constrains* the planet (resources others want, introduced species, contact-history, member-vs-quarantine). Step-1 law (domain E: extent & reachability) settles whether a wider community is *possible*; this asks whether one *exists*. Full rubric: `broader-community.md`.
2. **Planet** — the physical stage, **sized to what the story needs** (a region, not necessarily a globe — *scope is the first decision*), in its own internal stack: **geology** (tectonics, minerals, ore, soil, rivers) → **hydrology / climate** → **ecology — flora (botany) + fauna** (incl. mundane dangerous beasts; an apex predator is step-2 ecology, not a law-level "monster"). Plus geography, biomes, resources, distances. Bounds what circumstance is reachable and what's scarce where; civ-history (step 3) plays out on it. (Its deep-time *natural history* — how geology + life became this — is the planet's implicit justification, the natural-history analog of civ-history at step 3.) → **Full rubric — scope-first, with resource-spread and creature-habitats as first-class outputs, all before the people: `planet.md`.**
3. **History** — what happened over time, on the planet, under the law. **It opens with the origin of peoples** — history's *time-zero* move: how sapients came to be here — **created** (law / divine), **evolved** (planet / ecology), or **arrived** (community / colonization) — resolved from the three upstream layers, then time runs forward. (Planet step 2 places only *non-sapient* life; peoples enter here.) **History is the *justification*, not the deliverable** — the same role backstory plays for a character (the world authors its present the way a character's formative world authors their baseline). "Why is this region poor and rebellious? — that war," not "poverty = high." History also writes man-made features back onto the map (cities, borders, ruins). → **Full process rules (8: curate-to-premise · causes-not-dates · earn-by-conditions · backward/forward · inherit-upstream · hinges/recency · end-on-tension · no-contradiction): `history.md`.** **The premise *curates* it:** history is a **cut** of all that could have happened, and the premise is the editor — same world + different premise (a war epic vs a domestic tragedy) → different threads authored to depth, the rest stubbed. It decides what history is *about*, governing how the depth rule applies to events. Same *selection-not-transcription* discipline as the novel-from-biographies, one layer up — **the editorial cut happens twice: premise → history, director → novel.**
4. **Present systems & state** — history's current output, and what the sim actually consumes: economy, governance / law-as-enforced, religion-as-lived, factions, culture — plus the **current world-state ledger** (the now). The layer that supplies circumstance + consequence to characters. → **Guide: `present-systems.md`** — designed by decomposing into components in dependency order (economy+culture → law → factions → ledger); details the **agnostic legal + custom presets** (schema + archetypes, instantiated *per culture*) and the **multi-culture contact layer** (dominance · legal pluralism · friction → factions + character-positions).

**Depth rule:** at each step author only the *hinges* the probe (and known characters/beats) lever on; mark the rest "undetermined — fill when levered." Don't pre-simulate.

**Placement rule (where does any element X go?).** Most world-elements live at **three layers, not one** — place X at each:
- **Existence** — the deepest layer that could *forbid* it: **law** for anything supernatural, **planet** for anything physical/biological.
- **Presence** — where it physically sits: **planet** (geology, biome, population, habitat).
- **Significance** — where agents engage it: **history** (step 3) if it was pivotal; **present systems** (step 4) if there's an economy / religion / faction around it.

Worked: geology = almost pure *presence* (step 2). Mundane flora/fauna = presence (step 2). **Monsters fork** — supernatural *existence* at step 1, *presence* at step 2, *role* at step 3–4. A sacred herb = botany (2) + religion (4); a blight that toppled a kingdom = botany (2) + history (3).

3. **Dynamics / consequence resolution** — **→ ANSWERED: `world-dynamics.md`.** How does the world push back resolves to **state + appraisal + decay like a character, but no will of its own — its will is the director, its books are the engine's.** Three channels, all landing as ledger events: rule-resolved consequence (world-appraisal by rule, future-dated events for delayed pushback, recurrence rates for standing processes), director-resolved choice within the engine-computed **plausible-response envelope** (factions as collective characters, `present-systems.md`), and placed circumstance (already canonical). Time passes via lazy **fold-forward(Δt)** at read time — pure function, replay-deterministic, depth-rule scoped. Hard rules resolve the computable; the director resolves the willful; the LLM resolves nothing at this layer. Also locates open-Q #5's teeth: denial = a rule says impossible, or the envelope says implausible — computable, not self-restraint.
4. **In-life coupling (runtime)** — **→ ANSWERED: `scene-assembly.md`.** How a world *fact* becomes a character *belief* mid-sim resolves to **perception is acquisition-in-progress**: a ledger event is scoped to the character's vantage (the perception wall), enters as a belief with witnessed-provenance (possibly false), and updates the vault for the next turn. The **two-streams-one-wall** model — perception + recall through the *same* epistemic boundary — is the runtime coupling; false beliefs diverge exactly as the vault's belief-not-fact store already allows. (The *formative* channel is handled above, under Two timescales; this was the runtime one.)
5. **The probe's "slice with teeth"** — the minimum world that can plausibly DENY a lever, so the probe can fail honestly (now the workflow's *depth rule*); concretely, which hinges the probe scenario forces at each step.
