# Building a cast — the procedure

**Status: PROCEDURE.** Written 2026-10-01, after the people of one institution in a working book were built one
person per roundtable session and then checked against each other. `guide-building-a-world.md` walks a blank page to a
world, and `character-authoring-rules.md` says what a character note must hold for the engine. Neither says how to
invent the people themselves so that each one is a recognizable person and the cast hangs together. This does.

It is the method, not a cast: examples use an invented place, a hill mill and the farms around it, so this file stays
upstreamable to the template. A book's own record of running it belongs with the book.

---

## The one thing to know before you start

A person is not their job. Every build is held to one test: **take away the person's part in the story — is a
recognizable person left?** A home, habits, tastes, people, a grudge, a joke. If only the job remains, the build fails.

Builds fail it the same way again and again: every friend, every grudge and the secret all sit inside the
institution. Ask for the outside life by name, or you will not get it.

## What a built person is: seven parts

1. **Who they are.** The kind of person; life outside work; a habit; what makes them laugh; a contradiction; how they
   talk (a phrase they say too often, what they never say, how they talk up and down); an ordinary fear, not about the
   work.
2. **Their days.** A typical day; home and who is in it; money (earned, owed, spent); food; the few things they own and
   care about; their body and how they keep it going; small pleasures and vices.
3. **Their gift**, where the world has one: what it does, written as an act and not a sense, and how far they have
   grasped it.
4. **Their history.** Dated from birth to the present; one petty regret; how they have changed.
5. **Their people.** Who they love; one friend outside the work; personal friction with at least two named people,
   each about something concrete, at least one of them outside the work; who they are to people who don't matter; what
   people say about them.
6. **What they know and keep.** Which of the place's secrets they hold; one private secret of their own.
7. **Why they go along with it.** The reason their world gives them, cited from the canon; how sure they are; the
   blind spot; a line they won't cross. For someone the place did something *to*, this part is how they live with it.

Then the test, in two or three lines: what remains with the work taken away.

## The rules every build keeps

- **No villains for evil's sake.** Anyone who does harm carries the reason their world gives them (a war, an
  institution, a custom, a stake), and the build cites where the canon says so.
- **Plain words, the way the person would think it.** No narrator's images ("the silence that had settled over his whole
  life") and no labels standing in for a thought ("values order"). If nobody would say it, rewrite it.
- **Friction is personal:** a named person and a concrete thing. Who reports to whom is not friction.
- **Never 100% sure.** The reason has a soft spot; say when it wobbles and what they do then.
- **The blind spot is a lie the person tells about themselves,** not a secret of the place they happen to know.
- **A petty regret is petty:** a meanness, a missed chance, a stupid purchase, a slight. Not a death.
- **Align where the world speaks; invent where it is silent;** mark every claim with which it is.

## How to run the sessions

- **One person per session.** A session that builds three people builds three thin ones.
- **Give the session the world, not a crowd.** A compact pack of what people live in (family custom, money, food,
  talk, class, the place's own facts), the fixed facts (the roster, the timeline, anyone already adopted), and one
  person to build.
- **Build the people others will touch first,** and carry into each later brief what the earlier builds already say
  about this person, line by line: "the miller's sister keeps bees behind the race", "he never sells flour on
  credit". A
  one-line summary of a neighbour gets misread, and the misreading becomes a contradiction you have to undo later.
- **Give linked people in full.** When the brief has to be trimmed, trim the least-linked people first, and keep the
  person this one will quarrel with.
- **Carry three running lists into every brief.** Names already used and names already turned down (see Names below);
  details already used ("give this person their own, don't repeat these"); and the place's fixed facts.
- **Fit the brief to what your runner can pass.** A brief that is too long fails before anyone speaks; check its size
  before each launch and trim in a fixed order.
- **Run the sessions back to back,** and write each one up while the next runs, so every brief carries the people
  built before it.

## Writing a session up

- **The writer invents nothing.** Every fact comes from a participant. Where the table did not settle a part, give the
  leading version and an **Open** line naming the alternative and who held it.
- **Anything that breaks a fixed fact or the canon is left out** and listed under **Flags**, with what it breaks.
- **Rewrite narration into the person's own thought,** keeping the content.
- **End with:** a short summary for later briefs, the test, the open points, and every new name with who it is.

## Checking the cast against itself

Sessions built one at a time drift apart. Before the author reviews the cast, check it as a whole.

1. **Index each person:** names, dates, money, details, and every statement their build makes about anyone else in
   the cast, one fact per line.
2. **For each person, check what everyone else says about them** against their own build: consistent, adds something
   new, or conflicts.
3. **Settle each conflict by one rule, in order.** The adopted facts win. Then a person's own build wins for their own
   life, habits and work: what the miller writes in his tally book is the miller's, how the shepherd talks is the
  shepherd's. Then, for
   something two people share, the version most builds agree on. Where both versions could be true at once (two people
   who each believe the other started it), keep both. Only what this leaves open goes to
   the author.
4. **Lay out how the place works,** step by step, from what every build claims: who receives, holds, does, records,
   signs, pays. Settle contradictions by the same rule, and list the steps and the years nobody covers.
5. **Find the repeats.** People invented apart converge on the same small things: the same fear, the same regret, the
   same lie told to oneself, the same kind of friend. Each repeat stays with the one person it does the most for; the
   others take something already in their own build, or lose it. A detail many people really would share (a custom of
   the world) stays.
6. **Fill what the repeats emptied** with a short session: one line per empty slot, carrying the full taken-lists, and
   new names only from a list already checked clear.
7. **Check names.** No two names one letter apart, two apart with the same first letter, sharing their first three
   letters, or rhyming. Check every new name against every name in use, including the book's own and the ones already
   turned down, and pick replacements from candidates already checked against all of them and against each other.
8. **Check dates.** Birth plus age equals the present; each person's own milestones land where the world's customs put
   them; anything outside a custom needs a reason the build already gives.

## What usually goes wrong

- **The outside life is missing.** Require a friend and a friction outside the work, and something that makes them
  laugh, in every brief.
- **A neighbour is misread** from a one-line summary. Carry what the other builds say, line by line.
- **The same small detail turns up in many people.** Keep a running list from the first session, not the last.
- **Money.** When the canon sets no wages or prices, every session invents its own scale, and the scales will not
  agree: one person's week of food costs another's month. Note it as open and settle one scale as the build expands,
  rather than per person.
- **The name space fills up.** Late in a cast, every name a session tries may clash with one already in use. Hand the
  session a list of names already checked clear.
