# Cut List — Maren Chronicle (25 turns)

## Arc Candidates Found

**Arc A: Bryn's fever — the almost-loss (turns 2–11)**
Maren works a climbing fever over four days, refuses to delegate, survives the third night. The child lives. Shape: threat → escalation → threshold → resolution. The Súil ghost is invoked explicitly at turn 4 ("It was the second night with Súil too"). Structural problem: the resolution at turn 10 feels like an ending but comes at the midpoint of the chronicle. What follows it is a grief arc — Tobin's death — which is a different story.

**Arc B: Tobin's death — the loss she couldn't prevent (turns 15–20)**
Maren tends Tobin through his last days, does everything right, watches him die, washes the body, stands apart at the mourning, reorders her shelves in silence. Shape: doing everything right → losing anyway → aftermath. This is the structural inversion of Arc A: Bryn was the near-miss she controlled through; Tobin is the man control couldn't hold. The counting tic doesn't stop at his death — "one, two" through the washing rites. The aftermath (turns 19–20) lands harder than the death scene itself: Joss asks her if he could have done more, and she redirects the weight without answering the question that's actually being asked.

**Arc C: Full season arc — from first herb-gather to first frost (turns 0–24)**
All 25 turns as a single arc: the healer who keeps counting and cannot stop, who saves some and loses some, who refuses rest, who will not speak her wound. Shape is there (gathering → crisis → survival → loss → aftermath → next case, repeat), but without a transformative beat it reads as a loop, not a story. The final turn (turn 24, first frost) is formally resonant — she's back to counting yarrow, same as turn 0 — but the character hasn't moved. The symmetry is ironic, not earned.

**Arc D: The Joss problem — can she let him carry weight? (turns 6, 11, 19, 23)**
The apprentice thread: she refuses to let him take the night watch (t6), doesn't quite sleep (t11), won't give him the guilt (t19), deflects Edda's rest-command by naming her own compresses (t23). This is the craft/delegation conflict from her character sheet. It never resolves. Not a standalone arc — it would need to be a subplot inside A or B.

---

## Chosen Arc: **B + A compressed**, centered on Tobin's death

**Selection rationale:**

Arc B alone is too thin without context — we need to know what Maren successfully controls before we can feel the weight of what she can't. But making Arc A the full story loses the harder truth. The cut I'm making: use Arc A (Bryn) as act one, compressed and treated as prologue/context rather than story-proper, then pivot to Arc B (Tobin) as the true dramatic spine. The piece earns its ending at turn 20 (the shelf reordering) because the reader has seen her succeed with Bryn and knows exactly what her counting means.

The Súil wound threads both arcs and is the governing subtext: "Second night. It was the second night with Súil too" (t4) and the washing rite's "I should have tried the willow bark earlier" (t17) are structurally answering each other. The whole arc is: she beat the second night once; she cannot beat death.

The Joss thread (Arc D) folds in as a coda: turn 19 is the moment she hands him the weight she won't carry, and says exactly the wrong true thing.
